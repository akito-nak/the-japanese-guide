# Chapter 12 — Japanese for Media

Somewhere along the way, most learners have a moment like this: a line of anime dialogue lands before the subtitle appears, a manga panel makes them laugh without a dictionary, or a headline on a station screen suddenly reads as words instead of shapes. Those moments are the reward for everything in the earlier chapters, and media is where they happen most often.

This chapter opens the last part of the book, where the focus shifts from what Japanese is to what you want to do with it. It covers three paths that millions of learners take. Anime gives you hours of natural-speed listening, if you know which speech to copy and which to admire from a distance. Manga gives you reading practice with a picture attached to every line, plus a whole hidden language of sound effects and furigana tricks. The news gives you standard Japanese at its densest and most formal, packed into headlines, kanji compounds and abbreviations that reward a little decoding skill.

You don't have to follow all three. Start with the one you love, borrow techniques from the others, and remember that the best study material is the material you can't stop reading.

## In This Chapter

- Use anime as a study tool, watching in passes with decreasing subtitle support, shadowing lines and mining sentences into flashcards
- Recognize common anime lines and character types, and turn rough anime speech into something you could say to a friend
- Read a manga page in the right order and understand the vocabulary of panels, bubbles and lettering
- Decode manga sound effects and mimetic words, including the famous sound of silence, シーン
- Read furigana that says something different from its kanji, and expand the contractions of casual dialogue
- Unpack news headlines: dropped particles, one-kanji country abbreviations and the "set to" ending へ
- Read news numbers, era-year dates, crime-report titles and weather warnings

## Before You Start

You should read kana fluently and know the basic kanji from [chapter 3](../03-writing-system/README.md), the sentence patterns in [chapter 4](../04-building-sentences/README.md) and the casual and polite registers in [chapter 5](../05-politeness/README.md). Anime and manga lean heavily on the role language of [section 6.3](../06-spoken-japanese/03-role-language.md) and the slang of [chapter 7](../07-living-language/README.md), and the news section builds on the era calendar in [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md).
