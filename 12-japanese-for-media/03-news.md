# Reading the News — Headlines, Kanji Compounds and NHK

首相、増税案を撤回へ. Seven kanji, a comma and two hiragana, and a Japanese reader has the whole story: the prime minister is set to withdraw the tax-increase plan. No verb, no subject marker, no wasted space. A news headline is Japanese compressed to its densest form, and learning to unpack it is one of the most satisfying milestones in your Japanese.

News Japanese (ニュースの日本語) is the language at its most formal, standard and neutral. There is no dialect and almost no slang, but there is a specific toolkit of grammar patterns, kanji compounds, abbreviations and conventions that you will meet nowhere else in such concentration. Once you know that toolkit, NHK, the national newspapers and the evening broadcast all open up to you.

## What Makes News Japanese Different

News writing has six features that set it apart from everyday speech and even from business writing:

1. *Kanji compounds.* Two-kanji Sino-Japanese words (発表, 決定, 逮捕) replace everyday verbs and phrases.
2. *Nominalization.* Actions become noun phrases: 導入を決定 rather than 導入することにした.
3. *Passive voice where the agent is obvious or unknown.* 逮捕された (was arrested) puts the event first.
4. *Formal connectors.* Patterns such as 〜をめぐって and 〜を受けて link long chains of information.
5. *Long, packed sentences.* A single sentence can carry who, what, when, why and the source of the claim.
6. *Abbreviation.* Countries, organizations and events shrink to one or two kanji in headlines.

## Start With NHK News Web Easy

Before you tackle regular news, read [NHK News Web Easy](https://news.web.nhk/news/easy/) (NEWS WEB EASY). It is NHK's own service of real news stories rewritten in simpler Japanese for learners and children:

- Furigana on every kanji, which you can switch off
- Pop-up definitions for harder words
- Audio for every article
- Free, with new articles every weekday

It is one of the most widely recommended reading resources for learners. Read it daily from about N4 level ([chapter 14](../14-the-jlpt/README.md)).

The reason to start here is coverage. Comfortable reading needs about 98% of the words in a text known (Hu and Nation, 2000), and a regular news article, dense with kanji compounds, sits far below that for most learners until about N2. Easy rewrites the same stories with a smaller vocabulary, which lifts you close to the threshold years earlier; [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md) explains why that matters.

A natural progression:

1. NHK News Web Easy, daily for three to six months
2. Regular [NHK News Web](https://news.web.nhk/newsweb) articles, once Easy feels comfortable
3. National newspapers such as 朝日新聞 (*Asahi Shimbun*), 読売新聞 (*Yomiuri Shimbun*) and 毎日新聞 (*Mainichi Shimbun*)
4. **社説** (*shasetsu*, newspaper editorials), the hardest and most rewarding step

## Grammar Patterns of the News

These patterns appear constantly in news writing and rarely in conversation:

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 〜によると | 警察によると | "According to ~" |
| 〜として | 首相として初めて | "As ~," "in the capacity of ~" |
| 〜をめぐって | 増税をめぐって | "Over (the issue of) ~" |
| 〜において | 国会において | "At," "in" (formal) |
| 〜に対し | 政府に対し | "Toward," "against" |
| 〜を受けて | 批判を受けて | "In response to ~" |
| 〜にもかかわらず | 反対にもかかわらず | "Despite ~" |
| 〜に加え | 円安に加え | "In addition to ~" |
| 〜を通じて | 交渉を通じて | "Through ~," "via ~" |
| 〜とみられる | 原因とみられる | "Is believed to be ~" (a reporter's hedge) |
| 〜模様だ | 合意する模様だ | "Appears to be ~" (a hedge) |
| 〜との見方もある | 悪化するとの見方もある | "There is also a view that ~" |
| 〜ことが明らかになった | 事実が明らかになった | "It has emerged that ~" |
| 〜とのことだ | 続けるとのことだ | "It is reported that ~" |

The hedges matter. News Japanese is careful to separate what is known from what is believed, so 〜とみられる and 〜模様だ tell you a claim is not yet confirmed.

> 警察によると、火事の原因はたばことみられる。\
> *Keisatsu ni yoru to, kaji no gen'in wa tabako to mirareru.*\
> According to the police, the fire is believed to have been caused by a cigarette.

## The Passive in the News

News Japanese uses the passive to put the event first, especially when the agent is obvious (the police arrest people) or unknown:

| Active (spoken) | Passive (news style) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 首相が発表した | 発表された | Was announced |
| 警察が逮捕した | 逮捕された | Was arrested |
| 政府が決定した | 決定された | Was decided |
| 会社が解雇した | 解雇された | Was dismissed |
| 裁判所が判決を下した | 判決が下された | A ruling was handed down |

## Headlines

### Country Abbreviations

Headlines shrink country names to a single kanji, usually the first character of the country's old kanji name:

| Country | Abbreviation | Usual name |
|---|---|---|
| United States | 米 (*Bei*) | アメリカ, 米国 |
| China | 中 (*Chū*) | 中国 |
| South Korea | 韓 (*Kan*) | 韓国 |
| Russia | 露 (*Ro*) | ロシア |
| United Kingdom | 英 (*Ei*) | イギリス, 英国 |
| France | 仏 (*Futsu*) | フランス |
| Germany | 独 (*Doku*) | ドイツ |
| Italy | 伊 (*I*) | イタリア |
| Australia | 豪 (*Gō*) | オーストラリア |
| India | 印 (*In*) | インド |

They combine freely: 日米首脳会談 is a Japan–US summit, and 米中 means "US–China."

### Unpacking a Headline

A **見出し** (*midashi*, headline) drops everything a reader can supply. Rebuild the full sentence and the meaning falls out:

> 首相、増税案を撤回へ　野党の反発受け\
> *Shushō, zōzei-an o tekkai e, yatō no hanpatsu uke*\
> Prime minister to withdraw tax-increase plan after opposition backlash

Unpacked: 首相が、野党の反発を受けて、増税案を撤回する方向だ。

> 大手企業、賃上げ率過去最高　春闘で妥結\
> *Ōte kigyō, chin'age-ritsu kako saikō, shuntō de daketsu*\
> Major companies settle record pay rises in spring wage talks

Unpacked: 大手企業が、春闘（春季労使交渉）で、過去最高の賃上げ率で妥結した。

The patterns to look for:

- The comma after the subject replaces が or は: 首相、
- Particles, especially を and が, are dropped where they can be: 反発受け for 反発を受けて
- A sentence-final へ means "is set to," "is heading toward": 撤回へ
- A noun can end the headline in place of a verb: 妥結 for 妥結した
- Events and organizations are abbreviated: 春闘 (*shuntō*) is the annual spring wage negotiation

## Reading an NHK Sentence

News sentences are long, but they are built in a predictable order: topic, background, time, content, then the verb that says how we know.

> 政府は、少子化対策の強化に向けて、来月から新たな支援金制度を導入することを決定したと発表した。\
> The government announced that it has decided to introduce a new financial-support system from next month, aimed at strengthening measures against the falling birthrate.

Broken into chunks:

| Chunk | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 政府は | As for the government (topic) |
| 少子化対策の強化に向けて | Toward strengthening measures against the falling birthrate |
| 来月から | From next month |
| 新たな支援金制度を | A new financial-support system (object) |
| 導入することを決定したと | That it decided to introduce (quoted content) |
| 発表した | Announced |

> **Tip:** Read a long news sentence from the end first. The final verb (発表した, 明らかにした, 決定した) tells you what kind of statement it is, and the rest fills in the details.

## Dates and Era Names

Japanese news mixes the Western calendar, **西暦** (*seireki*), with the Japanese era calendar, **和暦** (*wareki*), introduced in [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md). You need to convert on the fly.

The Reiwa rule: Reiwa year + 2018 = Western year. So 令和8年 is 2026, and in reverse 2026 − 2018 = 令和8年.

| Era | Reading | Span | Formula | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 昭和 | Shōwa | 1926–1989 | + 1925 | Ended in 昭和64年, which lasted only seven days |
| 平成 | Heisei | 1989–2019 | + 1988 | Ended in 平成31年 (April 30, 2019) |
| 令和 | Reiwa | 2019– | + 2018 | 令和元年 = 2019 |

- 元年 (*gannen*) is "year one" of an era. News writing almost always prints 令和元年, not 令和1年.
- 改元 (*kaigen*) is a change of era. A transition year is split: 2019 was 平成31年 until April 30 and 令和元年 from May 1.
- Domestic and official stories (budgets, laws, local government) often use 和暦; international news, sports and science mostly use 西暦. Many outlets print both: 2026年（令和8年）.
- **年度** (*nendo*) is the fiscal or school year, from April 1 to March 31. 2026年度 runs from April 2026 to March 2027.

## Big Numbers and Approximations

Japanese groups large numbers in fours, by 万 (10,000), not in threes, while printed numerals still use the Western comma every three digits. This trips up every learner.

| Unit | Reading | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 万 | *man* | 10,000 (10⁴) |
| 億 | *oku* | 100 million (10⁸) |
| 兆 | *chō* | 1 trillion (10¹²) |
| 京 | *kei* | 10 quadrillion (10¹⁶), rarely needed |

To read a big number, cut it into blocks of four digits from the right:

| Written | Reading | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1億2000万人 | *ichioku nisenman nin* | 120 million people, roughly Japan's population |
| 115兆円 | *hyaku jūgo chō en* | ¥115 trillion, roughly the size of the national budget in the mid-2020s |

Headlines rarely give exact figures. These approximation words are essential:

| Word | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 約 | *yaku* | "About," placed before the number: 約3000人 |
| 〜余り | *-amari* | "Just over," placed after: 100人余り |
| 〜強 / 〜弱 | *-kyō* / *-jaku* | "A little over" / "a little under" a round figure: 3割強 is about 30–34%, 3割弱 about 26–29% |
| 割 / 分 / 厘 | *wari* / *bu* / *rin* | 1割 = 10%, 1分 = 1%, 1厘 = 0.1% (3割2分 = 32%) |
| ポイント | *pointo* | Percentage point: 2ポイント増 means up two points, not up 2% |

> **Note:** 弱 can confuse native speakers too. Some readers take 7割弱 to mean slightly *more* than 70%. Writers still use 弱 for "a little under"; learn the traditional rule and know the confusion exists.

## People in Crime Reports

Japanese media follow strict conventions for naming people in criminal cases, and the title attached to a name signals where the case stands:

| Term | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 容疑者 | *yōgisha* | Suspect; name + 容疑者 after an arrest |
| 被告 | *hikoku* | Defendant; name + 被告 after indictment |
| 〜の疑い | *no utagai* | "On suspicion of ~," the standard hedge before guilt is proven |
| 自称 | *jishō* | "Self-described," when police cannot confirm a job or identity: 自称・会社員 |
| 実名報道 / 匿名報道 | *jitsumei hōdō* / *tokumei hōdō* | Reporting by name / anonymously; minors are not named under the Juvenile Act (少年法) |
| 呼び捨て | *yobisute* | Using a bare name with no title |

In 1989 the major media switched from 呼び捨て to name + **容疑者**, partly to avoid treating suspects as already guilty. After indictment the title becomes **被告**.

The case vocabulary follows the case from arrest to final appeal:

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 逮捕 | *taiho* | Arrest |
| 送検 | *sōken* | Referral from police to prosecutors |
| 起訴 / 不起訴 | *kiso* / *fukiso* | Indictment / decision not to prosecute |
| 求刑 | *kyūkei* | The sentence the prosecution asks for |
| 判決 | *hanketsu* | Verdict and sentence |
| 実刑 / 執行猶予 | *jikkei* / *shikkō yūyo* | Prison term / suspended sentence |
| 控訴 / 上告 | *kōso* / *jōkoku* | Appeal to a high court / to the Supreme Court |

Attitudes to names are shifting inside the system too: since 2024, the Ministry of Justice (法務省) has had prison staff address inmates as 〜さん rather than by bare surname, a change made after abuse cases at a prison in Nagoya.

## The Weather Forecast

Weather is on every broadcast, and it has its own set phrases beyond 晴れ, 曇り and 雨:

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 前線 | *zensen* | Weather front (寒冷前線 cold, 温暖前線 warm, 梅雨前線 rainy-season) |
| 気圧の谷 | *kiatsu no tani* | A trough of low pressure, bringing unsettled weather |
| 大気の状態が不安定 | *taiki no jōtai ga fuantei* | "The atmosphere is unstable": expect thunder, squalls and downpours |
| 〜のおそれ | *no osore* | "Risk of ~": 大雨のおそれ |
| ところにより | *tokoro ni yori* | "In places," patchy rather than widespread |
| 線状降水帯 | *senjō kōsuitai* | A "linear rainband" of stalled storms dumping intense rain; the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) began issuing alerts for it in 2021 |

### Warning Levels

Since May 29, 2026, the JMA's weather warnings carry numbered levels that match evacuation advice, with a new tier, 危険警報 (*kiken keihō*, danger warning), between the old warning and emergency warning. As of 2026 the scale runs as follows (details on the [JMA's warnings page](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/kishou/know/bosai/keiho.html), in Japanese):

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 注意報 (*chūihō*) | Advisory: be careful |
| 3 | 警報 (*keihō*) | Warning: older people and others who need time should evacuate |
| 4 | 危険警報 (*kiken keihō*) | Danger warning: everyone in the danger area should evacuate |
| 5 | 特別警報 (*tokubetsu keihō*) | Emergency warning: disaster is imminent or happening; protect your life now |

Names on the screen combine the hazard and the level, such as レベル4大雨危険警報 (level-4 heavy-rain danger warning). See [section 8.4](../08-getting-around/04-emergencies.md) for what to do when you hear one.

### Heat

Temperature words are defined by the day's maximum, except 熱帯夜:

| Japanese | Reading | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 夏日 | *natsubi* | Maximum of 25°C or more |
| 真夏日 | *manatsubi* | Maximum of 30°C or more |
| 猛暑日 | *mōshobi* | Maximum of 35°C or more |
| 酷暑日 | *kokushobi* | Maximum of 40°C or more, an official JMA term since April 2026 ([announcement](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/press/2604/17a/20260417_40degree_name.pdf)) |
| 熱帯夜 | *nettaiya* | Overnight minimum of 25°C or more |

In summer you will also hear 熱中症警戒アラート (*necchūshō keikai arāto*, heatstroke alert), issued nationwide since 2021, and its higher tier, 熱中症特別警戒アラート, added in 2024.

## Listening to the News

### NHK Radio

NHK radio news is formal, clear and in the standard accent, which makes it an excellent model for pitch accent ([section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)) and a good script for shadowing once you are past the beginner stage: take one 30-second item, read the transcript, then speak along with the announcer ([section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md) explains the method). NHK News Web Easy's audio, read more slowly, is the gentler place to start. NHK announcers read briskly but articulate every mora crisply, so the sounds are clear even when the pace is not. Programs stream live and as podcasts through [NHK Radio](https://www.nhk.or.jp/radio/).

### NHK Television

- ニュース7 (*Nyūsu Sebun*) — the main 7 p.m. evening news
- おはよう日本 (*Ohayō Nippon*) — the morning news
- クローズアップ現代 (*Kurōzu Appu Gendai*) — in-depth documentary reporting
- 日曜討論 (*Nichiyō Tōron*) — Sunday political debate; difficult and rewarding
- [NHK for School](https://www.nhk.or.jp/school/) — short, well-produced educational programs for schoolchildren; ideal intermediate listening

## A Reading Path by Level

| Level | Resource | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| N4 | NHK News Web Easy | One article a day; look up everything |
| N3 | NHK News Web Easy plus short NHK articles | Two articles a day |
| N3–N2 | Regular NHK News Web articles | One article a day; mine vocabulary |
| N2 | Several news sources | Compare coverage of the same story to build topic vocabulary |
| N1 | National newspapers | Editorials (社説) and long-form features |

Each row mixes two kinds of reading. **Intensive reading** is the slow work in the table: one article, every unknown word looked up, the best sentences mined into flashcards. **Extensive reading** is its partner: many easy articles read quickly for the gist, stopping only for words that block the meaning. Intensive reading teaches new words; extensive reading, kept near the 98% threshold, makes them automatic. Once News Web Easy stops being an intensive read, keep it as your extensive read while regular articles take over the intensive role.

## Summary

- News Japanese relies on kanji compounds, nominalization, hedges such as 〜とみられる and long sentences whose final verb tells you how the information is known.
- NHK News Web Easy rewrites real news in simpler Japanese with furigana and is the best starting point from about N4, because it brings you close to the 98% word coverage that comfortable reading needs; pair intensive reading of one article with extensive reading of easier ones.
- Headlines drop particles, mark the subject with a comma, end in a noun or in へ ("is set to") and abbreviate countries to one kanji: 米 for the US, 中 for China, 韓 for South Korea.
- To convert dates, add 2018 to a Reiwa year, 1988 to Heisei and 1925 to Shōwa; 年度 is the fiscal year from April 1 to March 31.
- Large numbers group by 万 (10⁴), 億 (10⁸) and 兆 (10¹²), and 強 and 弱 mean "a little over" and "a little under" a round figure.
- A named suspect is 容疑者 after arrest and 被告 after indictment, and JMA warnings run from level 2 注意報 to level 5 特別警報, with 危険警報 at level 4 since 2026.
