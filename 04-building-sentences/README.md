# Chapter 4 — Building Sentences

With kana and a first handful of kanji under your belt, you can read Japanese sounds. This chapter is where you start saying things. A Japanese sentence is a small, tidy machine: nouns tagged with particles that announce their roles, an optional pile of time and place in the middle, and a verb waiting patiently at the end. Once you can see that machine, even a long sentence breaks down into parts you recognize.

The chapter has three movements. Grammar gives you the structure: word order, the essential particles, the three verb groups and their most useful forms, the two kinds of adjective, questions and counters. Vocabulary gives you the bricks: a few hundred high-frequency words for greetings, numbers, time, family, food, places, travel and the body. Phrases give you the polish: the set expressions, idioms and sound words that make Japanese sound like Japanese rather than translated English.

Expect the verb tables to feel like a lot at first. They are, briefly. But Japanese conjugation is remarkably regular (two irregular verbs, compared with English's hundreds), and every form you learn here is reused for the rest of this book.

## In This Chapter

- Build sentences in subject–object–verb order and mark each noun's role with は, が, を, に and で
- Tell godan, ichidan and irregular verbs apart and produce their masu, nai, te and ta forms
- Conjugate i-adjectives and na-adjectives in the present, past and negative
- Ask questions with か and the essential question words, and count things with the right counter
- Recognize and use several hundred core words for daily life in Japan
- Say the right set phrase at the right moment, from いただきます to お疲れ様です
- Understand everyday idioms, four-kanji compounds, proverbs and sound words

## Before You Start

You should be able to read hiragana comfortably and recognize katakana, as taught in [chapter 3](../03-writing-system/README.md). Kanji in this chapter always come with a reading, so you don't need to know them yet; treat each one you meet as a bonus.
