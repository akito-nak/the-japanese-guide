# Chapter 12 — The JLPT

Every learner eventually wants to know: how good is my Japanese, really? Friends say 「日本語、上手ですね」 after you order coffee, which is lovely and tells you nothing. Employers, universities and immigration officers want something more exact, and for most of the world that means the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test, the JLPT, with its five levels from N5 to N1.

The JLPT is more than a certificate. With a date on the calendar and a clear list of what each level demands, a vague ambition ("get better at Japanese") becomes a plan with milestones. Each passed level is proof, to others and to yourself, that the hours added up to something. And because the test changes little from year to year, preparing for it is unusually predictable: you can know exactly what is coming.

This chapter explains how the test works as of 2026, then walks through each level in turn, from N5's first hundred kanji to N1's literary grammar and four-character idioms. Each level gets its grammar, vocabulary, kanji, reading and listening demands, a study plan and an honest picture of what it lets you do in real life.

## In This Chapter

- Understand the JLPT's sections, timing, scoring and pass marks, and why you must pass every section
- Know the 2026 test dates, how registration and fees work, and when results arrive
- Treat word and kanji counts for each level as the community estimates they are
- Learn the key grammar, vocabulary and kanji for each level from N5 to N1
- Recognize what JLPT reading and listening questions demand at each level
- Choose the right level to sit and follow a study plan sized to it
- Weigh what a JLPT certificate proves (and does not prove) to employers, schools and immigration

## Before You Start

The N5 and N4 sections build directly on [chapter 1](../01-writing-system/README.md) and [chapter 2](../02-building-sentences/README.md); you should read hiragana and katakana fluently before preparing for N5. From N3 upward, the politeness levels of [chapter 3](../03-politeness/README.md) and the news reading skills of [section 10.3](../10-japanese-for-media/03-news.md) become increasingly useful.
