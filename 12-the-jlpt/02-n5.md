# N5: The Starting Line

Six months ago, 「駅はどこですか」 was a row of mysterious shapes. Now you can read it, say it and understand the answer. That is N5: the first official proof that you have crossed from "interested in Japanese" to "learning Japanese". Don't underestimate it, because it takes real study, but don't fear it either. You will be surprised how much you can communicate with N5 knowledge alone.

N5 is the entry level of the JLPT. Its official description is "the ability to understand some basic Japanese": reading words and sentences in hiragana, katakana and basic kanji, and following slow, simple conversations about everyday topics. Many learners reach it in three to six months of steady study from zero, and everything above it is built on this foundation.

## What N5 Covers

Remember that the JLPT publishes no official lists, so the targets below are the usual community estimates.

| Area | Target (est.) |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | ~800 words |
| Kanji | ~100 characters |
| Grammar | ~80–100 patterns |
| Reading | Short, simple sentences and passages on daily topics |
| Listening | Slow, clear conversations about daily life |

Hiragana and katakana are assumed throughout. If you are not yet reading them fluently, start with [chapter 1](../01-writing-system/README.md).

## N5 Kanji: A Core Set

Most N5 kanji lists build on roughly 100 characters. The set below, grouped by theme, is close to what you will find in most of them; a few lists add or drop a handful.

| Theme | Kanji |
|---|---|
| Numbers and money | 一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九 十 百 千 万 円 |
| Time | 年 月 日 時 分 半 今 毎 週 午 前 後 間 |
| Days of the week | 日 月 火 水 木 金 土 |
| People | 人 父 母 子 女 男 友 名 先 生 |
| Nature | 山 川 天 気 雨 花 空 |
| Directions | 上 下 中 外 右 左 東 西 南 北 |
| Places | 国 学 校 会 社 店 駅 道 |
| Actions | 行 来 食 飲 見 聞 話 読 書 買 言 休 入 出 立 |
| Descriptions | 大 小 高 安 新 古 長 白 多 少 |
| Body and things | 口 手 目 耳 足 電 車 何 本 語 魚 |

> **Tip:** Learn kanji inside words, not alone: 電車 (*densha*, train), 学校 (*gakkō*, school), 毎日 (*mainichi*, every day). The test asks you to read words, and a kanji you met in a word is a kanji you will recognize.

## N5 Grammar Patterns

### Sentence Structure

| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A は B です。 | A is B. |
| A は B じゃないです。 | A is not B. |
| A は B でした。 | A was B. |
| これ / それ / あれ / どれ | this / that / that over there / which |
| ここ / そこ / あそこ / どこ | here / there / over there / where |

### Particles

| Particle | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| は | Topic | 私**は**学生です。 |
| が | Subject | 猫**が**います。 |
| を | Object | 水**を**飲みます。 |
| に | Destination, time, location of existence | 学校**に**行きます。 |
| で | Place of action, means | 電車**で**来ました。 |
| と | And, with | 友達**と**行きます。 |
| も | Also | 私**も**です。 |
| の | Possession, linking nouns | 私**の**本 |
| か | Question | 学生です**か**。 |
| ね | Seeking agreement | そうです**ね**。 |
| よ | Telling the listener something | 分かります**よ**。 |

### Verbs

| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 〜ます / 〜ません / 〜ました / 〜ませんでした | Polite present, negative, past, past negative |
| 〜てください | Please do ~ |
| 〜たいです | I want to do ~ |
| 〜ています | Ongoing action or state |
| 〜てもいいですか | May I ~? |
| 〜てはいけません | You must not ~ |
| 〜ことができます | Can, be able to ~ |

### Adjectives

い-adjectives and な-adjectives conjugate differently, and the test checks both.

| Type | Present | Negative | Past | Before a noun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | 大きい | 大き**くない** | 大き**かった** | 大きい犬 |
| な-adjective | きれい | きれい**じゃない** | きれい**でした** | きれい**な**花 |

### Common Expressions

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 〜はどこですか | 駅はどこですか。 | Where is ~? |
| 〜はいくらですか | これはいくらですか。 | How much is ~? |
| 〜をください | 水をください。 | ~, please. |
| 〜がほしいです | 新しい本がほしいです。 | I want ~. |
| 〜が好きです / 嫌いです | 猫が好きです。 | I like / dislike ~. |
| 〜が上手です | 日本語が上手です。 | (Someone) is good at ~. |
| 〜が分かります | 英語が分かります。 | (Someone) understands ~. |

## N5 Vocabulary: The Essentials

These words turn up again and again at N5, grouped so you can learn them in families.

**Food and drink:** 食べ物 (food), 飲み物 (drink), 朝ごはん (breakfast), 昼ごはん (lunch), 晩ごはん (dinner), 水 (water), お茶 (tea), ジュース (juice), ご飯 (rice, meal), パン (bread), 魚 (fish), 肉 (meat), 野菜 (vegetables), 卵 (egg).

**People:** 人 (person), 友達 (friend), 先生 (teacher), 学生 (student), 会社員 (company employee), 家族 (family), 父 (my father), 母 (my mother), 兄 (my older brother), 姉 (my older sister).

**Places:** 家 (home), 学校 (school), 会社 (company), 駅 (station), 銀行 (bank), コンビニ (convenience store), レストラン (restaurant), 病院 (hospital), 図書館 (library).

**Time:** 今日 (today), 明日 (tomorrow), 昨日 (yesterday), 今週 (this week), 今月 (this month), 今年 (this year), 朝 (morning), 昼 (noon, daytime), 夜 (night).

**Adjectives:** 大きい / 小さい (big / small), 高い / 安い (expensive / cheap), 新しい / 古い (new / old), いい / 悪い (good / bad), 多い / 少ない (many / few), 難しい / やさしい (difficult / easy), 楽しい (fun), きれい (pretty, clean), 暑い / 寒い (hot / cold, of weather).

**Verbs:** 行く (go), 来る (come), 帰る (go home), 食べる (eat), 飲む (drink), 見る (see), 聞く (listen, ask), 話す (speak), 読む (read), 書く (write), 買う (buy), 使う (use), 起きる (get up), 寝る (sleep), 待つ (wait), 分かる (understand), ある / いる (exist, for things / for people and animals), する (do), 入る (enter), 出る (leave, go out).

## What N5 Questions Look Like

The questions are short and test one thing at a time. A typical vocabulary item asks for the word that fits:

> 田中さんは　まいにち　がっこうに（　　）。\
> a. いきます　b. ねます　c. かいます　d. のみます\
> Mr. Tanaka ( ) to school every day.

The answer is a, いきます (goes). Notice the spaces between words: N5 and N4 questions put spaces between words, as children's books do, to help beginners.

A grammar item asks for the missing particle or form:

> これは　だれ（　　）かばんですか。\
> a. が　b. の　c. を　d. に\
> Whose bag is this?

The answer is b, の: だれのかばん, "whose bag".

A reading item gives a short text and asks about a detail:

> 私の名前は田中です。毎朝、7時に起きます。朝ごはんはパンを食べます。\
> My name is Tanaka. I get up at 7 every morning. I eat bread for breakfast.

To the question 田中さんは何時に起きますか (What time does Tanaka get up?), the answer is 7時 (seven o'clock).

## A Six-Week Plan for Exam Preparation

If you already know your kana and have started a textbook, six focused weeks can carry you to exam day. Starting from absolute zero, stretch the same plan over three to six months.

| Week | Focus | Daily activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hiragana and katakana | 30 minutes of kana drills and writing |
| 2 | Core vocabulary (200 words) and numbers | 20 new Anki cards a day |
| 3 | です/ます, particles, basic sentences | Genki I, chapters 1–4 |
| 4 | Verbs, adjectives, questions | Genki I, chapters 5–8 |
| 5 | The N5 kanji and vocabulary review | Kanji practice and Anki review |
| 6 | Practice tests and weak areas | Two full practice tests, then review every mistake |

Aim for at least 45 minutes a day; about an hour and a half is ideal.

## After N5

N5 gives you survival Japanese: around 800 words, about 100 kanji and the grammar to make simple requests, ask questions and talk about your day. With it, you can start a beginner manga such as よつばと！ (*Yotsuba&!*) with a dictionary, catch whole sentences in a children's anime like *Doraemon*, handle basic exchanges while traveling and hold short, simple conversations.

Aim for N4 next. With the N5 foundation in place, N4 usually takes another three to four months at the same pace.

## Summary

- N5 tests "some basic Japanese": kana, about 100 kanji, around 800 words and simple grammar, all as community estimates.
- The core grammar covers です sentences, the main particles, ます-form verbs, て-form requests and the two adjective types.
- N5 questions are short, test one point at a time and are written with spaces between words.
- Language knowledge and reading share one score out of 120 at N5, with listening scored separately out of 60.
- Many learners reach N5 in three to six months of steady study, and N4 typically takes a further three to four.
