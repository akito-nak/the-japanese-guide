# N1: The Summit

There is a moment many advanced learners describe: you look up a word in a Japanese-Japanese dictionary, understand the definition and realize you no longer need English to learn Japanese. N1 is the level where that becomes normal. It is not the end of learning Japanese. It is the point where you finally have enough tools to learn everything else from the language itself.

Officially, N1 is "the ability to understand Japanese used in a variety of circumstances": reading logically complex and abstract writing such as editorials and critiques, understanding the content and intent of in-depth texts on many topics, and following conversations, news and lectures at natural speed. Only a fraction of the learners who start Japanese ever reach it, and those who do can take part in Japanese professional, academic and social life at a sophisticated level.

## What N1 Covers

| Area | Target (est.) |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | ~10,000 words |
| Kanji | ~2,000 characters, close to the full jōyō list |
| Grammar | Formal, literary and classically influenced patterns |
| Reading | Editorials, essays, critiques, literary and specialist texts |
| Listening | Natural-speed speech: lectures, discussions, subtle nuance |

The **jōyō kanji** (常用漢字, *jōyō kanji*, kanji for everyday use) are the official list of 2,136 characters taught in Japanese schools and used in newspapers; N1 lists estimate around 2,000, which covers most of it. N1 vocabulary adds formal and literary words that survive in modern writing, technical words from many fields and, above all, fine distinctions between near-synonyms.

## N1 Grammar: Advanced Patterns

### Literary and Classically Influenced Forms

These patterns descend from classical Japanese and give formal writing its weight.

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜ごとし / 〜ごとく | Like, as if (literary) | 嵐の**ごとく**去っていった。 |
| 〜べく | In order to (formal) | 問題を解決す**べく**努力する。 |
| 〜たる | As one who is ~ (formal) | 教師**たる**者、生徒の手本であるべきだ。 |
| 〜しかるべき | Appropriate, proper | **しかるべき**対応をとる。 |
| 〜にして | Even for; only (a person of that kind) | 天才**にして**初めてできることだ。 |
| 〜いかん | Depending on | 結果**いかん**では再考する。 |
| 〜といわず〜といわず | Both ~ and ~, without distinction | 昼**といわず**夜**といわず**働く。 |

### Conditions and Nuance

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜をよそに | Ignoring, indifferent to | 周囲の心配**をよそに**出発した。 |
| 〜もさることながら | ~ goes without saying, but also | 技術**もさることながら**経験も必要だ。 |
| 〜に至るまで | Right down to, even | 細部**に至るまで**確認した。 |
| 〜ならではの | Unique to, only possible with | 京都**ならではの**景色。 |
| 〜にたえる | Worth, able to stand up to | 読む**にたえる**文章。 |
| 〜に即して | In line with, based on | 実態**に即して**考える。 |
| 〜をもって | By means of; as of (a date) | 本日**をもって**終了いたします。 |

### Advanced Stance and Concession

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜ではあるまいか | Might it not be that ~ | 誤りなの**ではあるまいか**。 |
| 〜ずにはいられない | Can't help doing | 笑わ**ずにはいられない**。 |
| 〜を余儀なくされる | Be forced to | 中止**を余儀なくされた**。 |
| 〜ならいざしらず | It may be different for ~, but | 昔**ならいざしらず**、今は違う。 |
| 〜はさておき | Setting ~ aside | 冗談**はさておき**、本題に入ろう。 |

## N1 Vocabulary: Nuance over Numbers

At N1, vocabulary is less about how many words you know and more about choosing the precise one.

### Near-Synonyms

| Words | Distinction |
|---|---|
| 見る / 見える / 目にする | Look at (deliberately) / be visible / happen to see |
| 思う / 考える / 信じる | Feel, think (impression) / think through (reasoning) / believe |
| 言う / 述べる / 語る | Say / state (formal) / tell, narrate |
| 変わる / 変化する / 変容する | Change / change (formal, measurable) / transform (deeply) |
| 行う / 実施する / 遂行する | Do, hold / implement / carry out to completion |
| 始まる / 開始する / 着手する | Begin / commence (formal) / set to work on |

### Formal and Literary Words

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 由来 | *yurai* | Origin, derivation |
| 趣 | *omomuki* | Charm, atmosphere; gist |
| 様相 | *yōsō* | Aspect, appearance |
| 所以 | *yuen* | Reason, grounds (literary) |
| 齟齬 | *sogo* | Discrepancy, mismatch |
| 辻褄 | *tsujitsuma* | Coherence (辻褄が合う, to add up) |
| 見識 | *kenshiki* | Discernment, insight |
| 蔑ろ | *naigashiro* | Neglect, disregard (蔑ろにする) |
| 弊害 | *heigai* | Harmful effect |
| 妥当 | *datō* | Appropriate, valid |

### Four-Character Idioms

**Yojijukugo** (四字熟語, *yojijukugo*) are idioms of four kanji, many from Chinese classics. They appear in N1 reading and vocabulary, and educated writers use them constantly.

| Idiom | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 五里霧中 | *gori muchū* | Completely at a loss (lost in a thick fog) |
| 孤軍奮闘 | *kogun funtō* | Fighting a lonely battle single-handed |
| 試行錯誤 | *shikō sakugo* | Trial and error |
| 前途多難 | *zento tanan* | Many difficulties ahead |
| 一石二鳥 | *isseki nichō* | Killing two birds with one stone |
| 針小棒大 | *shinshō bōdai* | Making a mountain out of a molehill |
| 朝令暮改 | *chōrei bokai* | Rules that change from morning to evening; inconsistency |
| 玉石混交 | *gyokuseki konkō* | Good and bad mixed together (also written 玉石混淆) |
| 本末転倒 | *honmatsu tentō* | Putting the cart before the horse |
| 換骨奪胎 | *kankotsu dattai* | Reworking an earlier work into something new of one's own |

## Reading at N1

N1 reading includes editorial columns and opinion essays, literary excerpts, technical and specialist articles, and integrated questions that compare two or more texts on the same topic. Four things make it hard: speed (the passages are long and time is short), subtlety (the answer choices are close and you must find the best one), formal and literary language and inference (the correct answer is often not stated directly).

To prepare, read widely in editorials, essays and fiction for months before the exam, build your reading speed with timed practice, learn the classical forms that survive in modern writing (〜べし, 〜ず, 〜ごとし) and, for comparison questions, note each author's position before you look at the choices.

## Listening at N1

N1 listening includes lectures and talks, conversations where you must infer what the speaker intends, and integrated questions that combine information from different parts of the recording. Speakers use all the contractions, hedging and implication of real Japanese, so you must understand both what is said and what is meant.

## A Study Plan for N1

N1 preparation usually takes six to 12 months from a solid N2, depending on how much you already read and listen.

| Phase | Months | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–3 | Vocabulary: complete the jōyō kanji, work through [Shin Kanzen Master N1 vocabulary](https://www.3anet.co.jp/np/en/) and mine new words from reading into [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) |
| 2 | 3–5 | Grammar and expressions: Shin Kanzen Master N1 grammar, four-character idioms and the basics of classical forms |
| 3 | 5–8 | Intensive reading: one or two newspaper articles a day (the 社説 editorials of the national papers are ideal), short stories and articles in your own field |
| 4 | 8–10 | Intensive listening: [NHK radio](https://www.nhk.or.jp/radio/) news, discussion programs, drama without subtitles, official practice audio |
| 5 | 10–12 | Test preparation: three or more full practice tests, error analysis, timed reading |

## The N1 Mindset

N1 is not won in the last months of cramming. It is built over years: roughly, a year or two from zero to N3, another year to N2 and one to three more to N1, though every learner's road is different. Successful candidates describe the same habit again and again: reading and listening to Japanese every day, not only studying it. The vocabulary and nuance N1 demands are too large to memorize; they have to be absorbed through sustained exposure.

## After N1

N1 holders often say Japanese stops being a subject and becomes a tool: you stop thinking about Japanese and start thinking in it. N1 is the standard for many university and graduate programs and professional roles in Japan, it is required of doctors trained abroad before they can sit Japan's national medical examination, it earns 15 points in the immigration system for highly skilled professionals and it is the foundation for translation work and for enjoying Japanese literature, film and media without aids.

Plenty remains to explore after N1:

- **方言 (*hōgen*, dialects).** Rapid Osaka or rural Tōhoku speech can still floor you ([section 4.2](../04-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md)).
- **古文 (*kobun*, classical Japanese).** Reading pre-modern texts is a discipline of its own.
- **専門用語 (*senmon yōgo*, technical terms).** Law, medicine and engineering each have deep vocabularies.
- **Living Japanese.** Slang, internet language and generational speech never stop changing ([chapter 5](../05-living-language/README.md)).

> 千里の道も一歩から。\
> *Senri no michi mo ippo kara.*\
> A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step.

You started with hiragana. This is where that step leads.

## Summary

- N1 is "the ability to understand Japanese used in a variety of circumstances", including abstract editorials and natural-speed lectures.
- N1 needs about 10,000 words and about 2,000 kanji (community estimates), close to the 2,136 jōyō kanji.
- N1 grammar draws on literary and classically influenced patterns such as 〜べく, 〜たる and 〜ごとく.
- At N1, choosing between near-synonyms and knowing four-character idioms matters more than raw vocabulary size.
- N1 needs an overall 100 out of 180 with at least 19 in each section, and typically takes six to 12 months of preparation from a solid N2.
- Dialects, classical Japanese, technical vocabulary and living slang remain to explore after N1.
