# Chapter 3 — Politeness and Register

By now you can read kana, recognize a growing stack of kanji and build sentences of your own. Here is the twist: in Japanese, a grammatically perfect sentence can still be the wrong sentence. Say 食べる？ (*taberu?*) to a close friend and you sound warm. Say it to your manager and you sound as if you have forgotten who signs your timesheet. Say 召し上がりますか？ (*meshiagarimasu ka?*) to that same friend and they will wonder why you are suddenly speaking like a hotel concierge.

Japanese builds the relationship between speakers into its grammar. Verb endings, whole verbs, nouns and even the word for "I" shift with who is talking to whom, and about whom. This chapter takes that system apart one level at a time: the casual speech of friends and family, the polite です and ます of everyday public life and 敬語 (*keigo*), the honorific language of business and formal occasions. It finishes at the convenience-store counter, where politeness is evolving in real time and where you will hear forms that textbooks call wrong.

None of this needs to be intimidating. Japanese speakers are generous with learners who make an honest effort, and the system runs on a handful of principles you can learn in an afternoon and refine for years. Master them and you will not only sound more natural; you will understand far more of what people are telling you between the lines.

## In This Chapter

- Choose between casual, polite and honorific speech for a given person and situation
- Apply uchi and soto: elevate outsiders, humble your own group and drop さん for colleagues when speaking to clients
- Understand fast casual speech, with its dropped particles, contractions and sentence-final particles
- Use the ます forms, です and the set phrases of polite conversation, phone calls and email
- Convert verbs into sonkeigo and kenjōgo with the special verbs and the お〜になる and お〜する patterns
- Recognize バイト敬語, double keigo and overused させていただく, and know the traditional alternatives

## Before You Start

You need to read hiragana, katakana and basic kanji from [chapter 1](../01-writing-system/README.md), and know the plain and ます forms of verbs, the copula and the main particles from [chapter 2](../02-building-sentences/README.md). Every example in this chapter has romanization, so a shaky kanji is no obstacle.
