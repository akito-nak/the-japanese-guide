# Practice and Further Reading

Politeness is a skill you build by switching, so most of these exercises ask you to move one sentence between levels or to pick the level a situation calls for. Write your answers down, in kana or kanji, before you check them in the answers at the back of the book. Say the converted sentences aloud as well: keigo verbs feel long and strange in the mouth until you have said them a few dozen times.

## Practice

### 3.1 The Politeness Levels

1. Which level would you start with, casual, polite or keigo? (a) A close friend from university. (b) A convenience-store clerk. (c) A client at a business meeting. (d) A stranger asking you for directions at a festival. (e) Your younger brother.
2. Without using あなた, say in polite Japanese: "Ms. Sato, are you going tomorrow?"
3. A caller from another company asks for your colleague 山田 (*Yamada*), who is out. Which reply is correct, and why? (a) 山田さんはいらっしゃいません。 (b) 山田はおりません。
4. Match each pronoun to its usual impression: 俺, 僕, 私 (read *watakushi*), わし. Impressions: formal and careful; elderly and regional; masculine and direct; gentle and modest.
5. You meet the parents of a Japanese friend for the first time. Your friend talks to you casually. Which level do you use with the parents, and which with your friend, in the same conversation?

### 3.2 Casual Japanese

6. Rewrite in casual speech: (a) 明日は暇ですか？ (b) 宿題をしています。 (c) 分かりません。 (d) 映画を見に行きませんか？ (e) 電車に乗らなければいけません。
7. Give the full form of each contraction and its meaning: (a) 食べちゃった (b) 書いとく (c) 行かなきゃ (d) 寝てた
8. Fill in the sentence-final particle, choosing from ね, よ, かな and っけ: (a) 今日は暑い＿。(seeking agreement) (b) 明日は雨だ＿。(telling someone something they don't know) (c) 彼、来る＿。(wondering) (d) 会議、何時だった＿？(trying to remember)
9. A friend asks 土曜日、カラオケ行かない？ Write a casual acceptance and a soft casual refusal.

### 3.3 Polite Japanese

10. Give the ます forms of 書く (*kaku*, write): present, negative, past, past negative, volitional ("let's") and invitation ("won't you?").
11. Make these polite: (a) 学生だ。 (b) 学生じゃなかった。 (c) 静かだった。
12. Add お or ご where natural: 名前, 連絡, 茶, 家族, 電話, コーヒー.
13. Translate into Japanese, using the humble verb for your name: "Nice to meet you. My name is Smith. I look forward to getting to know you."
14. Put these requests in order from least to most polite: (a) 見せていただけますか？ (b) 見せて。 (c) お見せいただけますでしょうか？ (d) 見せてください。
15. Write the standard first line and last line of a business email to a client you work with regularly.

### 3.4 Honorific Japanese

16. Give the sonkeigo form of: (a) 言う (b) 食べる (c) いる (d) 見る (e) する (f) 待つ
17. Give the kenjōgo form of: (a) 行く (to the other person's office) (b) 言う (c) する (d) もらう (e) 見る (f) 会う
18. Rewrite with the お〜になる or お〜する pattern: (a) 部長が書きました。(the manager wrote it; sonkeigo) (b) 私が荷物を持ちます。(I'll carry the bags; kenjōgo)
19. Convert each sentence into polite Japanese and then into keigo: (a) 先生は明日来る？ (Is the teacher coming tomorrow?) (b) 明日、先生の研究室に行く。 (I'll go to the teacher's office tomorrow.)
20. Each sentence contains a keigo error. Correct it. (a) To an outside caller: 田中さんはいらっしゃいません。 (b) To your company president at a dinner: 社長、どうぞいただいてください。 (c) To a client: 私が弊社の新商品をご説明になります。
21. Say in keigo, as a receptionist answering a business call: "Thank you for calling. This is ABC Corporation. Could you hold for a moment, please?"

### 3.5 Modern and Manual Keigo

22. Identify the バイト敬語 in each sentence and give the traditional version: (a) こちら、ハンバーグになります。 (b) 5000円からお預かりします。 (c) お箸のほう、おつけしますか？ (d) ご注文はよろしかったでしょうか？
23. Correct the excessive keigo: (a) 社長がおっしゃられました。 (b) 先生がご覧になられました。 (c) 資料を拝見させていただきます。
24. In which of these sentences does させていただく fit the official guidelines, and why? (a) 本日は休業させていただきます。 (b) 先日は研究室を見学させていただき、ありがとうございました。 (c) 今回は私が担当させていただきます。
25. What would you say, working in a shop, when: (a) a customer asks for something and you need them to wait a moment; (b) you come back to the customer after making them wait; (c) you need to ask the customer's name?
26. Your manager says いい仕事をしたね (*ii shigoto o shita ne*, "nice work"). Give two polite ways to accept the praise modestly, and say which common reply some careful speakers dislike.

## Further Reading

- [敬語の指針 (Guidelines on Keigo)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/keigo_tosin.pdf) — the 2007 report of Japan's Council for Cultural Affairs, with the five-way classification and 36 worked questions on real dilemmas such as させていただく and とんでもございません; in Japanese, and the final word on what counts as standard.
- [Tae Kim's Guide to Learning Japanese: Polite Form](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/polite) — a clear, free explanation of the ます form and です, with the plain-to-polite conversions drilled in this chapter.
- [Tae Kim's Guide to Learning Japanese: Honorific and Humble Forms](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/honorific) — a compact reference for sonkeigo and kenjōgo verbs and patterns, useful for review.
- [Erin's Challenge! I Can Speak Japanese](https://www.erin.jpf.go.jp/en/) — the Japan Foundation's free video series about an exchange student in Japan; hearing the same characters switch between casual and polite speech shows register in action.
- [Marugoto: Japanese Language and Culture](https://www.marugoto.org/en/) — the Japan Foundation's course, whose situational units model the level of speech appropriate to each everyday scene.
- [NHK World: Learn Japanese](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/learnjapanese/) — free audio and video units from Japan's public broadcaster, with short polite dialogues for daily life.

Register is only half of how real Japanese sounds. In [chapter 4](../04-spoken-japanese/README.md) you leave the tidy sentences of textbooks behind and listen to the language as people speak it: the gap between spoken and written Japanese, the regional dialects that bend the standard and the stylized "role language" that tells you in one sentence whether a character is a samurai, a professor or a schoolgirl.
