# Polite Japanese — The Everyday Standard

Walk into a shop, ask a stranger for directions, meet a new coworker or call a hotel, and you will hear the same two little words holding every sentence together: です (*desu*) and ます (*masu*). They are the Japanese equivalent of "please" and "thank you" built into the verb itself, the baseline of courteous speech with anyone who is not a close friend.

Polite Japanese (丁寧語, *teineigo*, also called ですます体, *desu-masu tai*, the desu-masu style) is the default register for public life in Japan. Shop staff use it with you, you use it with strangers, coworkers use it when they first meet, and textbooks spend most of their time on it. If casual Japanese is what people wear at home, polite Japanese is smart casual: right almost everywhere and almost never a mistake.

## The Core Forms

Two things make a sentence polite: a verb in the ます form and the copula です in place of だ.

### Verbs in the ます Form

The polite form is made from the verb's stem plus ます; [section 2.1](../02-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md) shows how to find the stem for each verb group.

| Plain form | ます form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 行く *iku* | 行きます *ikimasu* | go |
| 食べる *taberu* | 食べます *tabemasu* | eat |
| 飲む *nomu* | 飲みます *nomimasu* | drink |
| 来る *kuru* | 来ます *kimasu* | come |
| する *suru* | します *shimasu* | do |
| 話す *hanasu* | 話します *hanashimasu* | speak |
| 見る *miru* | 見ます *mimasu* | see |
| 読む *yomu* | 読みます *yomimasu* | read |
| 書く *kaku* | 書きます *kakimasu* | write |

### The ます Form in Every Tense

Once you have the stem, the endings are completely regular.

| Tense or mood | Ending | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present or future | 〜ます *-masu* | 食べます *tabemasu* | I eat, I will eat |
| Negative | 〜ません *-masen* | 食べません *tabemasen* | I don't eat |
| Past | 〜ました *-mashita* | 食べました *tabemashita* | I ate |
| Past negative | 〜ませんでした *-masen deshita* | 食べませんでした *tabemasen deshita* | I didn't eat |
| Volitional | 〜ましょう *-mashō* | 食べましょう *tabemashō* | Let's eat |
| Invitation | 〜ませんか *-masen ka* | 食べませんか *tabemasen ka* | Won't you eat with me? |
| Request | 〜てください *-te kudasai* | 食べてください *tabete kudasai* | Please eat |

### The Copula です

| Form | Japanese | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Present | です | *desu* |
| Negative | じゃないです / ではありません (more formal) | *ja nai desu* / *de wa arimasen* |
| Past | でした | *deshita* |
| Past negative | じゃなかったです / ではありませんでした | *ja nakatta desu* / *de wa arimasen deshita* |

> 学生です。\
> *Gakusei desu.*\
> I'm a student.
>
> 昨日は休みでした。\
> *Kinō wa yasumi deshita.*\
> Yesterday was my day off.

## Set Phrases for Everyday Situations

Much of polite Japanese is a repertoire of set phrases. Learn these as whole units and you can handle a remarkable range of situations.

### Introductions

> はじめまして。田中と申します。どうぞよろしくお願いします。\
> *Hajimemashite. Tanaka to mōshimasu. Dōzo yoroshiku onegaishimasu.*\
> Nice to meet you. My name is Tanaka. I look forward to getting to know you.
>
> こちらこそよろしくお願いします。\
> *Kochira koso yoroshiku onegaishimasu.*\
> The pleasure is mine.

申します (*mōshimasu*) is a humble verb from keigo; self-introductions are one place where even beginners use it as a fixed phrase.

### Making Requests

> 〜をお願いできますか？\
> *… o onegai dekimasu ka?*\
> Could I ask you for …?
>
> 〜てください。\
> *… -te kudasai.*\
> Please … (the standard polite request).
>
> 〜ていただけませんか？\
> *… -te itadakemasen ka?*\
> Would you be so kind as to …? (already edging into keigo)

### Offering and Receiving

> どうぞ。\
> *Dōzo.*\
> Please, go ahead. Here you are.
>
> ありがとうございます。\
> *Arigatō gozaimasu.*\
> Thank you very much.
>
> いただきます。\
> *Itadakimasu.*\
> Said before eating: "I humbly receive."
>
> ごちそうさまでした。\
> *Gochisōsama deshita.*\
> Said after eating: "Thank you for the meal."

### Apologies

> すみません。\
> *Sumimasen.*\
> Excuse me. I'm sorry.
>
> 申し訳ありません。\
> *Mōshiwake arimasen.*\
> I'm very sorry. (Stronger than すみません.)
>
> ご迷惑をおかけしました。\
> *Gomeiwaku o okake shimashita.*\
> I'm sorry for the trouble I caused.
>
> 失礼いたしました。\
> *Shitsurei itashimashita.*\
> Excuse my rudeness. (A formal apology for a small lapse.)

## Polite Vocabulary: The お and ご Prefixes

Many nouns gain a polite prefix, お (*o*) or ご (*go*). This prefixing is called **美化語** (*bikago*, beautifying language): it makes speech sound refined without elevating or humbling anyone in particular. Some words almost always take the prefix in everyday speech (お茶, お金), while others take it mainly when they belong to the person you are talking to (お名前, お宅).

| Plain | With prefix | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 家 *ie* | お宅 *otaku* | (your) home |
| 名前 *namae* | お名前 *onamae* | (your) name |
| 弁当 *bentō* | お弁当 *obentō* | boxed lunch |
| 金 *kane* | お金 *okane* | money |
| 茶 *cha* | お茶 *ocha* | tea |
| 腹 *hara* | お腹 *onaka* | stomach (note the different reading) |
| 酒 *sake* | お酒 *osake* | alcohol, sake |
| 見舞い *mimai* | お見舞い *omimai* | a visit to someone who is ill |
| 祝い *iwai* | お祝い *oiwai* | celebration, congratulatory gift |
| 礼 *rei* | お礼 *orei* | thanks, a thank-you gift |

The general pattern:

- お goes with native Japanese words (お茶, お名前) and ご with words of Chinese origin (ご家族 *gokazoku*, ご連絡 *gorenraku*). There are exceptions, such as お電話 (*odenwa*) and お時間 (*ojikan*).
- The prefix is common on words for the other person's things and on shared experiences such as meals and celebrations.
- Loanwords usually go without: コーヒー (*kōhī*) is fine, and おコーヒー sounds affected.

> **Warning:** Don't put お or ご on your own possessions when talking about yourself. 私のお名前 (*watashi no onamae*) sounds as if you are honoring your own name; say 私の名前 instead.

## From Casual to Keigo: One Request, Four Ways

The same request, "Could you show me this?", climbs the politeness ladder like this:

| Level | Japanese | Reading | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casual | 見せて | *misete* | The bare て form, direct |
| Polite | 見せてください | *misete kudasai* | Standard polite |
| More polite | 見せていただけますか？ | *misete itadakemasu ka?* | Uses humble いただく, so already a light form of keigo |
| Full keigo | お見せいただけますでしょうか？ | *omise itadakemasu deshō ka?* | Formal business or service |

Each step adds distance and deference. Choose the step that matches the relationship, not the highest one you know.

## On the Telephone

Phone calls follow their own conventions, and the opening line tells you immediately whether the call is personal or business.

> はい、田中です。\
> *Hai, Tanaka desu.*\
> Hello, this is Tanaka. (A private phone.)
>
> お電話ありがとうございます。〇〇会社でございます。\
> *Odenwa arigatō gozaimasu. 〇〇 kaisha de gozaimasu.*\
> Thank you for calling. This is the 〇〇 Company. (A business line.)
>
> 〇〇さんはいらっしゃいますか？\
> *〇〇-san wa irasshaimasu ka?*\
> Is 〇〇 there, please?
>
> 後ほどまたご連絡いたします。\
> *Nochihodo mata gorenraku itashimasu.*\
> I'll be in touch again later.

〇〇 (read *maru maru*) is the Japanese placeholder for a name, like "so-and-so" or "X".

## Polite Writing: Emails and Letters

Written polite Japanese leans on set openings and closings, much as English emails lean on "I hope you're well" and "Best regards".

### Email Openings

| Phrase | Reading | Use |
|---|---|---|
| お世話になっております。 | *Osewa ni natte orimasu.* | "Thank you for your continued support"; the standard business opener |
| いつもお世話になっております。 | *Itsumo osewa ni natte orimasu.* | The same with "always"; the most common opener of all |
| 突然のご連絡失礼いたします。 | *Totsuzen no gorenraku shitsurei itashimasu.* | "Please excuse this sudden message"; first contact |
| ご無沙汰しております。 | *Gobusata shite orimasu.* | "It has been a long time"; reconnecting |

### Email Closings

| Phrase | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| よろしくお願いいたします。 | *Yoroshiku onegai itashimasu.* | "I appreciate your help"; the all-purpose closing |
| ご確認よろしくお願いいたします。 | *Gokakunin yoroshiku onegai itashimasu.* | "Please kindly check this" |
| 引き続きよろしくお願いいたします。 | *Hikitsuzuki yoroshiku onegai itashimasu.* | "I look forward to continuing to work with you" |
| お返事お待ちしております。 | *Ohenji omachi shite orimasu.* | "I look forward to your reply" |

### Formal Letters

Formal letters open with 拝啓 (*haikei*, a formal salutation) and a seasonal or well-wishing line, and close with 敬具 (*keigu*, "respectfully yours").

> 拝啓　時下ますますご清祥のこととお喜び申し上げます。\
> *Haikei. Jika masumasu goseishō no koto to oyorokobi mōshiagemasu.*\
> Dear Sir or Madam, I am delighted to trust that you are in ever better health.

This level of formality belongs in official letters, invitations to weddings and formal ceremonies, and some business correspondence. For everyday email, start with お世話になっております and you will be fine.

## Saying No Politely

Polite refusals almost never contain a direct "no". Instead, speakers signal difficulty and let the listener draw the conclusion.

| Strategy | Example | Reading | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trailing off | ちょっと難しくて… | *Chotto muzukashikute…* | It's a little difficult… |
| Describing difficulty | なかなか難しい状況でして… | *Nakanaka muzukashii jōkyō deshite…* | The situation makes it rather difficult… |
| Promising to consider | 検討してみます。 | *Kentō shite mimasu.* | I'll think about it. (Often a no.) |
| Redirecting | また次の機会に。 | *Mata tsugi no kikai ni.* | Perhaps another time. |
| Expressing regret | あいにく〜でして… | *Ainiku … deshite…* | Unfortunately, … |

The pause after a trailing refusal is itself the answer. Pressing for a clear yes or no puts the other person in an uncomfortable position and is considered rude.

## Polite Japanese in Service

Shops, restaurants and hotels speak a polished blend of polite forms and keigo. As a customer you mainly need to understand it; you answer in ordinary polite Japanese.

> いらっしゃいませ！\
> *Irasshaimase!*\
> Welcome! (A greeting from staff; no reply is expected.)
>
> こちらへどうぞ。\
> *Kochira e dōzo.*\
> This way, please.
>
> ただいまご案内いたします。\
> *Tadaima goannai itashimasu.*\
> I'll show you to your seat right away.
>
> 少々お待ちくださいませ。\
> *Shōshō omachi kudasaimase.*\
> Please wait a moment.
>
> ご注文はお決まりでしょうか？\
> *Gochūmon wa okimari deshō ka?*\
> Have you decided on your order?
>
> ありがとうございました。またのお越しをお待ちしております。\
> *Arigatō gozaimashita. Mata no okoshi o omachi shite orimasu.*\
> Thank you very much. We look forward to your next visit.

Service speech has its own controversial habits, from こちらコーヒーになります to 1000円からお預かりします; [section 3.5](05-modern-keigo.md) explains them.

## Moving from Polite to Casual

The shift from polite to casual speech usually happens gradually as a relationship warms. Signs that the other person is inviting it:

- They start dropping です and ます themselves.
- They say タメ語でいいよ (*tamego de ii yo*, "you can talk casually").
- They are clearly your age or younger, and relaxed.
- The conversation has turned personal.

When in doubt, stay polite. Polite speech with someone who would prefer casual is a small, easily fixed distance; casual speech with someone who expected politeness is much harder to undo.

## Summary

- Polite Japanese (丁寧語) uses verbs in the ます form and the copula です, and it is the default for strangers, service encounters and most of working life.
- The ます endings are fully regular: 〜ます, 〜ません, 〜ました, 〜ませんでした, 〜ましょう and 〜ませんか.
- The prefixes お and ご (美化語) make nouns sound refined; お generally goes with native words, ご with words of Chinese origin, and neither goes on your own things.
- Set phrases such as よろしくお願いします, お世話になっております and すみません carry much of polite conversation and email.
- Polite refusals avoid a direct "no" and rely on trailing off, expressing difficulty or promising to consider.
