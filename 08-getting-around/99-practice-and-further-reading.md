# Practice and Further Reading

These exercises rehearse the moments you will actually meet: a departure board, a cashier's question, a 119 operator's first words. Say every Japanese answer aloud, because in these situations you will be speaking rather than writing. The items in each group get harder as they go, and the answers are at the back of the book.

## Practice

### 8.1 Trains and Transit

1. Give the reading and meaning of each word: 改札, 乗り換え, 終電, 各駅停車, 指定席.
2. Which of these trains stops at every station, and which is most likely to need an extra ticket: 快速, 特急, 各駅停車?
3. A platform display reads 3番線　快速　新宿行き. Explain in English what it tells you.
4. Translate the announcement: 次は渋谷、渋谷です。お出口は左側です。
5. Your IC card balance is too low and the exit gate closes on you. What machine do you look for? Give its Japanese name and reading.
6. What would you say to a taxi driver to go to Kyoto station, and then to ask for a receipt?
7. Translate into Japanese: "Does this train stop at Shinagawa?" and "Which track do I transfer to?"

### 8.2 Cities and Regions

8. Match each food to its city: 博多ラーメン, 味噌カツ, ジンギスカン, ゴーヤーチャンプルー, たこ焼き. Cities: Osaka, Fukuoka, Nagoya, Sapporo, Naha.
9. Rewrite each Osaka-ben phrase in standard Japanese: めっちゃええやん, ほんまにおおきに, それはあかん.
10. What does 都道府県 stand for, and how many prefectures of each kind does Japan have?
11. A Japanese friend says she is from 中国地方. Where is she from, and why could the word confuse a learner?
12. What does 一見さんお断り mean on the door of a Gion teahouse, and why does the custom exist?

### 8.3 Shopping and Money

13. Read these prices aloud in Japanese: ¥3,980, ¥12,000, ¥30,000, ¥158,000.
14. A price tag shows 税込1,100円 (税抜1,000円). What does each figure mean, and which one do you pay?
15. In a supermarket at 8 p.m. you see a sticker reading 半額 on a bento. What does it mean?
16. The cashier asks ポイントカードはお持ちですか and then 袋はご利用ですか. Answer politely that you don't have a point card and don't need a bag.
17. Translate into Japanese: "Can I pay by card?" and "I'm just looking."
18. On one day you spend ¥4,800 before tax at one store and ¥3,000 at another. Can you shop tax-free at either? Explain.
19. You are visiting Japan in December 2026 and buy a camera tax-free. How is the 10% tax handled, and what must you do before you check in for your flight home?

### 8.4 Emergencies

20. Which number would you call for each: a stolen bag, a friend who has collapsed, a kitchen fire?
21. You dial 119 and hear 火事ですか、救急ですか. Your friend has collapsed and isn't responding. What are your first two sentences?
22. Say in Japanese: "I'm allergic to eggs," "I've lost my passport" and "It hurts here."
23. Your hotel room shakes, and the news reports 震度5強 for your city and マグニチュード6.8. What does each number describe?
24. Your phone shows 避難指示（警戒レベル4）for your area. What does it mean, and what should you do?
25. You are on a beach when you feel a strong, long earthquake and then hear 津波警報. What do the words mean, and what should you do?

## Further Reading

- [Japan Visitor Hotline (JNTO)](https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/hotline/) — the official 24-hour multilingual helpline for visitors, with what it can help with and how to reach it.
- [Safety Tips for Travelers (JNTO)](https://www.jnto.go.jp/safety-tips/eng/) — the official site for the Safety tips alert app, with disaster information and guidance written for visitors.
- [Guide for When You Are Feeling Ill (JNTO)](https://www.jnto.go.jp/emergency/eng/mi_guide.html) — how to find a hospital that accepts foreign patients, what to expect and how insurance works.
- [Japan Rail Pass official site](https://japanrailpass.net/en/) — current prices, what the pass covers and where to buy it, straight from the JR Group.
- [Japan Meteorological Agency](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/indexe.html) — the source of Japan's earthquake, tsunami and weather warnings, with English explanations of the shindo scale and the alert system.
- [Travel Japan (JNTO)](https://www.japan.travel/en/) — the national tourism organization's guide to regions, cities, transport and practical matters.
- [NHK World Learn Japanese](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/learnjapanese/) — free audio courses from the national broadcaster, with short dialogues set in shops, stations and restaurants.
- [Jisho](https://jisho.org/) — a free online dictionary that makes station names, menu items and sign kanji easy to look up.

The phrases in this chapter get you through the door. [Chapter 9](../09-customs-and-daily-life/README.md) is about what happens once you are inside: the customs of daily life, from bowing and gift-giving to food, festivals and the seasons that shape the Japanese year.
