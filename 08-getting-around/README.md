# Chapter 8 — Getting Around Japan

There is a particular thrill in the first sentence of Japanese you use for real: not in a classroom or an app, but at a ticket gate, a ramen counter or a taxi door, with a real person who understands you and answers. Travel is where Japanese stops being a subject and becomes a tool, and the language of getting around is wonderfully learnable, because the same signs, announcements and questions repeat all day, everywhere.

This chapter opens Part III, Japanese in context. It takes you through the systems a visitor or new resident meets in the first week: the railways and buses, the character of the major cities and regions, the shops and the money, and what to say if something goes wrong. Along the way it teaches the vocabulary on every station sign and receipt, the set phrases that staff will say to you and the short answers you need to give back.

Some of this information changes. Fares, passes, payment apps and tax-free rules are given as of 2026, and the most time-sensitive details carry a date. The emergency section is there to help with the language; for safety decisions, always follow local officials and emergency services.

## In This Chapter

- Use IC cards, read train types and station numbers, and follow the standard platform and on-board announcements
- Ride the Shinkansen, buses and taxis with the phrases that each one needs
- Recognize the character, food and key dialect words of Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto and six other major cities
- Read prices in units of 万, handle cash and cashless payment, and shop tax-free under the rules that apply from November 2026
- Understand what cashiers and shop staff say to you, and answer naturally
- Call 110 and 119, describe symptoms and report a loss in simple Japanese
- Recognize earthquake, tsunami and evacuation alerts and the words they use

## Before You Start

You should be able to read hiragana and katakana and recognize the common kanji from [chapter 3](../03-writing-system/README.md), and be comfortable with the basic sentence patterns and set phrases of [chapter 4](../04-building-sentences/README.md). The polite です and ます forms from [chapter 5](../05-politeness/README.md) are all you need to speak to staff, and recognizing a little keigo from that chapter will help you understand what they say back.
