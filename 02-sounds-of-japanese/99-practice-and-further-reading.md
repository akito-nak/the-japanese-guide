# Practice and Further Reading

Pronunciation is a physical skill, so do this practice out loud, with OJAD, Forvo or YouGlish open beside you. Say every Japanese word in the items, check it against a recording, and record yourself for the length and pitch items. The written answers at the back of this book tell you what is right; only your ears, and a Japanese speaker, can tell you whether you said it right.

## Practice

### 2.1 How Japanese Sounds Work

1. Count the morae in each word, and show the beats with hyphens: ねこ (*neko*), ほん (*hon*), きって (*kitte*), にっぽん (*Nippon*), とうきょう (*Tōkyō*), がっこう (*gakkō*), しんぶん (*shinbun*), おばあさん (*obāsan*).
2. How many syllables does each word have in English, and how many morae in Japanese? McDonald's (マクドナルド), Christmas (クリスマス), strike (ストライク), karaoke (カラオケ).
3. Explain why *Tōkyō* has two syllables to an English ear but four morae, and why *kyo* counts as one mora, not two.
4. You are playing しりとり (*shiritori*). With what sound must the next word begin after (a) りんご (*ringo*), (b) とうきょう (*Tōkyō*), (c) ラッパ (*rappa*)? Why does a player who says ほん (*hon*) lose?
5. Look up 寿司 (すし, *sushi*) and ありがとう (*arigatō*) on Forvo or YouGlish and listen to at least four different speakers. What differences between the speakers do you notice, and why is hearing many voices better than replaying one recording?

### 2.2 Vowels and Consonants

6. An English speaker says *sake* as "SAH-kay" and *soko* (there) as "SOW-coe". Describe what is wrong with each vowel and how to fix it.
7. How does ん sound in each word: *m*, *n*, *ng* or the soft nasal at the back of the mouth? さんぽ (*sanpo*), みんな (*minna*), りんご (*ringo*), ほん (*hon*), しんぶん (*shinbun*), まんが (*manga*), でんわ (*denwa*).
8. Count the morae in きねん (*kinen*) and きんえん (*kin'en*), and give their meanings. What does the apostrophe in *kin'en* tell you?
9. Which vowels are usually devoiced in standard Japanese? Mark them: です (*desu*), すき (*suki*), あさ (*asa*), した (*shita*), ひと (*hito*), ちかてつ (*chikatetsu*), なまえ (*namae*), きく (*kiku*).
10. A tour guide says "sunami" for つなみ and pronounces ふじさん with the teeth on the lower lip. Describe how to say つ and ふ correctly.
11. Describe the Japanese *r* and give an English word that contains a similar sound. Which English sound gives away a foreign accent at once?

### 2.3 Long Vowels and Double Consonants

12. Give the meaning and the number of morae of each word in these pairs: おばさん and おばあさん; ここ and こうこう; きて and きって; しゅじん and しゅうじん.
13. Write each word in romaji, marking long vowels with macrons and double consonants with doubled letters, then count its morae: びょういん, びよういん, ざっし, コーヒー, ちいさい, ちょっと.
14. In a bar you ask for *biru*. In an office you ask where the *bīru* is. What did you say in each place, and what did you mean?
15. Why does せんせい usually sound like *sensē* in conversation? Give two more words that follow the same pattern.
16. Based on the training studies by Hirata (2004a), Hirata, Whitehurst and Cullings (2007) and Tajima and colleagues (2008), list four features that a two-week practice plan for hearing length should have.
17. Search YouGlish for おじさん (*ojisan*) and then for おじいさん (*ojīsan*). Listen to five clips of each, from different speakers, and note which you could tell apart without reading the captions. What does the exercise train?

### 2.4 Pitch Accent and Intonation

18. Write the pitch of each word with が after it, using H and L for each mora: 箸 (*hashi*, chopsticks), 橋 (*hashi*, bridge), 端 (*hashi*, edge), 雨 (*ame*, rain), 飴 (*ame*, candy).
19. Name the pattern (heiban, atamadaka, nakadaka or odaka) and write the pitch with が for each word from its accent number: 学生 (がくせい, *gakusei*, student) [0]; 本 (ほん, *hon*, book) [1]; 先生 (せんせい, *sensei*, teacher) [3]; 山 (やま, *yama*, mountain) [2]; 電話 (でんわ, *denwa*, telephone) [0].
20. Said on its own, はな sounds the same whether it means 花 (flower) or 鼻 (nose). Explain why, and how the particle test tells them apart.
21. Explain the two rules of Tokyo pitch accent. Then explain why a word can never be HLH.
22. How does the intonation differ between 行く。 (*Iku.*) and 行く？ (*Iku?*), and what do they mean? What does ね add to いい天気ですね (*Ii tenki desu ne*), and what does よ add to もう九時ですよ (*Mō kuji desu yo*)?
23. Look up 日本語 (にほんご, *nihongo*, the Japanese language) and 先生 in OJAD. Write each pattern in H and L, then type the sentence 先生は日本語を話します (*Sensei wa nihongo o hanashimasu*, The teacher speaks Japanese) into OJAD's Prosody Tutor and describe where the pitch falls.
24. Shadowing: choose a one-minute clip with a transcript, such as an NHK News Web Easy article with audio. Plan five days of shadowing it, and say what you will listen for in your recordings.
25. What did Shport (2008) find about American learners who had studied Japanese for about three years without being taught pitch accent? What does it suggest about how you should learn new words?

## Further Reading

- [OJAD, the Online Japanese Accent Dictionary](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/pages/home) — the University of Tokyo's free tool for the pitch of nouns and every conjugated form of verbs and adjectives, with audio and the Prosody Tutor *Suzuki-kun* for whole sentences.
- 『NHK日本語発音アクセント新辞典』 (*NHK Japanese Pronunciation and Accent Dictionary*, NHK Publishing, 2016) — the broadcaster's standard reference, with about 75,000 entries, also sold as an app with announcers' recordings (as of 2026).
- Timothy J. Vance, *The Sounds of Japanese* (Cambridge University Press, 2008) — a clear introduction for English speakers with no background in linguistics, covering vowels, consonants, morae, accent and intonation, with audio by native speakers and more than fifty exercises.
- [Dogen's Japanese Phonetics series](https://www.patreon.com/dogen) — a paid video course on Patreon, in English, that teaches pitch accent, devoicing and the other topics of this chapter in depth, with many native recordings (as of 2026).
- Yukari Hirata, "Training Native English Speakers to Perceive Japanese Length Contrasts in Word versus Sentence Contexts", *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* 116 (2004) — the study showing that ear training with feedback improves English speakers' hearing of Japanese length, especially when it uses sentences.
- [Forvo](https://forvo.com) and [YouGlish](https://youglish.com/japanese) — free sources of the same word in many voices, for high-variability listening practice.

With the sounds in place, you are ready to see them written. [Chapter 3](../03-writing-system/README.md) teaches hiragana and katakana, where each mora you have learned to hear becomes a character, and then the kanji that give Japanese its meaning on the page.
