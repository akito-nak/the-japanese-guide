# Practice and Further Reading

These exercises mix vocabulary drills, reading, translation and short comprehension questions about the culture. Do them with the book closed first, then check the sections and the answers at the back of the book. For the chat-reading exercises, try to read each line at a glance before you translate it: that is how fans read them.

## Practice

### 8.1 Traditional Arts

1. Give the reading and English meaning of each art: 茶道, 生け花, 歌舞伎, 能, 書道.
2. Read these tea-room words aloud and give their meanings: 抹茶, 茶碗, 茶筅, 床の間.
3. Which aesthetic term fits each description? (a) A cracked, lopsided bowl is more beautiful than a perfect one. (b) The bittersweet feeling as cherry blossoms fall. (c) The empty space around a single branch. (d) The mysterious, suggested beauty at the heart of noh.
4. Why do guests enter a tea room through the low にじり口, and why are female roles in kabuki played by men (女形)?

### 8.2 Traditional Music

5. Match each instrument to its description: 箏, 三味線, 尺八, 笙, 太鼓. (a) End-blown bamboo flute (b) Three-stringed lute played with a large plectrum (c) Drum (d) 13-string zither (e) Mouth organ of bamboo pipes that plays sustained chords.
6. Which piece fills Japanese stores and stations every New Year, and who composed it?
7. Give the reading and meaning of 旅, 涙, 故郷 and 哀愁, then translate: 故郷の歌を聞くと、涙が出る。
8. Where does the name 尺八 come from, and what was the instrument used for by the monks of the Fuke sect?

### 8.3 J-Pop

9. Put these in the order they became prominent: J-pop, Vocaloid, kayōkyoku, city pop.
10. Translate into Japanese: (a) I like city pop. (b) My favorite song is "Lemon." (c) I'm learning Japanese through songs.
11. Read and explain each word: 歌謡曲, 歌い手, 主題歌, ボカロP.
12. What is Hatsune Miku, and how did Ado and Yonezu Kenshi each come out of the Vocaloid scene?

### 8.4 Pop Culture

13. Which label fits each story? (a) A romance manga for teenage girls. (b) A dark, complex story aimed at adult men. (c) An office worker wakes up in a fantasy world. (d) Four friends at a camping club, nothing dramatic happens.
14. Which sound effect would a manga use for (a) a pounding heart, (b) heavy rain, (c) devastating shock, (d) sparkling eyes?
15. What would you say when you want to photograph a cosplayer at an event?
16. Translate both ways: (a) 私の推しはこのキャラです。 (b) "Who is your favorite member?" using 推し.

### 8.5 Vtubers and Streaming

17. Decode each chat comment: 草, 888888, 乙, 神回, 初見です, 待機.
18. Read this live chat and answer the questions. 「待機」「初見です！」「きたあああ」「草」「神回確定」「888888」「おつでした」 (a) Which comment was posted before the stream started? (b) Who is watching for the first time? (c) What happened at the end, and how do viewers say goodbye?
19. Give the streaming term for each: (a) the avatar model itself (b) the performer behind it (c) an edited excerpt of a stream (d) the number of people watching at the same time (e) a paid, highlighted comment.
20. What would you type in chat when (a) you have just arrived ten minutes before a stream starts, (b) it is your first time watching, (c) the stream ends?

### 8.6 Baseball and Sumo

21. Read these baseball terms and give their meanings: 投手, 打者, 三振, 本塁打, 満塁.
22. Translate this radio call: 九回裏、ツーアウト満塁。代打の一振り、ホームラン！逆転サヨナラ勝ち！
23. Put these sumo ranks in order from highest to lowest: 小結, 横綱, 前頭, 大関, 関脇.
24. Wrestler A finishes a tournament 9–6 and wrestler B finishes 6–9. Which word describes each record, and what is likely to happen to each on the next banzuke?
25. Why is the Giants–Tigers rivalry so charged, and what do losing teams at Kōshien famously take home?

## Further Reading

- [Japan Sumo Association](https://www.sumo.or.jp/En/) — the official English site, with tournament results, the current banzuke, wrestler profiles and an explanation of all the kimarite.
- [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://npb.jp/eng/) — NPB's official English site, with schedules, standings and statistics for all 12 teams.
- [Invitation to Kabuki](https://www2.ntj.jac.go.jp/unesco/kabuki/en/) — the Japan Arts Council's bilingual introduction to kabuki, with videos of techniques, stage design and famous plays; its sister site covers noh and kyōgen.
- [the-noh.com](https://www.the-noh.com/en/) — a detailed English introduction to noh and kyōgen, with play summaries, masks and the vocabulary of the stage.
- [Uta-Net](https://www.uta-net.com/) — a free database of official Japanese song lyrics, searchable by artist, title or phrase.
- [Jisho](https://jisho.org/) — a free online dictionary that handles kanji, slang and fan vocabulary well, ideal while reading lyrics or chat.
