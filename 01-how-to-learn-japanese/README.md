# Chapter 1 — How to Learn Japanese

Tell a friend you are learning Japanese and watch their face. Three writing systems, thousands of kanji, verbs parked at the end of every sentence, a politeness system with its own vocabulary: the reputation arrives before the language does, and plenty of people give up on the strength of the reputation alone. They miss the other half of the story. Japanese has five vowels that never change, a spelling system that matches its sounds almost perfectly, a handful of irregular verbs, and no articles, plurals, grammatical gender or tones to fight with.

The hard parts are real, and this book never pretends otherwise. Kanji take years. Listening to fast, casual speech takes thousands of hours. But hard is not the same as mysterious, and every one of those hurdles has been studied: researchers have measured how memory fades and how to stop it, how adults learn to hear sounds their own language ignores, and how much of a page you need to know before reading turns from torture into pleasure. You can put all of that to work from your first week.

What separates the learners who reach fluency from the ones who stall is rarely talent. It is method, and a habit kept for long enough. This chapter sets out the method the rest of this book follows and the evidence behind it, the tools worth a place on your phone, and the map that turns "learn Japanese" into a journey with stops along the way, from your first こんにちは (*konnichiwa*, hello) to reading a novel.

## In This Chapter

- Separate the myths about Japanese from its real hurdles, and see how long the journey takes
- Run the learning loop of study, input, output and review, and know which research supports each step
- Use spaced repetition, retrieval practice, ear training, shadowing and extensive reading, and know what each is good for
- Plan a daily routine that fits your level and shifts as you improve
- Choose a dictionary, a flashcard app, reading and listening sources and a tutor, and type Japanese on your phone and computer
- Read the JLPT's five levels as a map of the whole journey, and set a realistic goal for the next year
