# Practice and Further Reading

Spoken Japanese is learned with the ears, so read every Japanese item in this practice aloud, at natural speed, before you answer it. Most items ask you to move between forms: from textbook Japanese to casual speech and back, from dialect to standard, from a character's voice to plain Japanese. Check your answers at the back of the book, then say the corrected versions aloud once more.

## Practice

### 6.1 Spoken Versus Written Japanese

1. Give the full form of each contraction: (a) 見てる (b) 買っとく (c) 忘れちゃった (d) 行かなきゃ (e) 分かんない (f) 飲んじゃった
2. Contract each phrase as a friend would say it: (a) 読んでいる (b) 準備しておく (c) 食べてしまった (d) 帰らなければ (e) つまらない
3. Rewrite this textbook question as a casual question to a friend: 今晩、映画を見に行きますか？
4. Rewrite each sentence in the written である style: (a) これは大きな問題だ。 (b) 原因は分からなかった。 (c) この件については後で話します。 (use に関して)
5. A sign at a temple reads 撮影するべからず. What does it tell you, and which modern phrase means the same?
6. Which word in each pair has the HL (high, low) pitch pattern in standard Japanese: (a) 箸 or 橋 (b) 雨 or 飴?
7. Put these requests to wait in order from most casual to most formal: (a) 少々お待ちください。 (b) ちょっと待って。 (c) お待ちくださいますようお願い申し上げます。 (d) 少し待ってください。

### 6.2 Dialects

8. Translate these Kansai-ben phrases into standard Japanese: (a) ほんまに？ (b) それ、ちゃうで。 (c) あかん！ (d) 知らへん。 (e) めっちゃおもろい。
9. Which region does each sentence most likely come from? Choose from Kansai, Hakata (Fukuoka), Hiroshima and Nagoya. (a) これ、うまかばい。 (b) 何しとるんじゃ。 (c) それ、どえりゃあうみゃあがや。 (d) 先生、もう帰らはったで。
10. A colleague from Nagoya says 今日はえらかった after a long day. What does she mean? What might a listener from Tokyo misunderstand?
11. Why do linguists describe Okinawan as a separate language rather than a dialect of Japanese?
12. You are visiting Osaka. A shopkeeper hands you your change and says おおきに. What does it mean, and what is a natural way to reply?

### 6.3 Role Language

13. Match each line to its character type (professor, samurai, rich young lady, cat character): (a) 拙者が参るでござる。 (b) わしが知っておるんじゃ。 (c) 楽しみですわ。 (d) お腹すいたニャ。
14. Rewrite each line from exercise 13 in plain, neutral casual Japanese.
15. A learner introduces himself to his new Japanese coworkers with 俺様は田中だぜ. Explain what impression this gives and suggest a better self-introduction.
16. Which "you" word would be safest with a stranger whose name you don't know, and why do most Japanese speakers avoid "you" words altogether?
17. In a manga, 本気 is printed with the furigana マジ. What two things does the reader take in at once?
18. Which sentence ending in exercise 13 or the tables of section 6.3 is common in real conversation among young people, and what does it come from?

## Further Reading

- [Tae Kim's Guide to Learning Japanese: Casual Patterns and Slang](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/casual) — a free, clear tour of the contractions and casual patterns in section 6.1, with many example sentences.
- [Online Japanese Accent Dictionary (OJAD)](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/) — a free tool from the University of Tokyo that shows and plays the pitch accent of words, including every conjugated form of a verb.
- [National Institute for Japanese Language and Linguistics (NINJAL): Databases](https://www.ninjal.ac.jp/database/) — Japan's national language institute, whose dialect atlases and corpora are the primary sources behind most claims about regional speech.
- [『ヴァーチャル日本語 役割語の謎』 by 金水敏 (Kinsui Satoshi)](https://www.kinokuniya.co.jp/f/dsg-08-EK-1685388) — the book that named role language, packed with examples from manga and fiction; in Japanese, and readable at an upper-intermediate level.
- [NHK News Web Easy](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/news/easy/) — real news rewritten in simple Japanese with furigana, the gentlest bridge from spoken style to the written style of the news.

The Japanese in this chapter is still the Japanese of people talking face to face.
