# Chapter 6 — Spoken Japanese

Part I gave you the machinery of Japanese: the scripts, the grammar and the politeness system. Now comes the moment every learner remembers, when you first hear Japanese spoken at full speed by people who aren't performing for a textbook. Particles vanish, verbs shrink, an Osaka shopkeeper says ちゃう where you expected 違う, and the anime you love is full of grandfathers who talk like no grandfather alive.

None of this is chaos. Casual speech contracts in regular, learnable ways. Dialects follow their own consistent rules, and a handful of endings will let you recognize the big ones. Even the theatrical speech of fiction is a system, with a name and a long history. This chapter gives you the keys, so that real conversation, regional speech and the voices of anime and manga start to make sense.

## In This Chapter

- Recognize and produce the common contractions of casual speech, such as 〜てる, 〜ちゃう and 〜なきゃ
- Tell spoken style from written style, including である, formal connectors and classical remnants on signs
- See where pitch accent and intonation fit into spoken registers, building on the full treatment in section 2.4
- Identify Kansai, Hakata, Tohoku, Nagoya and Hiroshima speech from their signature features
- Explain why Okinawan is classed as a separate language
- Read the pronouns and sentence endings that mark character types in fiction, and know which ones not to copy

## Before You Start

You should be comfortable with plain and polite verb forms and with the politeness levels from [chapter 5](../05-politeness/README.md), since casual speech is built from the plain forms of [section 5.2](../05-politeness/02-casual-japanese.md). The kana from [chapter 3](../03-writing-system/README.md) are enough to read every example; kanji come with readings where they matter.
