# Resources for Learning Japanese

This page collects the resources recommended throughout the book, grouped by what they do, with a sentence on what each is best for. Apps, prices and features change often; everything here is as of 2026. [Section 1.3](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/03-apps-courses-and-tools.md) explains how to combine them into a daily routine, [chapter 12](../12-japanese-for-media/README.md) how to learn from Japanese media, and [chapter 14](../14-the-jlpt/README.md) how to prepare for the JLPT.

## Dictionaries

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| Jisho (jisho.org) | A fast, free Japanese-English dictionary that searches by English, romaji, kana, kanji or drawn kanji, with example sentences and the kanji in each word. |
| Yomitan (browser extension) | Pop-up definitions when you hover over Japanese on any web page, with one-click cards for Anki; the core tool for sentence mining. |
| Weblio (weblio.jp) | A large free Japanese-Japanese and Japanese-English dictionary site, with many example sentences. |
| Kotobank (kotobank.jp) | Free entries from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias at once, for when you are ready to look things up in Japanese. |
| Kanji alive (kanjialive.com) | Free kanji entries with stroke-order animations, components and audio for the readings of example words. |

## Grammar References

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar*, by Seiichi Makino and Michio Tsutsui (The Japan Times), with its *Intermediate* and *Advanced* volumes | The standard reference: each pattern explained, compared with its look-alikes and shown in many examples. |
| Tae Kim's Guide to Learning Japanese (guidetojapanese.org) | A free online grammar that teaches casual forms first, the way Japanese is spoken among friends. |
| Imabi (imabi.org) | A free, very detailed online grammar running from beginner topics to classical Japanese. |
| Bunpro | A grammar app that drills patterns by JLPT level with spaced repetition and fill-in-the-blank sentences; subscription after a free trial. |

## Courses and Textbooks

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| *Genki I* and *Genki II* (The Japan Times) | The most widely used university course for beginners, with workbooks and audio, up to about N4. |
| *Tobira: Gateway to Advanced Japanese* (Kurosio) | The bridge from beginner textbooks to intermediate reading, about N3 to N2. |
| Marugoto (Japan Foundation) | A course built on everyday tasks and culture, with free online materials and audio alongside the textbooks. |
| Erin's Challenge! I Can Speak Japanese (Japan Foundation) | Free video skits about a student's life in Japan, with subtitles you can switch between Japanese and English. |
| NHK World Easy Japanese | Free audio and video courses for beginners from Japan's public broadcaster, in many languages. |

## Flashcards and Kanji

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| Anki | Free, configurable spaced-repetition flashcards with audio; free on computers and Android, a one-time purchase on iPhone. [Section 3.4](../03-writing-system/04-studying-kanji.md) suggests settings. |
| WaniKani | Kanji and vocabulary taught in a fixed order through components and mnemonics, with built-in spaced repetition; the first three levels are free, then a subscription. |
| *Remembering the Kanji*, by James W. Heisig | Keyword stories for about 2,200 kanji, built from their components; meanings first, readings later. |
| *The Kodansha Kanji Learner's Course*, by Andrew Scott Conning | About 2,300 kanji in one book, ordered so that shared components and meanings reinforce each other. |
| Tofugu's Learn Hiragana and Learn Katakana, Dr. Moku | Picture mnemonics for every kana, for the first two weeks of study. |

## Graded Reading

| Resource | Level | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese Graded Readers (レベル別日本語多読ライブラリー, Ask Publishing) | Starter and levels 0–4, from absolute beginner | Over 120 short books written by NPO Tadoku Supporters within a controlled vocabulary, with furigana and audio. |
| Taishukan Japanese Readers (Taishukan) | Levels 0–5 | Folk tales, short novels, biographies and culture, heavily illustrated. |
| Web Tadoku Books (tadoku.org) | Starter to level 5 | Free graded readers to read online or print. |
| Satori Reader | Beginner to intermediate | Stories and articles written for learners, with native audio, switchable furigana and notes on every sentence; subscription. |
| NHK News Web Easy | About N4–N3 | Real news rewritten in simpler Japanese, with furigana, pop-up definitions and audio; [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md) explains how to use it. |

## Listening: Podcasts and Video

| Resource | Level | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Nihongo con Teppei for Beginners (podcast) | N5–N4 | Hundreds of three- to four-minute episodes of slow, simple Japanese about everyday life. |
| Comprehensible Japanese (YouTube) | Beginner to intermediate | Slow, clear videos with pictures and gestures that make the meaning plain, ideal comprehensible input. |
| Nihongo con Teppei (podcast) | N3 and up | Longer episodes in natural spoken Japanese on a huge range of topics. |
| NHK for School | N3 and up | Short educational programs made for Japanese schoolchildren, clear and well produced. |
| NHK Radio News, anime and dramas with Japanese subtitles | N3 and up | Native input; [section 12.1](../12-japanese-for-media/01-anime.md) explains how to watch for learning. |

## Pitch Accent and Pronunciation

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| OJAD, the Online Japanese Accent Dictionary (University of Tokyo) | Free: shows and plays the pitch accent of words, every conjugated form of a verb and whole typed sentences. |
| *NHK日本語発音アクセント新辞典* (NHK Publishing, 2016; also as a Monokakido app) | The broadcaster's own accent dictionary, about 75,000 entries, with audio in the app. |
| *新明解日本語アクセント辞典*, second edition (Sanseido, 2014) | The other standard accent dictionary, over 70,000 entries, with audio. |
| Forvo, YouGlish (Japanese) | Many different speakers saying the same word or phrase, useful for the high-variability ear training in [section 2.1](../02-sounds-of-japanese/01-how-japanese-sounds-work.md). |

## Tutors and Conversation

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| italki, Preply | Tutors and teachers by video, from professional teachers to informal conversation partners, at a wide range of prices. |
| HelloTalk, Tandem | Free text, voice and video exchange with Japanese speakers learning English. |
| Japan Foundation centers, universities, libraries, Japanese community groups | Conversation tables, cultural events and volunteering in person. |

## Exams

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| jlpt.jp | The official site of the JLPT: test dates, registration, sample questions and score reports. |
| JLPT Official Practice Workbooks | Full-length practice tests published by the JLPT's organizers, for every level. |
| BJT Business Japanese Proficiency Test (kanken.or.jp/bjt) | A computer-based test of the Japanese used at work, with a score from 0 to 800 instead of a pass or fail; see [chapter 13](../13-business-japanese/README.md). |

## Method

The research behind the method of this book, cited in [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md) and the chapters that apply it, with two books on learning for general readers.

| Work | What it is |
|---|---|
| Stephen Krashen, *Principles and Practice in Second Language Acquisition* (1982) | The case for comprehensible input as the engine of acquisition. |
| Merrill Swain, "Communicative Competence: Some Roles of Comprehensible Input and Comprehensible Output in Its Development" (1985) | The immersion-school evidence that input alone leaves gaps, and the case for output. |
| Richard Schmidt, "The Role of Consciousness in Second Language Learning," *Applied Linguistics* (1990) | The noticing hypothesis: you learn what you notice. |
| Michael Long, "The Role of the Linguistic Environment in Second Language Acquisition" (1996) | How conversation and feedback drive learning. |
| John Norris and Lourdes Ortega, "Effectiveness of L2 Instruction," *Language Learning* (2000) | A meta-analysis showing that explicit instruction produces substantial, lasting gains. |
| Hermann Ebbinghaus, *Über das Gedächtnis* (*Memory*, 1885) | The forgetting curve, measured on himself. |
| Nicholas Cepeda and colleagues, "Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks," *Psychological Bulletin* (2006) | The meta-analysis behind spaced repetition. |
| Jeffrey Karpicke and Henry Roediger, "The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning," *Science* (2008) | Self-testing against restudying: about 80% recall against about 36% a week later. |
| Richard Atkinson and Michael Raugh, "An Application of the Mnemonic Keyword Method to the Acquisition of a Russian Vocabulary," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Learning and Memory* (1975) | The classic test of keyword mnemonics, the principle behind picture hooks for kana and kanji stories. |
| Mary Flaherty and Mary Sisk Noguchi, "Effectiveness of Different Approaches to Kanji Education with Second Language Learners," *JALT Journal* (1998) | Component analysis against whole-kanji copying, in favor of components. |
| Batia Laufer, "What Percentage of Text-Lexis Is Essential for Comprehension?" (1989) | The 95% coverage minimum for reading. |
| Marcella Hu and Paul Nation, "Unknown Vocabulary Density and Reading Comprehension," *Reading in a Foreign Language* (2000) | The 98% coverage threshold for comfortable reading. |
| Scott Logan, John Lively and David Pisoni, "Training Japanese Listeners to Identify English /r/ and /l/," *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* (1991) | The first high-variability phonetic training study, run with Japanese listeners. |
| Ron Thomson, "High Variability [Pronunciation] Training (HVPT)," *Journal of Second Language Pronunciation* (2018) | A review of HVPT across dozens of studies and languages. |
| Yukari Hirata, "Training Native English Speakers to Perceive Japanese Length Contrasts in Word versus Sentence Contexts," *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* 116(4) (2004a) | Ear training for vowel and consonant length, and why sentences beat isolated words. |
| Yukari Hirata, "Computer Assisted Pronunciation Training for Native English Speakers Learning Japanese Pitch and Durational Contrasts," *Computer Assisted Language Learning* 17(3–4) (2004b) | Practice with on-screen pitch contours, the journal version of a 2002 conference report. |
| Yukari Hirata, "Effects of Speaking Rate on the Vowel Length Distinction in Japanese," *Journal of Phonetics* 32(4) (2004c) | How long a Japanese long vowel really is, at slow and fast speaking rates. |
| Irina Shport, "Training English Listeners to Identify Pitch-Accent Patterns in Tokyo Japanese," *Studies in Second Language Acquisition* 38(4) (2016) | Pitch-accent training with feedback, and why the flat heiban pattern is hardest. |
| Yo Hamada, "Shadowing: Who Benefits and How?," *Language Teaching Research* (2016) | Shadowing and listening, with Japanese learners of English. |
| Maribel Montero Perez, Wim Van Den Noortgate and Piet Desmet, "Captioned Video for L2 Listening and Vocabulary Learning: A Meta-Analysis," *System* (2013) | Same-language captions help listening and vocabulary. |
| Holger Mitterer and James McQueen, "Foreign Subtitles Help but Native-Language Subtitles Harm Foreign Speech Perception," *PLoS ONE* (2009) | Why Japanese subtitles beat English ones for training your ear. |
| Yukari Hirata and Spencer Kelly, "Effects of Lips and Hands on Auditory Learning of Second-Language Speech Sounds," *Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research* (2010) | Length training with and without seeing the speaker's mouth. |
| Yukari Hirata, Elizabeth Whitehurst and Emily Cullings, "Training Native English Speakers to Identify Japanese Vowel Length Contrast with Sentences at Varied Speaking Rates," *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* (2007) | Why training at more than one speaking rate works best. |
| Keiichi Tajima and colleagues, "Training English Listeners to Perceive Phonemic Length Contrasts in Japanese," *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* (2008) | What length training does and doesn't transfer to. |
| Yukari Hirata, "Effects of Production Training with Visual Feedback on the Acquisition of Japanese Pitch and Durational Contrasts," ICSLP (2002) | The pitch-display training study whose figures section 1.2 and section 2.4 report. |
| Irina Shport, "Acquisition of Japanese Pitch Accent by American Learners" (2008) | Evidence that intermediate learners don't pick up pitch accent from classroom input alone. |
| Natasha Warner and Takayuki Arai, "Japanese Mora-Timing: A Review," *Phonetica* (2001) | How even the rhythm of morae really is. |
| Chiaki Hitosugi and Richard Day, "Extensive Reading in Japanese," *Reading in a Foreign Language* (2004) | A small classroom study of extensive reading in Japanese. |
| Shuhei Kadota, 『シャドーイングと音読の科学』 (*The Science of Shadowing and Reading Aloud*, 2007) | The book that made shadowing a fixture of language teaching in Japan. |
| Heath Rose, "L2 Learners' Attitudes Toward, and Use of, Mnemonic Strategies When Learning Japanese Kanji," *Modern Language Journal* (2013) | When kanji mnemonics help and when leaning on them hurts. |
| Yoshiko Mori, "The Roles of Context and Word Morphology in Learning New Kanji Words," *Modern Language Journal* (2003) | Kanji components and sentence context work best together. |
| Stephen Krashen, Michael Long and Robin Scarcella, "Age, Rate and Eventual Attainment in Second Language Acquisition," *TESOL Quarterly* (1979) | Adults learn faster than children at first. |
| Joshua Hartshorne, Joshua Tenenbaum and Steven Pinker, "A Critical Period for Second Language Acquisition: Evidence from 2/3 Million English Speakers," *Cognition* (2018) | What the age research does and doesn't say. |
| Harold Pashler and colleagues, "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence," *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* (2008) | Why "visual learner" and "auditory learner" are not a basis for study. |
| *Make It Stick*, by Peter Brown, Henry Roediger and Mark McDaniel (2014) | The memory research behind spaced repetition and retrieval practice, for general readers. |
| *How Languages Are Learned*, by Patsy Lightbown and Nina Spada (fifth edition, 2021) | A readable introduction to second-language acquisition research. |
| *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language*, by Paul Nation (third edition, 2022) | The standard work on vocabulary learning, coverage and extensive reading. |
