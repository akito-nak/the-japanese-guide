# Business Email and Written Japanese — The Art of the Fixed Form

Your first business email in Japanese might take you an hour. You will agonize over how to start, whether "Dear" has an equivalent, how to ask for something without sounding pushy and how to sign off. Then you will discover the secret that makes it all manageable: Japanese business email is one of the most formulaic genres of writing anywhere. The opening, the transitions and the closing come from a short list of set phrases. Learn the list, and the only part you write from scratch is the actual message.

Email (メール, *mēru*) is the main written channel of Japanese business, alongside chat tools in many younger companies. Its structure is fixed, and readers notice when a piece is missing.

## The Shape of a Business Email

Every business email follows the same skeleton, from top to bottom:

1. **Subject line** (件名, *kenmei*): specific and often ending in につきまして or について ("regarding").
2. **Addressee**: company name, department, then the person's name with 様 (*sama*).
3. **Opening greeting**: almost always お世話になっております.
4. **Self-identification**: your company and your name.
5. **Thanks or context**: a line referring to the last contact.
6. **The business itself**, introduced with a transition phrase.
7. **The request and deadline**, softened with an apology for the trouble.
8. **The closing phrase**: よろしくお願いいたします in some form.
9. **Signature block**.

Here is a complete model. The placeholders 〇〇 stand for names you fill in; the full-width spaces and line breaks are part of the convention, since Japanese emails break lines at natural pauses rather than letting the text run.

```text
件名：〇〇のご確認につきまして

〇〇株式会社
〇〇部　田中様

お世話になっております。
株式会社〇〇の山田でございます。

先日はご多忙のところお時間をいただきまして、
誠にありがとうございました。

早速ではございますが、先日ご相談申し上げた件について
ご回答をいただきたく、ご連絡差し上げました。

つきましては、下記についてご確認いただけますでしょうか。

【ご確認事項】
・〇〇について
・〇〇について

ご多忙のところ大変恐縮ですが、
〇月〇日（〇）までにご返答いただけますと幸いです。

何かご不明な点がございましたら、
お気軽にご連絡くださいませ。

引き続きよろしくお願いいたします。

--
山田　太郎
株式会社〇〇　〇〇部
Tel: 〇〇-〇〇〇〇-〇〇〇〇
Email: yamada@example.co.jp
```

In English, the body says roughly: "Thank you for taking the time to meet the other day. Getting straight to the point, I am writing to ask for your answer on the matter we discussed. Could you please confirm the following points? I am very sorry to trouble you when you are busy, but I would be grateful for your reply by (date). If anything is unclear, please feel free to contact me. Thank you for your continued support."

## Openers

The opener does the work of "Dear" and "I hope you're well" at once. The default is so universal that leaving it out looks like a mistake.

| Phrase | When to use |
|---|---|
| お世話になっております | The standard opener for anyone you already deal with |
| いつもお世話になっております | Stresses an ongoing relationship |
| ご無沙汰しております | Reconnecting after a long gap ("it's been a while") |
| 初めてご連絡いたします | First contact with someone |
| 突然のご連絡失礼いたします | Cold outreach, with an apology for writing out of the blue |

**お世話になっております** (*o-sewa ni natte orimasu*) literally means "I am being looked after by you", and it is used even with someone you have never met if your companies already work together. Think of it as a greeting, not a statement of fact.

> 〇〇株式会社の山田でございます。\
> *〇〇 kabushiki-gaisha no Yamada de gozaimasu.*\
> This is Yamada of 〇〇 Corporation.

## Transitions

Transitions move the reader from the courtesies to the business and from one point to the next.

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 早速ですが | Getting straight to the point |
| つきましては | Accordingly, in light of this |
| なお | Additionally, please also note |
| また | Also |
| ご確認いただきたく | I would like to ask you to confirm |

つきましては is the formal hinge between background and request: you explain the situation, then つきましては introduces what you want the reader to do.

## Making the Request

Requests are softened in layers: an apology for the trouble, a request phrased as a question or a hope, and a polite deadline.

> ご多忙のところ大変恐縮ですが、ご確認いただけますでしょうか。\
> *Go-tabō no tokoro taihen kyōshuku desu ga, go-kakunin itadakemasu deshō ka.*\
> I'm very sorry to trouble you when you're busy, but could you please check this?

> 〇日までにご返信いただけますと幸いです。\
> *〇-nichi made ni go-henshin itadakemasu to saiwai desu.*\
> I would be grateful for your reply by the 〇th.

〜いただけますと幸いです ("it would make me happy if I could receive…") is one of the most useful patterns in written business Japanese. It turns any request into something gracious.

## Closers

The closing phrase is the email's handshake. It is never optional.

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| よろしくお願いいたします | Best regards; thank you in advance |
| ご確認のほど、よろしくお願いいたします | Thank you in advance for checking |
| お返事お待ちしております | I look forward to your reply |
| 何かご不明な点がございましたら、お気軽にご連絡ください | If anything is unclear, please feel free to contact me |
| 引き続きよろしくお願いいたします | Thank you for your continued support |

> **Warning:** Don't translate English sign-offs such as "Best regards" or "Sincerely" word for word; Japanese business email has no equivalent, and a literal rendering looks odd. End with よろしくお願いいたします and your signature, and nothing else.

## The Words of Documents and Deadlines

Emails constantly refer to the documents that run a business. These words appear in subject lines, attachments and reminders.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 資料 | *shiryō* | Materials, documents |
| 報告書 | *hōkokusho* | Report |
| 提案書 | *teiansho* | Proposal |
| 見積もり | *mitsumori* | Estimate, quote |
| 契約書 | *keiyakusho* | Contract |
| 請求書 | *seikyūsho* | Invoice |
| 添付 | *tenpu* | Attachment |
| 納期 | *nōki* | Delivery deadline |
| 締め切り | *shimekiri* | Deadline |

> **Tip:** When you attach a file, say so in the body: 資料を添付いたしましたので、ご確認ください ("I have attached the materials; please take a look"). Japanese readers expect the attachment to be announced, not discovered.

## Summary

- Japanese business email follows a fixed order: subject, addressee with 様, greeting, self-identification, body, request, closing phrase and signature.
- お世話になっております is the standard opener, even with people you have not met when your companies already work together.
- つきましては introduces what you want the reader to do after you have explained the background.
- Requests are softened with ご多忙のところ恐縮ですが and phrased as hopes, such as 〜いただけますと幸いです.
- Every business email ends with a form of よろしくお願いいたします.
