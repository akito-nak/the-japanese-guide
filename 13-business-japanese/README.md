# Chapter 13 — Business Japanese

Somewhere between your first 「よろしくお願いします」 and your first day in a Japanese office, the language changes gear. The same polite Japanese that served you in shops and classrooms is suddenly not enough: clients' companies get special names, your boss loses their さん when you mention them to outsiders, and an email that skips お世話になっております looks as odd as a letter with no greeting at all.

The encouraging part is that business Japanese is the most predictable Japanese there is. It runs on set phrases, fixed email structures and well-worn rituals that Japanese graduates themselves learn from books before their first job. Learn the formulas and you can handle a business card exchange, a client email or a phone call with a confidence that would take years to build by improvising.

This chapter is for anyone heading toward Japanese work: job seekers preparing for interviews, people working with Japanese clients or colleagues and learners who want to understand what is happening in every office drama they watch. It covers the words, the written forms and the culture behind them, including the moments when "we will consider it" means no.

## In This Chapter

- Name your company and the other side's company correctly (弊社, 御社, 貴社) and use the core vocabulary of office life
- Introduce yourself at a new workplace or interview with 申します and よろしくお願いいたします
- Exchange business cards with the right choreography and phrase
- Write a complete Japanese business email, from お世話になっております to the signature
- Open, steer and close a meeting, and recognize a polite refusal when you hear one
- Answer and make business phone calls, including taking messages for absent colleagues
- Explain 報連相, 根回し and the traditional patterns of Japanese company life

## Before You Start

This chapter assumes polite Japanese and keigo. Work through [chapter 5](../05-politeness/README.md) first, especially [section 5.3](../05-politeness/03-polite-japanese.md) on です/ます and [section 5.4](../05-politeness/04-honorific-japanese.md) on honorific and humble forms; the gap between spoken and written style in [section 6.1](../06-spoken-japanese/01-spoken-vs-written.md) also helps. Grammar around JLPT N3 (see [chapter 14](../14-the-jlpt/README.md)) is a comfortable starting point.
