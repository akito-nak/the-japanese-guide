# Chapter 9 — History

## 9.1 Japanese History in Brief

1. 明治 *Meiji*, 大正 *Taishō*, 昭和 *Shōwa*, 平成 *Heisei*, 令和 *Reiwa*.
2. 幕府 (*bakufu*) — shogunate, military government; 大名 (*daimyō*) — regional feudal lord; 鎖国 (*sakoku*) — "closed country," the Edo policy of restricted foreign contact; 明治維新 (*Meiji Ishin*) — the Meiji Restoration of 1868; 戦国時代 (*Sengoku jidai*) — the Warring States period.
3. 令和元年 = 2019; 平成31年 = 2019 (January to April); 昭和64年 = 1989 (January 1–7 only); 令和8年 = 2026.
4. 1964 = 昭和39年 (1964 − 1925); 1995 = 平成7年 (1995 − 1988); 2020 = 令和2年 (2020 − 2018).
5. The Tale of Genji: Heian. Tea ceremony and Noh: Muromachi. Ukiyo-e and kabuki: Edo. The first shogunate: Kamakura. The first constitution (1889): Meiji.
6. They are defined differently. "Sengoku" describes a condition, a century of civil war that historians end anywhere from 1568 to 1615, while the Edo period starts with a political event, Tokugawa Ieyasu becoming shogun in 1603. Fighting continued until the fall of Osaka Castle in 1615, so the two labels overlap.

## 9.2 Samurai and Bushido

7. 侍 (*samurai*) — warrior, retainer; 武士道 (*bushidō*) — the way of the warrior; 切腹 (*seppuku*) — ritual suicide; 浪人 (*rōnin*) — masterless samurai (today also a student retaking entrance exams); 義理 (*giri*) — social obligation; 人情 (*ninjō*) — human feeling, compassion.
8. (a) 敵に塩を送る (*teki ni shio o okuru*), to send salt to your enemy. (b) 義理チョコ (*giri choko*), obligation chocolate. (c) 残心 (*zanshin*), lingering awareness.
9. 覚悟しろ！ — "Prepare yourself!" or "Brace yourself!" (a challenge, common in anime). 面目ない。 — "I'm ashamed," "I have no face to show."
10. Model answer: the list (gi, yū, jin, rei, makoto, meiyo, chūgi) comes from Nitobe Inazō's *Bushido: The Soul of Japan*, written in English and published in 1900, after the samurai class had been abolished. Historians are cautious because it presents an idealized, unified code that real warriors, who often changed sides for land and pay, did not consistently follow.
11. The abolition of the domains (1871), the conscription law (1873), the Sword Abolition Edict (1876), the Satsuma Rebellion (1877).

## 9.3 Postwar Japan

12. 戦後 (*sengo*) — postwar; 終戦 (*shūsen*) — the end of the war; 復興 (*fukkō*) — reconstruction, recovery; 少子化 (*shōshika*) — declining birthrate; 引きこもり (*hikikomori*) — social withdrawal.
13. 三種の神器 (TV, washing machine, refrigerator): late 1950s. The 3C (color TV, air conditioner, car): 1960s. バブル経済: late 1980s to about 1991. 就職氷河期: the 1990s and early 2000s.
14. "It is no longer the postwar period." It announced that Japan's economy had recovered to its prewar level and that growth would now have to come from modernization, and it became a slogan for the start of the high-growth era.
15. Model answer: Article 9 renounces war as a sovereign right and the threat or use of force to settle international disputes, and says war potential will never be maintained. The tension is that Japan created the Self-Defense Forces in 1954, and how far they may legally go has been debated ever since; the constitution has never been amended.
16. Any two: the tōyō kanji list (1,850 characters), which led to today's 2,136 jōyō kanji and limits the kanji you meet in newspapers; simplified character forms (国 instead of 國), which are the forms you learn; modern kana usage (現代かなづかい), which makes spelling follow pronunciation (きょう, not けふ).
17. The Great East Japan Earthquake and tsunami of March 11, 2011 (東日本大震災). It is read *san ten ichi ichi*. She means "When 3.11 happened, I was in Tokyo."
