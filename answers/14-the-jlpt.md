# Chapter 14 — The JLPT

## 14.1 How the JLPT Works

1. False: they fail. The total of 120 is above N3's overall pass mark of 95, but 15 in listening is below the sectional pass mark of 19, and every section must reach its minimum.
2. N4 and N5. Their scoring sections are Language Knowledge and Reading (0–120, sectional pass mark 38) and Listening (0–60, sectional pass mark 19).
3. Because the JLPT has not published official vocabulary, kanji or grammar lists since the 2010 revision. Every count is a community estimate, mostly based on the pre-2010 lists, so sources differ.
4. Speaking and writing. (Free conversation, or any production of Japanese, is also an acceptable answer.)

## 14.2 N5: The Starting Line

5. 電車 (*densha*) train; 毎日 (*mainichi*) every day; 学校 (*gakkō*) school; 先生 (*sensei*) teacher.
6. (b) を: わたしはまいあさコーヒーをのみます。"I drink coffee every morning." を marks the object.
7. (b) さむかった: きのうはとてもさむかったです。"It was very cold yesterday." The past of an い-adjective is 〜かった, and です stays unchanged.
8. On Sunday, Yamada saw a movie with friends, and afterward had dinner at a restaurant.

## 14.3 N4: Building Confidence

9. (a) おきます: "Before the party, I'll buy drinks (in advance)." 〜ておく means doing something in preparation.
10. (b) 行った: 日本に行ったことがありますか。"Have you ever been to Japan?" Experience is た-form + ことがある.
11. (a) そう: 雨が降りそうです。"It looks like it's going to rain." The verb stem + そうだ describes what looks about to happen, here based on the dark sky.
12. No, the library is closed next Monday because it is a public holiday. To return a book that day, put it in the return box at the entrance.

## 14.4 N3: The Great Leap

13. (b) せいで: "Because I overslept, I was late for the meeting." せいで marks the cause of a bad result; おかげで is for good results.
14. (a) らしい: "According to the weather forecast, it will apparently snow tomorrow." らしい reports information heard from another source.
15. さえ: 時間さえあれば、旅行に行けるのに。"If only I had the time, I could go traveling."
16. Mixed, leaning toward regret. The writer accepts that many people welcome the convenience, but notes (一方で) that small, long-established shops are losing customers, and sadly mentions that their own favorite bakery closed last month (閉店してしまった, with the regretful 〜てしまう).

## 14.5 N2: Near Fluent

17. (a) 伴って: "As the birth rate falls, the number of schools is decreasing." 〜に伴って links one change to another.
18. (a) からには: "Once you have taken something on, you should see it through." からには means "now that, since".
19. 根拠 (*konkyo*) grounds, basis; 傾向 (*keikō*) tendency, trend; 矛盾 (*mujun*) contradiction; 前提 (*zentei*) premise, precondition.
20. Remote work has the advantage of no commute, but many people feel isolated because casual conversations with colleagues disappear; so introducing the system alone is not enough, and the real question is how to create opportunities for interaction. The point comes after とはいえ and in the final sentence.

## 14.6 N1: The Summit

21. 試行錯誤 (*shikō sakugo*) trial and error; 本末転倒 (*honmatsu tentō*) putting the cart before the horse, confusing what matters with what doesn't; 一石二鳥 (*isseki nichō*) killing two birds with one stone.
22. (a) べく: "A team of experts was formed in order to solve the problem." 〜べく is a formal "in order to" attached to the dictionary form (する becomes す).
23. (a) 余儀なくされた: "Because of the heavy rain, the match was forced to be canceled."
24. "I enjoyed food you can only get in Kyoto." (Model answer; "cuisine unique to Kyoto" is also correct.)
25. The writer suggests that in chasing convenience we may have forgotten the value of waiting, and that the days spent waiting for a reply to a letter were time spent thinking about the other person. Yes: 〜ではあるまいか is a rhetorical question that presents the writer's own view while sounding modest.
