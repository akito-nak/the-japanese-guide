# Chapter 10 — Arts, Entertainment and Sport

## 10.1 Traditional Arts

1. 茶道 (*chadō* or *sadō*) — the Way of Tea; 生け花 (*ikebana*) — flower arrangement; 歌舞伎 (*kabuki*) — popular classical theater; 能 (*nō*) — noh, masked classical theater; 書道 (*shodō*) — calligraphy.
2. 抹茶 (*matcha*) — powdered green tea; 茶碗 (*chawan*) — tea bowl; 茶筅 (*chasen*) — bamboo whisk; 床の間 (*tokonoma*) — alcove for a scroll and flowers.
3. (a) 侘び寂び (*wabi-sabi*). (b) 物の哀れ (*mono no aware*). (c) 間 (*ma*). (d) 幽玄 (*yūgen*).
4. The にじり口 is so low that everyone must bow and crawl in, which makes all guests equal regardless of rank. Kabuki has been performed by all-male casts since women were banned from the stage in 1629, so male specialists, the onnagata, developed playing women into an art of its own.

## 10.2 Traditional Music

5. 箏 (d); 三味線 (b); 尺八 (a); 笙 (e); 太鼓 (c).
6. 春の海 (*Haru no Umi*, "The Sea in Spring"), written in 1929 by the koto master 宮城道雄 (*Miyagi Michio*).
7. 旅 (*tabi*) — journey; 涙 (*namida*) — tears; 故郷 (*furusato*) — hometown; 哀愁 (*aishū*) — pathos, wistful sadness. "When I hear songs from my hometown, I cry" (literally, "tears come out").
8. The name is the instrument's standard length, 一尺八寸 (*isshaku hassun*, one shaku and eight sun), about 54.5 cm. The monks of the Fuke sect played it as meditation, a practice called 吹禅 (*suizen*, "blowing Zen").

## 10.3 J-Pop

9. Kayōkyoku (1960s–80s), city pop (late 1970s–80s), J-pop (from about 1990), Vocaloid (from 2007).
10. (a) シティポップが好きです。 (b) 一番好きな曲は「Lemon」です。 (c) 歌で日本語を勉強しています。 Other natural versions are fine, such as 歌を通して日本語を学んでいます。
11. 歌謡曲 (*kayōkyoku*) — the orchestrated popular song of the 1960s to 1980s; 歌い手 (*utaite*) — a singer who posts cover versions online; 主題歌 (*shudaika*) — a theme song for an anime, film or drama; ボカロP (*bokaro-P*) — a Vocaloid producer who writes songs for synthesized voices.
12. Hatsune Miku is a Vocaloid singing-synthesizer voice with a character attached, released by Crypton Future Media in 2007. Yonezu Kenshi began as a Vocaloid producer under the name ハチ (*Hachi*), while Ado began as an utaite, a cover singer posting versions of Vocaloid songs.

## 10.5 Vtubers and Streaming

17. 草 — lol; 888888 — applause (pachipachi); 乙 — thanks, good work, bye (from お疲れ様); 神回 — an amazing, "god-tier" stream or episode; 初見です — I'm a first-time viewer; 待機 — standing by, waiting for the stream to start.
18. (a) 待機, typed while waiting for the stream to start. (b) The viewer who typed 初見です！ (c) Something amazing happened (きたあああ "here it is!", 草 laughter, 神回確定 "this is officially a legendary stream," 888888 applause), and viewers say goodbye with おつでした, "thanks for the stream."
19. (a) ガワ. (b) 中の人 or 魂. (c) 切り抜き. (d) 同接. (e) スパチャ (Super Chat).
20. (a) 待機. (b) 初見です. (c) おつ, 乙 or おつでした (also お疲れ様でした).

## 10.6 Baseball and Sumo

21. 投手 (*tōshu*) — pitcher; 打者 (*dasha*) — batter; 三振 (*sanshin*) — strikeout; 本塁打 (*honruida*) — home run; 満塁 (*manrui*) — bases loaded.
22. "Bottom of the ninth, two outs, bases loaded. One swing from the pinch hitter: a home run! A come-from-behind walk-off win!"
23. 横綱, 大関, 関脇, 小結, 前頭.
24. Wrestler A has a 勝ち越し (*kachikoshi*, winning record) and will usually move up the banzuke; wrestler B has a 負け越し (*makekoshi*, losing record) and will usually move down.
25. Model answer: the Giants are Tokyo's establishment team, owned by the Yomiuri newspaper group and the most successful in Japanese baseball, while the Tigers carry Osaka's pride, so the rivalry mirrors Tokyo against Osaka. Losing high school teams at Kōshien scoop up the stadium's infield dirt to take home as a keepsake.
