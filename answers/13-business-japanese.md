# Chapter 13 — Business Japanese

## 13.1 Workplace Language and Introductions

1. 上司 (*jōshi*) superior, boss; 同僚 (*dōryō*) colleague; 取引先 (*torihikisaki*) business partner, client; 担当者 (*tantōsha*) person in charge.
2. 貴社 (*kisha*). It is the written form; 御社 is spoken, and 弊社 means your own company.
3. "The person in charge at our company will contact you."
4. 本日からお世話になります、スミスと申します。(*Honjitsu kara o-sewa ni narimasu, Sumisu to mōshimasu.*) Ending with どうぞよろしくお願いいたします is a natural addition.
5. Say 頂戴いたします (*chōdai itashimasu*), receiving the card with both hands. During the meeting, keep it on the table in front of you (on your card holder), arranged to match the seating if there are several; never pocket it, write on it or leave it behind.
6. 正社員: regular employee; 派遣社員: agency worker; 残業: overtime; 有給休暇: paid leave.

## 13.2 Business Email and Written Japanese

7. (c) 件名 → (b) 〇〇株式会社　田中様 → (d) お世話になっております → (f) 株式会社〇〇の山田でございます → (e) the request → (a) よろしくお願いいたします.
8. ご無沙汰しております ("it's been a long time").
9. 添付 (*tenpu*): 資料を添付いたしましたので、ご確認ください。
10. 金曜日までにご返信いただけますと幸いです。(*Kin'yōbi made ni go-henshin itadakemasu to saiwai desu.*) ご返答 or お返事 in place of ご返信 is also correct.
11. It is the formal hinge meaning "accordingly" or "in light of this": after you explain the background, つきましては introduces what you want the reader to do, usually the request itself.
12. Model answer:\
    〇〇株式会社\
    田中様\
    お世話になっております。株式会社△△の山田でございます。

## 13.3 Meetings, Phone Calls and Office Culture

13. "If I may, let's get straight to the main topic." (Literally "It's sudden, but please let me enter the main subject.")
14. 承知いたしました. Many people consider 了解しました too casual toward a superior or client, while 承知いたしました is humble and safe with anyone.
15. Most likely a polite no. "That's a little difficult" in business usually means the answer is negative, unless it is followed by a concrete next step.
16. 申し訳ございません。鈴木はただいま席を外しております。Note 鈴木 alone, with no 部長 or さん, because you are speaking to an outsider about your own colleague.
17. 戻りましたら、折り返しお電話いたします。(*Modorimashitara, orikaeshi o-denwa itashimasu.*)
18. 報告 (report): "I finished the client presentation; here is how it went." 連絡 (contact): "The supplier says delivery will be two days late; I'm letting the team know." 相談 (consult): "The client wants a discount; how should I respond before I reply?" Any examples that report results, share information and seek advice before acting are correct.
19. Model answer: talk one-on-one with the key people (your manager and the most influential team members) before the meeting, show them the tool informally, listen to their objections and adjust the proposal, so that the team meeting confirms an agreement already reached rather than hearing the idea cold.
