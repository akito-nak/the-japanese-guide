# Chapter 1 — The Writing System

## 1.1 Hiragana

1. さくら *sakura* (cherry blossom), すし *sushi*, ねこ *neko* (cat), ともだち *tomodachi* (friend), ひらがな *hiragana*.
2. かぜ *kaze* (wind; also a cold), ごはん *gohan* (cooked rice, a meal), でんわ *denwa* (telephone), ぶどう *budō* (grapes), えんぴつ *enpitsu* (pencil).
3. *kite* and *kitte*; *saka* and *sakka*; *oto* and *otto*. In each second word the small っ adds a one-beat pause that doubles the following consonant. (きて means "come", きって a postage stamp; さか a slope, さっか a writer; おと a sound, おっと a husband.)
4. *obasan* and *obāsan*: an aunt or middle-aged woman, and a grandmother or elderly woman. *yuki* and *yūki*: snow and courage. The added vowel makes the sound twice as long.
5. きょう *kyō* (today), しゃしん *shashin* (photograph), りょこう *ryokō* (trip), じしょ *jisho* (dictionary), ぎゅうにゅう *gyūnyū* (milk).
6. びょういん (*byōin*) is the hospital and びよういん (*biyōin*) is the beauty salon. In びょういん the よ is small, so びょ is one syllable; in びよういん the よ is full size, so び and よ are separate syllables.
7. ありがとう, おはよう, がっこう, せんせい, とうきょう.
8. *Watashi wa gakusei desu.* (I am a student.) *Gakkō e ikimasu.* (I go to school.) *Mizu o nomimasu.* (I drink water.) As particles, は is read *wa*, へ is read *e* and を is read *o*.
9. く 1, し 1, ん 1, あ 3, ふ 4.
10. *nu* ぬ, *me* め, *ne* ね, *re* れ, *ru* る, *ro* ろ. ぬ and る end in a loop; め and ろ do not; ね ends in a loop and れ in an outward kick.

## 1.2 Katakana

11. テレビ *terebi* (television), カメラ *kamera* (camera), ホテル *hoteru* (hotel), パン *pan* (bread), ケーキ *kēki* (cake).
12. ビル (*biru*) is a building; ビール (*bīru*) is beer. The long vowel mark ー doubles the length of the *i*.
13. アイスクリーム *aisu kurīmu* (ice cream), コンピューター *konpyūtā* (computer), エレベーター *erebētā* (elevator), ハンバーガー *hanbāgā* (hamburger), スマートフォン *sumātofon* (smartphone).
14. アメリカ, タクシー, クリスマス, ジュース, ゲーム.
15. ツアー *tsuā* (a tour), シャツ *shatsu* (a shirt), ソース *sōsu* (sauce), ペン *pen* (a pen), ミシン *mishin* (a sewing machine, from "machine").
16. パーティー *pātī* (party), ファン *fan* (a fan, as in a supporter, or an electric fan), ディナー *dinā* (dinner), フェリー *ferī* (ferry), ウィキペディア *Wikipedia*.
17. マンション is an apartment or condominium building, スマート means slim or stylish, カンニング means cheating on a test, and バイク usually means a motorcycle.
18. アルバイト from German (*Arbeit*), パン from Portuguese (*pão*), アンケート from French (*enquête*), ランドセル from Dutch (*ransel*).
19. Model answers: Emma エマ, Chris クリス, Sarah サラ, Michael マイケル, David デイビッド. Names follow their pronunciation, so the same spelling can come out differently depending on how its owner says it.

## 1.3 Kanji

20. (a) The water radical 氵 (from 水): sea, swim, wash. (b) The person radical 亻 (from 人): rest, make, body. (c) The speech radical 言: talk, read, language. (d) The tree radical 木: forest, desk, root.
21. 鮭: fish (it means salmon). 猫: an animal (cat). 鉄: metal (iron). 晴: sun or weather (clear weather). 鶏: bird (chicken).
22. (a) Kun-yomi, やま *yama*. (b) On-yomi, さん *san* (ふじさん *Fujisan*). (c) Kun-yomi, みず *mizu*. (d) On-yomi, すい *sui* (すいようび *suiyōbi*, Wednesday). (e) On-yomi, にん *nin* (さんにん *sannin*, three people).
23. 入口 *iriguchi* (entrance), 出口 *deguchi* (exit), 電車 *densha* (train), 大学 *daigaku* (university), 新聞 *shinbun* (newspaper), 地震 *jishin* (earthquake).
24. Paper 紙, god 神, hair 髪.
25. (a) The horizontal comes first. (b) The stroke sweeping down to the left comes first. (c) The center comes first. (d) The contents come first; the bottom line is written last, sealing the box.
26. 山 has 3 strokes, 木 has 4 and 国 has 8.
27. 食 and 飲 are kanji; べる and みます are okurigana. The okurigana carry the grammatical ending, so the kanji stays the same while the verb changes form.
28. *Tanaka-san wa sūpā de pan o kaimashita.* Mr. or Ms. Tanaka bought bread at the supermarket. Kanji: 田中 (Tanaka) and 買 (the stem of "buy"). Hiragana: さん, は, で, を and the ending いました. Katakana: スーパー (supermarket) and パン (bread).

## 1.4 Studying Kanji

29. (a) Remembering the Kanji, with its keyword stories. (b) WaniKani. (c) Context first: look kanji up as you read and mine sentences into Anki.
30. At about 12 p.m., four hours later, at the first Apprentice stage. Each new item comes back several times in its first few days, so 50 new items become a flood of a few hundred reviews within two days, and the backlog grows faster than you can clear it.
31. (a) Hard. (b) Again. (c) Easy. (d) Good.
32. Front: 毎朝新聞を読みます。 Back: I read the newspaper every morning. 新聞 (しんぶん, *shinbun*) — newspaper.
