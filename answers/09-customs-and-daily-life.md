# Chapter 9 — Customs and Daily Life

## 9.1 Culture and Norms

1. 内 (*uchi*): inside, the in-group. 外 (*soto*): outside, people beyond the in-group. 建前 (*tatemae*): the public position, what is appropriate to say. 本音 (*honne*): one's true feelings. 迷惑 (*meiwaku*): trouble or inconvenience caused to others.
2. Model answer: いえいえ、まだまだです。(*Ie ie, mada mada desu.*) Other good answers: そんなことないです and まだ勉強中です.
3. Almost certainly no. ちょっと… trailing off and 検討します (we'll consider it) are polite, indirect refusals that let both sides avoid an open disagreement.
4. Take your shoes off in the lowered genkan area and step up onto the raised floor in socks or the slippers provided, turning your shoes to face the door if you can. At the toilet, change into the toilet slippers, and change back into the house slippers when you leave.
5. Model answer: よかったらどうぞ, or the traditional つまらないものですが、どうぞ. Offer it with both hands. A set of four is avoided because 四 can be read *shi*, like 死 (death).
6. 田中は席を外しております is correct. Speaking to a client (soto), your own manager belongs to your uchi, so you use the surname without a title and humble language (おります). いらっしゃいません would honor your own boss in front of an outsider.
7. 根回し (*nemawashi*) is consulting everyone informally before a meeting so that the meeting confirms a decision rather than fighting over it. The word comes from gardening: preparing a tree's roots before moving it. It avoids public disagreement and loss of face, which protects harmony (和).
8. Any four of these: wash thoroughly before entering the bath; no swimwear; keep your small towel out of the water; don't put your head under the water; check the tattoo policy in advance; keep your voice down.

## 9.2 Food

9. 焼き魚 (*yakizakana*): grilled fish. 揚げ出し豆腐 (*agedashi dōfu*): deep-fried tofu in broth. 蒸し鶏 (*mushidori*): steamed chicken. 野菜炒め (*yasai itame*): stir-fried vegetables.
10. おすすめは何ですか。わさび抜きでお願いします。
11. It is お通し (*otōshi*), a small appetizer served automatically and charged as a cover fee. It is normal, not a mistake.
12. 豚骨: Fukuoka (Hakata). 味噌: Sapporo. 醤油: Tokyo.
13. はい、お願いします。
14. Model answer: 出汁は何を使っていますか。Dashi is usually made with bonito flakes (fish), so soups, noodle broths and simmered dishes that look vegetarian often aren't.
15. お会計お願いします。別々でお願いします。As you leave: ごちそうさまでした。
## 9.3 Matsuri

16. Hina Matsuri: March 3. Kodomo no Hi: May 5. Tanabata: July 7 (August 7 in some regions; Sendai, August 6–8). Setsubun: around February 3.
17. 鬼は外！福は内！(*Oni wa soto! Fuku wa uchi!*): "Demons out! Good fortune in!"
18. Gion Matsuri (Kyoto), Tenjin Matsuri (Osaka) and Kanda Matsuri (Tokyo).
19. The left side goes over the right, so the right panel is underneath. Right over left is how the dead are dressed for burial, so getting it wrong is a well-known faux pas.
20. あけましておめでとうございます。今年もよろしくお願いします。初詣 (*hatsumōde*) is the first visit of the year to a shrine or temple, usually in the first three days of January.
21. Model answer: JLPTのN3に合格できますように。

## 9.4 Seasons and Nature

22. Spring: うぐいす, 花見. Summer: 蝉, 梅雨. Autumn: 紅葉, 名月. Winter: こたつ, 雪.
23. 蒸し暑いですね。寒くなりましたね。
24. 残暑お見舞い申し上げます. August 20 is after the traditional start of autumn (立秋, about August 7), so the heat is "lingering heat" (残暑); 暑中見舞い is sent only until then.
25. 食欲の秋 (*shokuyoku no aki*) is "autumn, the season of appetite", when food is at its best. Model answers: 読書の秋 (autumn for reading), 芸術の秋 (autumn for the arts), スポーツの秋 (autumn for sports).
26. ふるいけや (5), かわずとびこむ (7), みずのおと (5). The kigo is 蛙 (*kawazu*, frog), which marks the poem as spring, and the kireji is や after 古池.
