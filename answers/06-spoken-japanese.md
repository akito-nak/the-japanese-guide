# Chapter 6 — Spoken Japanese

## 6.1 Spoken Versus Written Japanese

1. (a) 見ている (b) 買っておく (c) 忘れてしまった (d) 行かなければ (short for 行かなければならない) (e) 分からない (f) 飲んでしまった
2. (a) 読んでる (b) 準備しとく (c) 食べちゃった (d) 帰らなきゃ (e) つまんない
3. 今晩、映画見に行く？ (*Konban, eiga mi ni iku?*) The particle を and the question particle か drop, ます becomes the plain form, and rising intonation carries the question.
4. (a) これは大きな問題である。 (b) 原因は分からなかった。 (already plain; in a formal report, 原因は不明であった is also possible) (c) この件に関しては後で述べる。
5. It means "photography is prohibited" (do not take pictures). The modern equivalent is 撮影してはいけません, or on a modern sign 撮影禁止.
6. (a) 箸 (chopsticks) is HL; 橋 (bridge) is LH. (b) 雨 (rain) is HL; 飴 (candy) is LH.
7. (b) ちょっと待って, (d) 少し待ってください, (a) 少々お待ちください, (c) お待ちくださいますようお願い申し上げます.

## 6.2 Dialects

8. (a) 本当に？ (Really?) (b) それ、違うよ。 (That's wrong.) (c) だめ！ (No! / That's no good!) (d) 知らない。 (I don't know.) (e) とても面白い。 (Really funny.)
9. (a) Hakata (Fukuoka): the か-adjective うまか and the ending ばい. (b) Hiroshima: とる for ている and じゃ for だ. (c) Nagoya: どえりゃあ (very), うみゃあ (tasty) and the ending がや. (d) Kansai: the honorific 〜はる and the ending で.
10. In Nagoya and much of central Japan, えらい means "tiring", so she means "today was exhausting". A listener from Tokyo might hear the standard meaning "great, admirable" and think she is praising herself or the day.
11. Okinawan belongs to the Ryukyuan branch of the Japonic family. It is related to Japanese, but speakers of the two cannot understand each other without study, which is the usual test for a separate language. UNESCO also lists it separately as an endangered language.
12. It means "thank you", a traditional Kansai expression heard from shopkeepers and older speakers. A natural reply is a simple ありがとうございます or, if you are feeling brave, おおきに back with a smile.

## 6.3 Role Language

13. (a) Samurai (拙者 and 〜でござる). (b) Professor or elderly sage (わし, 〜おる and 〜じゃ). (c) Rich young lady (〜ですわ). (d) Cat character (〜ニャ).
14. Model answers: (a) 私が行くよ。 (or, as a humble form, 私が参ります。) (b) 私が知ってるんだ。 (c) 楽しみだね。 / 楽しみです。 (d) お腹すいた。
15. 俺様 is the arrogant "the great me" of villains and spoiled princes, and 〜だぜ is a rough, swaggering ending. Together they make him sound like a cartoon character and could come across as rude. A better introduction is 田中です。よろしくお願いします。 (or, more formally, 田中と申します。よろしくお願いいたします。).
16. Use the person's name or title instead of a pronoun where possible. If you must use a word for "you" with a stranger, あなた is the least risky, but even it can sound distant or cold, which is why most speakers avoid "you" words: many have decayed from polite to rude over time, and names with さん are both safer and warmer.
17. The kanji 本気 give the written meaning, "seriousness, in earnest", while the furigana マジ give the slangy spoken word the character actually says, "for real". The reader gets the formal meaning and the casual voice at the same time.
18. 〜っす, a casual-polite contraction of です. Young people, especially in sports clubs and part-time jobs, use it to seniors they are close to, as in お疲れっす.
