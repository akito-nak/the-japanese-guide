# Chapter 5 — The Living Language

## 5.1 Internet Language and Slang

1. (a) lmao, loud laughter (w comes from 笑, *warai*). (b) lol; something is funny. (c) "thanks for your work", "good job" (from お疲れ様). (d) "by the way" (from ちなみに).
2. Laughter was written w, from the first letter of *warai* (笑). A long row of w's (wwwwww) looks like grass, so people started writing 草 (*kusa*, grass) for laughter instead.
3. (a) リア充: someone whose offline life is full of friends and romance; from リアル (real) and 充実 (fulfilled). (b) ぼっち: being alone, a loner; from ひとりぼっち (all alone). (c) バズる: to go viral; from English "buzz" plus the verb ending る. (d) 推し: your favorite member, character or creator; from 推す (to push, to recommend), originally in idol fandom.
4. Someone read their message (it shows 既読, "read") but did not reply. They feel left on read.
5. (a) よろしく (yo-ro-shi-ku), "nice to meet you" or "best regards". (b) おはよう (o-ha-yo-u), "good morning". (c) サンキュー (san-kyū), "thank you".
6. (a) Awkward or nervous; the ; is a drop of sweat. (b) Crying, sad. (c) Defeat or despair: a figure on hands and knees.
7. "This new song is so emotional" or "this new song really gets me". エモい is positive here: nostalgic, bittersweet, moving.

## 5.2 Contemporary and Emerging Japanese

8. (a) "Exactly." / "So true." (b) "This ramen is the best I've ever had." (c) "My fave is so precious it physically hurts." (しんどい in its fan sense, overwhelming joy, not a complaint.)
9. 〜界隈 (*kaiwai*) originally means "neighborhood, vicinity"; as slang it means "the … crowd, the people into …" and attaches to any hobby or habit. 風呂キャンセル界隈 is "the cancel-my-bath crowd": people too exhausted to take a bath tonight, used as self-deprecating humor.
10. For many young speakers, 普通に is a sincerity marker: 普通においしい means "genuinely tasty". For many older listeners, 普通に still means "ordinarily", so he hears "it's merely average".
11. (a) ググる, past ググった (b) バズる, past バズった (c) ディスる, past ディスった. All conjugate as godan verbs.
12. 役不足 traditionally means the role is too small for the person, which is the compliment you intend, but roughly half of speakers use it the other way round, to mean the person is not up to the role. It could sound like an insult. Safer: 〇〇さんにはもっと大きな仕事が向いていると思います (I think bigger work suits you) or praise her ability directly.
13. (b) ぴえん超えてぱおん, (c) インスタ映え and (e) り sound dated or cringeworthy as of 2026. (a) それな and (d) 過去一 are still current.
14. It is Japan's New Words and Buzzwords Award, organized since 1984 by the publisher 自由国民社, with nominees announced in November and the top ten and grand prize announced in early December. It leans toward news, sport, politics and television, so many winners are words people heard rather than words they say; the 2024 grand prize ふてほど was criticized for exactly that.

## 5.3 How Japanese Evolved

15. (b) the Kojiki (712), (e) The Tale of Genji (early 11th century), (d) 浮雲 (1887), (f) the kana spelling reform (1946), (c) emoji for i-mode (1999), (a) the jōyō revision (2010).
16. Man'yōgana is the use of kanji purely for their sounds to write Japanese, as in the Man'yōshū. Hiragana developed from cursive, simplified forms of whole man'yōgana characters (あ from 安); katakana developed from parts of characters used by Buddhist monks as reading aids (ア from 阿). Both took shape in the Heian period.
17. Chinese pronunciations reached Japan in separate waves from different periods and regions. ぎょう is go-on (5th–6th centuries, southern China), こう is kan-on (7th–9th centuries, Tang-dynasty Chang'an) and あん is tō-on (from the 12th century, Song and later China). Each wave left its reading in different words.
18. (a) philosophy (b) society (c) telephone (d) liberty, freedom
19. パン: Portuguese. コーヒー: Dutch. アルバイト: German. テレビ: English.
20. The 1946 kana spelling reform respelled words according to modern pronunciation but deliberately kept the historical spellings of the particles は, へ and を, so は keeps the kana for "ha".
21. Genbun itchi ("unifying speech and writing") was a Meiji-era movement to write in a style close to the spoken language instead of classical written Japanese (bungo). It produced the modern written styles based on です・ます and である, so that today's written Japanese is close to speech.
