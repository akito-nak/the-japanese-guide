# Role Language — 役割語 (Yakuwarigo) 🎭

> *"No real Tokyo grandfather says 「わしが知っておるんじゃ」. No real heiress ends every sentence with 「ですわ」. But the moment you hear those lines, you picture exactly who's speaking. That's role language — and anime, manga, and games run on it."*

**役割語 (yakuwarigo)** is a stylized way of speaking that instantly signals a character's *type* — their age, gender, class, era, region, or personality — from a single line. The term was coined by linguist **金水敏 (Kinsui Satoshi)** in his 2003 book *ヴァーチャル日本語 役割語の謎*.

This page is essential for two reasons:
1. **It unlocks fiction.** Once you can read role language, [anime](../tracks/anime-lover.md) and [manga](../tracks/manga-reader.md) characters tell you who they are before the plot does.
2. **It warns you what *not* to copy.** Role language is a *convention for the audience*, not a transcript of real speech. Speaking it in real life ranges from charmingly odd to genuinely strange.

> **⚠️ The one rule that matters:** Role language is **fictional**. Recognize it for comprehension; don't adopt it as your own Japanese. Heavy role language often marks a character as a *stereotype* (脇役) rather than a real person — that's part of how it works.

## First-Person Pronouns — Who Says "I"

English has one word for "I." Japanese has dozens, and the choice is a costume. This single chart explains a huge amount of character speech.

| Pronoun | Reading | Who uses it / what it signals |
|---|---|---|
| 私 | watashi | Universal neutral-polite, both genders. For men, reads formal/businesslike |
| 私 | watakushi | More formal and refined — heiresses, butlers, formal speeches |
| 僕 | boku | Boys and soft/intellectual men. Polite, gentle, the "nice guy" |
| 俺 | ore | Rough, casual, masculine. Tough guys, and ordinary men among friends |
| 俺様 | ore-sama | "The great me" — arrogant princes and villains |
| あたし | atashi | Casual feminine — softer/girlier than わたし (real women use it too) |
| あたい | atai | Rough, working-class or tomboy girl. Old downtown (下町) flavor; dated |
| うち | uchi | A girl's casual "I" — strongly **Kansai**-flavored, now widespread among young women |
| わし | washi | Elderly men and bosses. The "old geezer" register (West-Japan dialect, transplanted) |
| 拙者 | sessha | Samurai/ninja — "this unworthy one." Pairs with 〜でござる |
| 我輩 / 吾輩 | wagahai | Pompous, self-important older man. From Sōseki's *吾輩は猫である* |
| おいら | oira | Rustic, easygoing country boy. Folksy and low-key masculine |
| 朕 | chin | The **Emperor**, in formal edicts. (Hirohito used it in the 1945 surrender broadcast) |
| 麻呂 | maro | Heian court noble — the aristocrat caricature. Archaic |

## Second-Person Pronouns — Who Says "You" (and How Rudely)

A recurring pattern: Japanese "you" words tend to **decay from polite to rude** over centuries. 貴様 and お前 both started as honorifics.

| Pronoun | Reading | Politeness | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| あなた | anata | Neutral-polite, but distancing | Polite yet cold; also a wife → husband form. *Not* safe with superiors |
| 君 | kimi | Mild, slightly downward | Superior → junior, boy → girl, intimate. Condescending if aimed upward |
| お前 | omae | Rough, downward | Close male friends — or contempt. Originally honorific (御前), degraded over time |
| てめえ | temē | Very rude, aggressive | Picking a fight. From 手前 |
| 貴様 | kisama | Very rude, hostile | Spat at enemies. Like 御前, *started* honorific and fell |
| 汝 | nanji | Archaic, literary | Bible, fantasy "thou" — commanding or scriptural |
| おぬし | onushi | Archaic, peer-or-below | Samurai-era, toward equals or inferiors |
| お宅 | otaku | Polite-distant, stiff | Toward someone whose name you don't know. (Yes — *this* noun later named the subculture) |

## Sentence Enders & Copulas — The Character Tags (キャラ語尾)

The way a sentence *ends* is often the loudest signal of all. These markers are how a translator's "ye olde" or "kawaii" flavor gets built in the original.

| Ender | Reading | Archetype | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 〜じゃ / 〜のじゃ | -ja / -no ja | Professor / elderly (博士キャラ) | わしが博士じゃ |
| 〜ぜ / 〜ぞ | -ze / -zo | Rough, cool male | 行くぜ／やるぞ |
| 〜わ / 〜のよ / 〜かしら | -wa / -no yo / -kashira | Feminine "women's language" | そうなのよ／どうかしら |
| 〜ですわ / 〜ですの | -desu wa / -desu no | Rich young lady (お嬢様) | 楽しみですわ |
| 〜でござる | -de gozaru | Samurai / ninja | 拙者が参るでござる |
| 〜ニャ / 〜にゃん | -nya / -nyan | Cat characters | お腹すいたニャ |
| 〜だっちゃ | -daccha | Space alien — Lum from *Urusei Yatsura* (based on Tōhoku dialect) | 好きだっちゃ |
| 〜なのだ / 〜のだ | -nano da | Quirky/childlike — Bakabon's Papa | これでいいのだ |
| 〜っす | -ssu | Casual-polite junior (real slang!) — sporty younger men | お疲れっす |
| 〜なり | -nari | Classical copula, or robot/mascot characters | 我は機械なり |
| 〜アル | -aru | Stereotyped Chinese character | 私やるあるよ ⚠️ *(see below)* |

## The Archetypes at a Glance

Put the pronoun, the enders, and a few extra features together and you get a recognizable "kit":

| Archetype | "I" | Enders | Other tells |
|---|---|---|---|
| 博士キャラ (professor/sage) | わし | 〜じゃ / 〜おる | 知っておる, slow grave tone |
| お嬢様 (rich lady) | わたくし | 〜ですわ / 〜ですの | ごきげんよう, "ohohoho" laugh, lavish vocabulary |
| 武士 / 侍 (samurai) | 拙者 / 我輩 | 〜でござる / 〜なり | 〜申す, かたじけない, archaic verbs |
| 田舎者 (country bumpkin) | おら / おいら | 〜だべ / 〜ずら / 〜だっぺ | A composite *fake* dialect — no single real region talks this way |
| 関西人キャラ (Kansai) | うち / わて | 〜や / 〜やで / 〜やん / 〜へん | ほんま, あかん, なんでやねん — comedy association |
| 男の子 / 女の子 | 僕・俺 / あたし | 〜だ・〜ぜ / 〜わ・〜かしら | Boys assertive; girls softer, more final particles |
| オネエ言葉 | あたし | 〜のよ / 〜じゃない | *Exaggerated* women's language layered over a male voice — campy and performed |

---

## ⚠️ The Stereotypes Worth Flagging

Role language is built from stereotypes, and a few have aged badly. Knowing them is part of media literacy — but treat them as *history*, not models.

- **〜アル / 中華キャラ (the "Chinese" voice):** Comes from 協和語 (kyōwa-go), a broken Japanese associated with the colonial Manchukuo era. It's now widely recognized as an **ethnic caricature**. Understand it when you meet it in old media; never imitate it.
- **女言葉 (〜わ / 〜かしら / 〜のよ):** Today this is largely a *fictional and older-generation* convention. Most Japanese women under ~50 don't actually speak this way — but anime heroines and subtitles keep it alive, which can mislead learners into thinking it's standard "female Japanese." It isn't.
- **オネエ言葉:** A performed, exaggerated register — not an accurate representation of how LGBTQ+ people actually speak.

## Bonus: Furigana Wordplay (義訓 / ルビ表現)

Manga has a second trick for character and emotion: making the **furigana say something different from the kanji**. The kanji carries the literal/written meaning; the small reading above carries what's *spoken* or felt. You read both at once.

| Written | Furigana | Literal meaning | The effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 本気 | マジ (maji) | "seriousness" | Formal kanji, slangy spoken voice — at the same time |
| 機械鎧 | オートメイル (Automail) | "machine armor" | English ruby names the prosthetic (*Fullmetal Alchemist*) |
| 負けて死ね | バイツァ・ダスト (Bites the Dust) | "lose and die" | Kanji = the lethal command; English = the Queen-song attack name (*JoJo*) |
| 親友 | ライバル (rival) | "close friend" | Contrasting ruby compresses a whole relationship into one word |
| 永遠 | とわ | "eternity" (vs えいえん) | A softer, more poetic reading |

This is the creative extreme of **義訓 (gikun)** — improvised, meaning-based readings. (Compare the *fixed*, dictionary versions: 梅雨 = つゆ, 大人 = おとな.) More on this, plus sound effects and silence (シーン), in the **[Manga Reader track](../tracks/manga-reader.md)**.
