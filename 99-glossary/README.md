# Glossary

Every term defined in this book, A to Z. Japanese terms are listed under their romanization, with the Japanese script in the definition; each entry links to the section that explains it.

### A

**Accent nucleus** — アクセント核, the mora after which the pitch of a word falls. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Aizuchi** — 相槌, the short responses such as うん, はい and なるほど that a listener gives during a conversation to show they are following. [section 4.3](../04-building-sentences/03-phrases-and-idioms.md)

**Article 9** — 憲法第9条, the clause of Japan's 1947 constitution that renounces war and the maintenance of war potential. [section 11.3](../11-history/03-postwar-japan.md)

**Atamadaka** — 頭高, "head-high"; the pattern that falls after the first mora; accent number 1. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

### B

**Baburu keizai** — バブル経済, the bubble economy of about 1986 to 1991, when land and share prices soared before collapsing. [section 11.3](../11-history/03-postwar-japan.md)

**Baito keigo** — バイト敬語, the polite formulas spread by service-industry training manuals, such as こちらコーヒーになります and 1000円からお預かりします; also called manual keigo or konbini keigo. [section 5.5](../05-politeness/05-modern-keigo.md)

**Bakufu** — 幕府, a shogunate: the military government through which shoguns ruled Japan from 1185 to 1867 while the emperor reigned as a ceremonial figure. [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Banzuke** — 番付, the official sumo ranking list, published before every tournament. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Bikago** — 美化語, "beautifying language": the お and ご prefixes that make nouns sound refined, as in お茶 and ご家族. [section 5.3](../05-politeness/03-polite-japanese.md)

**BJT** — The Business Japanese Proficiency Test, scored from 0 to 800. [section 1.4](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/04-the-jlpt-map.md)

**Bon odori** — 盆踊り, the simple circle folk dances performed during Obon, which anyone can join. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Bungo** — 文語, classical written Japanese, based on the language of the Heian period and the standard for formal writing until the late 19th century. [section 7.3](../07-living-language/03-language-evolution.md)

**Burakku kigyō** — ブラック企業, a "black company" that exploits its employees with excessive overtime and poor treatment. [section 13.3](../13-business-japanese/03-meetings-and-office-culture.md)

**Bushidō** — 武士道, "the way of the warrior," the idealized ethical code of the samurai, largely systematized in the Edo and Meiji periods. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Business Japanese** — ビジネス日本語 (*bijinesu nihongo*), the vocabulary, set phrases and written conventions of professional life in Japan. [section 13.1](../13-business-japanese/01-workplace-language.md)

**Buzzword of the Year** — 新語・流行語大賞, Japan's annual New Words and Buzzwords Award, announced each December since 1984. [section 7.2](../07-living-language/02-contemporary-japanese.md)

### C

**Casual speech** — The plain-form register used with close friends, family and people younger than you, without です and ます; see tamego. [section 5.1](../05-politeness/01-levels-overview.md)

**CEFR** — The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages, whose six levels run from A1 to C2. [section 1.4](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/04-the-jlpt-map.md)

**Chadō** — 茶道 (also read *sadō*), the Way of Tea, the formal preparation and serving of powdered green tea. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Chikan** — 痴漢, groping or molestation, usually on crowded trains, and the person who commits it; a crime in Japan. [section 8.4](../08-getting-around/04-emergencies.md)

**Chōonpu** — 長音符, the long vowel mark ー used mainly in katakana, as in コーヒー (kōhī). [section 3.2](../03-writing-system/02-katakana.md)

**City pop** — シティポップ, the slick, funky Japanese pop of the late 1970s and 1980s that found a global audience online in the late 2010s. [section 10.3](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/03-j-pop.md)

**Clip** — 切り抜き (*kirinuki*), an edited excerpt of a livestream, often made by fans. [section 10.5](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/05-vtubers-and-streaming.md)

**Community estimate** — a word or kanji count for a JLPT level compiled by teachers and publishers, since the JLPT has published no official lists since 2010. [section 14.1](../14-the-jlpt/01-overview.md)

**Comprehensible input** — Language you hear or read and mostly understand, with a little that stretches you; the main engine of acquisition. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Concurrent viewers** — 同接 (*dōsetsu*), the number of people watching a livestream at the same moment, the key popularity metric. [section 10.5](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/05-vtubers-and-streaming.md)

**Copula** — The linking word that says what something is, です (polite) or だ (casual), the closest Japanese equivalent of "to be". [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Cosplay** — コスプレ, dressing up as characters from anime, manga and games, from "costume play." [section 10.4](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/04-pop-culture.md)

**Counter** — 助数詞 (josūshi), a suffix attached to a number that depends on the shape or kind of thing being counted, such as 枚 for flat things or 匹 for small animals. [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Cushion words** — クッション言葉 (kusshon kotoba), set phrases such as 恐れ入りますが placed before a request or refusal to soften it. [section 5.5](../05-politeness/05-modern-keigo.md)

### D

**Daimyō** — 大名, a regional feudal lord who ruled a domain under (or against) the shogun. [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Daishō** — 大小, the pair of long and short swords whose wearing marked a samurai in the Edo period. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Dakuten** — The two-stroke mark ゛ that voices a kana's consonant, turning か (ka) into が (ga). [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Danmaku** — 弾幕 ("barrage"), the flood of comments scrolling across a video at a big moment, pioneered by Niconico. [section 10.5](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/05-vtubers-and-streaming.md)

**Dashi** — 出汁, a light stock usually made from kelp and dried bonito flakes, the base of much Japanese cooking. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

**De aru tai** — である体, the plain written style that uses である as its copula, used in editorials, academic papers and many novels. [section 6.1](../06-spoken-japanese/01-spoken-vs-written.md)

**Depachika** — デパ地下, the basement food hall of a department store, selling sweets, prepared dishes and gifts. [section 8.3](../08-getting-around/03-shopping-and-money.md)

**Diphthong** — A vowel that glides from one quality to another within a syllable, like the English *ay* in "say"; Japanese vowels do not glide. [section 2.2](../02-sounds-of-japanese/02-vowels-and-consonants.md)

**Dohyō** — 土俵, the raised clay sumo ring, 4.55 m across, treated as a sacred space. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Double keigo** — 二重敬語 (nijū keigo), stacking two honorifics of the same kind on one word, as in おっしゃられる; generally considered excessive. [section 5.4](../05-politeness/04-honorific-japanese.md)

### E

**Enka** — 演歌, Japan's sentimental ballad genre, with minor keys, kobushi ornaments and themes of longing, travel and sake. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Extensive listening** — Listening to a lot of material easy enough to follow without pausing, for the gist and for pleasure. [section 12.1](../12-japanese-for-media/01-anime.md)

**Extensive reading** — Reading a lot of easy material quickly, for pleasure, without stopping to look up every word. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

### F

**Flick input** — A phone keyboard layout in which each key holds a row of the kana chart; you tap for the *a* sound or flick for the others. [section 1.3](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/03-apps-courses-and-tools.md)

**Fukidashi** — フキダシ (吹き出し), a speech or thought bubble in manga, whose shape signals how a line is delivered. [section 12.2](../12-japanese-for-media/02-manga.md)

**Fukubukuro** — 福袋, a "lucky bag" of goods sold sealed at a fixed price in the New Year sales. [section 8.3](../08-getting-around/03-shopping-and-money.md)

**Furigana** — Small kana printed above or beside kanji to show how they are read, common in children's books and learner materials. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

### G

**Gagaku** — 雅楽, the ancient court music of the imperial household, performed at court since the 8th century. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Gairaigo** — 外来語, a loanword borrowed into Japanese from another language, usually written in katakana. [section 3.2](../03-writing-system/02-katakana.md)

**Gannen** — 元年, the first year of an era, written instead of 1年 (令和元年 = 2019). [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Genbun itchi** — 言文一致, the Meiji-era movement to write in a style close to the spoken language instead of classical bungo. [section 7.3](../07-living-language/03-language-evolution.md)

**Gengō** — 元号, an imperial era name such as 令和, used to count years in the Japanese calendar (和暦). [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Genkan** — 玄関, the entrance area of a home, where shoes are removed before stepping up to the raised floor. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Gikun** — 義訓, a reading assigned to kanji by meaning rather than by their usual sounds, from fixed words such as 大人 (おとな) to creative furigana in manga. [section 6.3](../06-spoken-japanese/03-role-language.md)

**Giongo** — 擬音語, sound-symbolic words that imitate actual sounds, such as ザーザー for pouring rain. [section 4.3](../04-building-sentences/03-phrases-and-idioms.md)

**Giri** — 義理, social obligation owed to people who have helped you, to your employer or to your community. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Gitaigo** — 擬態語, sound-symbolic words that depict silent states, movements or feelings, such as ワクワク for excited anticipation. [section 4.3](../04-building-sentences/03-phrases-and-idioms.md)

**Godan verb** — 五段動詞, a u-verb whose final syllable shifts across the vowel rows when it conjugates (書く, 書きます, 書かない). [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Gojūon** — 五十音, "fifty sounds", the grid that arranges the basic kana by vowel column and consonant row. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Goroawase** — 語呂合わせ, wordplay that reads numbers as words, as in 4649 for よろしく. [section 7.1](../07-living-language/01-internet-and-slang.md)

**Graded reader** — A book written within a fixed list of words and kanji for a given level. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Gyaru** — ギャル, a youth fashion subculture of the 1990s and 2000s whose playful, contracted speech fed much vocabulary into Japanese slang. [section 7.1](../07-living-language/01-internet-and-slang.md)

**Gyokuon hōsō** — 玉音放送, the "Jewel Voice Broadcast" of August 15, 1945, in which Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's surrender. [section 11.3](../11-history/03-postwar-japan.md)

### H

**Haiku** — 俳句, a short poem of three phrases in a 5-7-5 pattern of sound units, traditionally containing a season word. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

**Haitōrei** — 廃刀令, the Sword Abolition Edict of 1876, which banned the wearing of swords in public. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Hanami** — 花見, "flower viewing": gathering under the cherry blossoms to eat, drink and enjoy them in spring. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Hanamichi** — 花道 ("flower path"), the raised walkway through a kabuki audience used for dramatic entrances and exits. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Hanashikotoba** — 話し言葉, spoken-style Japanese, with its contractions, dropped particles and sentence-final particles. [section 6.1](../06-spoken-japanese/01-spoken-vs-written.md)

**Handakuten** — The small circle ゜ that turns the h row of kana into p, as in ぱ (pa). [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Hatsumōde** — 初詣, the first visit of the year to a shrine or temple, usually in the first days of January. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Heiban** — 平板, "flat"; the pitch-accent pattern with no fall, in which a following particle stays high; accent number 0. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Heisha** — 弊社, "our (humble) company", the humble word for your own employer when speaking to outsiders. [section 13.1](../13-business-japanese/01-workplace-language.md)

**High-variability phonetic training** — HVPT, ear training with many words spoken by many voices and immediate feedback, used for hard sound contrasts such as vowel length and pitch accent. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Hikoku** — 被告, "defendant," the title news media attach to a person's name after indictment. [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)

**Hiragana** — ひらがな, the rounded phonetic script of 46 basic characters used for native words, grammatical endings and particles. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Hōgen** — 方言, a regional dialect with its own pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar and pitch accent, often named with 弁 (*ben*) as in 関西弁. [section 6.2](../06-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md)

**Honbasho** — 本場所, one of the six 15-day grand sumo tournaments held each year. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Honne** — 本音, a person's true feelings, shared mainly with people they trust. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Hōrensō** — 報連相, the workplace habit of reporting (報告), sharing information (連絡) and consulting (相談) with your superior early and often. [section 13.3](../13-business-japanese/03-meetings-and-office-culture.md)

**Hyaku-en shop** — 100円ショップ, a store where most items cost ¥100 before tax, such as Daiso or Seria. [section 8.3](../08-getting-around/03-shopping-and-money.md)

**Hyōjungo** — 標準語, standard Japanese, the variety based on educated Tokyo speech used in national news, schools and textbooks. [section 6.2](../06-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md)

### I

**I-adjective** — い形容詞, an adjective ending in い that conjugates by changing that い (大きい, 大きくない, 大きかった). [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**IC card** — ICカード, a rechargeable contactless card such as Suica, PASMO or ICOCA that pays train and bus fares and small purchases nationwide. [section 8.1](../08-getting-around/01-trains-and-transit.md)

**Ichi-go ichi-e** — 一期一会 ("one time, one meeting"), the tea-ceremony ideal of treating each encounter as unrepeatable. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Ichidan verb** — 一段動詞, a ru-verb ending in -iru or -eru whose stem never changes: drop る and add the ending (食べる, 食べます). [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Ichigen-san okotowari** — 一見さんお断り, "no first-timers," the custom of some Kyoto teahouses of accepting new guests only through an existing customer's introduction. [section 8.2](../08-getting-around/02-cities-and-regions.md)

**Idol** — アイドル, a young performer marketed on personality and closeness to fans as much as on music. [section 10.4](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/04-pop-culture.md)

**Ikebana** — 生け花 (also 華道 *kadō*), the art of flower arrangement, using few stems, asymmetry and empty space. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Iki** — 粋, the cool, understated urban stylishness prized by Edo-period townspeople. [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Input method editor** — IME, software that turns typed romaji or kana into Japanese text, offering kanji to replace the kana. [section 1.3](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/03-apps-courses-and-tools.md)

**Intensive listening** — Studying a short clip closely, replaying, looking up and shadowing it until every word is understood. [section 12.1](../12-japanese-for-media/01-anime.md)

**Intensive reading** — Slow, close study of a short, difficult text, looking up what you don't know. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Intonation** — The pitch movement of a whole phrase, which carries questions and attitude on top of word accent. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Irregular verb** — One of the two Japanese verbs, する (do) and 来る (come), that follow neither the godan nor the ichidan pattern. [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Izakaya** — 居酒屋, a casual Japanese pub serving drinks and small plates for sharing. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

### J

**J-pop** — Jポップ, the broad name for Japanese popular music since about 1990. [section 10.3](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/03-j-pop.md)

**Japan Rail Pass** — a pass for foreign visitors giving unlimited travel on most JR trains for seven, 14 or 21 days (¥50,000 for seven days online, ¥53,000 through agencies, as of October 2026). [section 8.1](../08-getting-around/01-trains-and-transit.md)

**Japonic** — The language family made up of Japanese and the Ryukyuan languages. [section 1.1](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/01-why-japanese.md)

**JFT-Basic** — The Japan Foundation Test for Basic Japanese, a computer-based test of everyday Japanese used for the Specified Skilled Worker visa. [section 1.4](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/04-the-jlpt-map.md)

**Jikō no aisatsu** — 時候の挨拶, the seasonal greeting that traditionally opens a formal letter, such as 盛夏の候. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

**JLPT** — the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (日本語能力試験, *Nihongo Nōryoku Shiken*), the most widely taken Japanese certification, with five levels from N5 to N1. [section 14.1](../14-the-jlpt/01-overview.md)

**Jōyō kanji** — 常用漢字, the government's list of 2,136 kanji for everyday use (2010 revision), taught in schools and used as the standard for newspapers. [section 3.3](../03-writing-system/03-kanji.md)

**Jukugo** — 熟語, a compound word made of two or more kanji, such as 電車 (densha, train). [section 3.3](../03-writing-system/03-kanji.md)

### K

**Kabuki** — 歌舞伎, Japan's flamboyant popular classical theater with all-male casts, performed since the early 17th century. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Kachikoshi** — 勝ち越し, a winning record in a sumo tournament (eight or more wins out of 15), which usually brings promotion. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Kaishakunin** — 介錯人, the assistant who beheaded a samurai performing seppuku to end his suffering. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Kakikotoba** — 書き言葉, written-style Japanese, the compact formal language of news, documents and academic writing. [section 6.1](../06-spoken-japanese/01-spoken-vs-written.md)

**Kanji** — 漢字, the meaning-carrying characters borrowed from Chinese, most of which have both on-yomi and kun-yomi readings. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Kaomoji** — 顔文字, Japanese emoticons read upright and built around the eyes, such as (^_^) and (T_T). [section 7.1](../07-living-language/01-internet-and-slang.md)

**Katakana** — カタカナ, the angular phonetic script that writes the same sounds as hiragana and is used mainly for loanwords, foreign names, sound effects and emphasis. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Kawaii** — かわいい ("cute"), the aesthetic and industry of cuteness, from mascots to fashion. [section 10.4](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/04-pop-culture.md)

**Kayōkyoku** — 歌謡曲, the orchestrated Japanese popular song that dominated from the 1960s through the 1980s. [section 10.3](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/03-j-pop.md)

**Keigo** — 敬語, honorific language: the system of respectful, humble and polite forms that encodes the relationship between speaker, listener and the person discussed. [section 5.1](../05-politeness/01-levels-overview.md)

**Keikai reberu** — 警戒レベル, the five-step alert level scale for evacuation information, in which level 4 (避難指示) means everyone should evacuate. [section 8.4](../08-getting-around/04-emergencies.md)

**Kenjōgo** — 謙譲語, humble language: verbs and expressions that lower the speaker or the speaker's group, such as 参る, 申す and お〜する. [section 5.4](../05-politeness/04-honorific-japanese.md)

**Kenson** — 謙遜, modesty, expressed by deflecting praise and playing down one's own work or gifts. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Kigo** — 季語, a season word in a haiku that places the poem in a particular time of year. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

**Kimarite** — 決まり手, the officially recognized sumo winning techniques, of which there are 82. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Kinkyū jishin sokuhō** — 緊急地震速報, the Earthquake Early Warning sent to phones, TV and radio by the Japan Meteorological Agency when strong shaking is expected. [section 8.4](../08-getting-around/04-emergencies.md)

**Kireji** — 切れ字, a "cutting word" such as や that creates a pause and a turn in a haiku. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

**Kisha** — 貴社, "your company" in written business Japanese such as cover letters and emails. [section 13.1](../13-business-japanese/01-workplace-language.md)

**Kizukai** — 気遣い, thoughtful consideration: anticipating others' needs and taking care not to impose. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Kōban** — 交番, a small neighborhood police box, the first place to go for lost property, directions and non-urgent reports. [section 8.4](../08-getting-around/04-emergencies.md)

**Kobushi** — こぶし, the quick wavering vocal ornament characteristic of enka and folk song. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Kōdo keizai seichō ki** — 高度経済成長期, the high-growth period from the mid-1950s to 1973, when Japan's economy grew about 10 percent a year. [section 11.3](../11-history/03-postwar-japan.md)

**Koma** — コマ, a single panel on a manga page. [section 12.2](../12-japanese-for-media/02-manga.md)

**Konbini** — コンビニ, a convenience store, usually open 24 hours, selling food and daily goods and offering ATMs, printing, bill payment and parcel shipping. [section 8.3](../08-getting-around/03-shopping-and-money.md)

**Kōshien** — 甲子園, the stadium near Osaka that hosts the national high school baseball tournaments each spring and summer. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Kotatsu** — こたつ, a low table with a heater underneath and a quilt over the frame, the center of winter home life. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

**Koto** — 箏, a long 13-string zither, Japan's iconic string instrument. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Kotowaza** — 諺, a traditional Japanese proverb, usually a short sentence carrying a moral, such as 継続は力なり. [section 4.3](../04-building-sentences/03-phrases-and-idioms.md)

**Kuidaore** — 食い倒れ, "eating yourself into ruin," Osaka's proud unofficial motto about its love of food. [section 8.2](../08-getting-around/02-cities-and-regions.md)

**Kūki o yomu** — 空気を読む, "reading the air": sensing what a situation requires without being told. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Kun-yomi** — 訓読み, a kanji reading that is a native Japanese word matched to the kanji's meaning, typically used when the kanji stands alone or with okurigana. [section 3.3](../03-writing-system/03-kanji.md)

**Kyōgen** — 狂言, the comic theater traditionally performed between noh plays. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Kyōtsūgo** — 共通語, "common language", the shared variety that speakers from different regions use with each other; often used interchangeably with hyōjungo. [section 6.2](../06-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md)

### L

**Language knowledge** — 言語知識 (*gengo chishiki*), the JLPT's test of vocabulary, kanji and grammar. [section 14.1](../14-the-jlpt/01-overview.md)

**Learning loop** — The cycle this book follows: study a structure, meet it in real input, use it in output, and review it with spaced retrieval. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Leech** — A flashcard or study item that you keep getting wrong and that needs separate, focused attention. [section 3.4](../03-writing-system/04-studying-kanji.md)

**Long vowel** — A vowel held for two beats instead of one, written in hiragana with an extra vowel character and in katakana with the mark ー; vowel length changes meaning. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

### M

**Ma** — 間, the meaningful gap or pause, valued in conversation, architecture and the arts as much as what fills it. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Makekoshi** — 負け越し, a losing record in a sumo tournament, which usually brings demotion. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Makuuchi** — 幕内, the top division of professional sumo, from yokozuna down to maegashira, with at most 42 wrestlers. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Man'yōgana** — 万葉仮名, kanji used purely for their sounds to write Japanese, the ancestor of hiragana and katakana. [section 7.3](../07-living-language/03-language-evolution.md)

**Masu form** — ます形, the everyday polite verb ending (食べます, 行きます), the safest form for a beginner to speak with. [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Matsuri** — 祭り, a festival, usually rooted in Shinto or Buddhist practice and held by a local shrine or temple. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Meiji Ishin** — 明治維新, the Meiji Restoration of 1868, which returned formal power to the emperor and launched Japan's rapid modernization. [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Meishi kōkan** — 名刺交換, the ritual exchange of business cards, offered and received with both hands. [section 13.1](../13-business-japanese/01-workplace-language.md)

**Meiwaku** — 迷惑, trouble or inconvenience caused to others, which people work hard to avoid. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Menmoku** — 面目, one's standing or "face" in the eyes of others, as in 面目ない (I'm ashamed). [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Menzei** — 免税, tax exemption; tax-free shopping that lets short-term visitors recover the 10% consumption tax on purchases of ¥5,000 or more per store per day. [section 8.3](../08-getting-around/03-shopping-and-money.md)

**Midashi** — 見出し, a headline, which drops particles and verbs and abbreviates names to fit the most information into the fewest characters. [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)

**Midori no madoguchi** — みどりの窓口, the "green window," JR's staffed ticket office at major stations. [section 8.1](../08-getting-around/01-trains-and-transit.md)

**Mie** — 見得, a dramatic frozen pose struck by a kabuki actor at a climax. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Mikoshi** — 神輿, a portable shrine carried through the streets on the shoulders of a team of bearers during a festival. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Min'yō** — 民謡, Japan's regional folk songs: work songs, festival songs and fishermen's chants. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Modified Hepburn** — The romanization used in this book, with macrons for long vowels (*Tōkyō*) and an apostrophe after the moraic n before a vowel or *y* (*kin'en*). [section 2.1](../02-sounds-of-japanese/01-how-japanese-sounds-work.md)

**Mono no aware** — 物の哀れ, a gentle, bittersweet awareness of the impermanence of things, classically linked to falling cherry blossoms. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Mora** — The unit of timing in Japanese, one beat of a word: a consonant plus vowel, a vowel alone, the moraic ん, the held part of a double consonant or the second half of a long vowel. Plural *morae*. [section 2.1](../02-sounds-of-japanese/01-how-japanese-sounds-work.md)

**Mora-timed** — Describes a language such as Japanese whose rhythm gives each mora roughly equal time. [section 2.1](../02-sounds-of-japanese/01-how-japanese-sounds-work.md)

**Moraic n** — ん, the only consonant that forms a mora by itself; it sounds like *m*, *n*, *ng* or a back nasal depending on the next sound. [section 2.1](../02-sounds-of-japanese/01-how-japanese-sounds-work.md)

### N

**Na-adjective** — な形容詞, an adjective that behaves like a noun, taking な before a noun and the copula as a predicate (きれいな花, きれいです). [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Nakadaka** — 中高, "middle-high"; the pattern that falls after a mora inside the word. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Nasal g** — 鼻濁音 (*bidakuon*), *g* said as *ng* in the middle of a word; now mainly heard from older speakers and broadcasters. [section 2.2](../02-sounds-of-japanese/02-vowels-and-consonants.md)

**Nemawashi** — 根回し, informally consulting everyone before a meeting so the meeting confirms a decision; from the gardening term for preparing a tree's roots. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Nendo** — 年度, the fiscal or school year in Japan, running from April 1 to March 31. [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)

**Nenkō joretsu** — 年功序列, the seniority system in which pay and position rise largely with years of service. [section 13.3](../13-business-japanese/03-meetings-and-office-culture.md)

**Ninjō** — 人情, human feeling and compassion, the classic counterweight to giri in Japanese drama. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Noh** — 能, the slow, masked classical theater of the warrior aristocracy, developed in the 14th and 15th centuries. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Nominication** — 飲みニケーション (*nominikēshon*), bonding with colleagues over after-work drinks. [section 13.3](../13-business-japanese/03-meetings-and-office-culture.md)

### O

**O-sewa ni natte orimasu** — お世話になっております, the standard greeting that opens business emails and calls with people your company already deals with. [section 13.2](../13-business-japanese/02-business-email.md)

**Obon** — お盆, the Buddhist festival in summer (mid-August in most areas) when ancestral spirits are believed to return home. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

**Odaka** — 尾高, "tail-high"; the pattern that falls after the last mora, so a following particle is low. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Okurigana** — 送り仮名, the hiragana that follow a kanji stem and carry its grammatical ending, as べる in 食べる. [section 3.3](../03-writing-system/03-kanji.md)

**Omakase** — お任せ, "I leave it to you": letting the chef choose what to serve, usually at a set price. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

**Omiyage** — お土産, small gifts, usually local sweets, brought back from a trip for coworkers, friends and family. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**On-yomi** — 音読み, a kanji reading based on the Chinese pronunciation at the time of borrowing, typically used in compounds. [section 3.3](../03-writing-system/03-kanji.md)

**Onnagata** — 女形, a male kabuki actor who specializes in female roles. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Onsha** — 御社, "your company", the respectful spoken word for the company of the person you are addressing. [section 13.1](../13-business-japanese/01-workplace-language.md)

**Oshi** — 推し, the idol, character or performer you support above all others. [section 10.4](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/04-pop-culture.md)

**Otaku** — オタク, a devoted fan of anime, manga, games or any hobby, once a negative label and now often used with pride. [section 10.4](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/04-pop-culture.md)

**Otōshi** — お通し, a small appetizer served automatically at an izakaya and charged as a cover fee. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

**Output** — Speaking and writing in the language you are learning, which shows you what you can't yet say and invites feedback. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

### P

**Particle** — A short grammar word, such as は, を or へ, that follows a word and marks its role in the sentence. [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Particle test** — Saying a word with が after it to hear whether the pitch falls on the particle, which tells odaka from heiban. [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Pitch accent** — 高低アクセント, the pattern of high and low pitch that belongs to each Japanese word and distinguishes pairs such as 箸 (chopsticks) and 橋 (bridge). [section 2.4](../02-sounds-of-japanese/04-pitch-accent-and-intonation.md)

**Plain form** — The dictionary form of a verb and its relatives (行く, 行かない, 行った), used in casual speech and inside longer sentences. [section 5.2](../05-politeness/02-casual-japanese.md)

**Polite speech** — The everyday courteous register built on です and the ます form, the default with strangers and at work; see teineigo. [section 5.1](../05-politeness/01-levels-overview.md)

### R

**Radical** — 部首 (bushu), the component under which a kanji is filed in a dictionary, which often hints at its meaning. [section 3.3](../03-writing-system/03-kanji.md)

**Register** — The variety of language a speaker chooses for a situation, from very casual speech to formal written style. [section 6.1](../06-spoken-japanese/01-spoken-vs-written.md)

**Retrieval practice** — Recalling an answer from memory before checking it, which strengthens memory more than rereading. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Role language** — 役割語 (*yakuwarigo*), stylized speech in fiction that signals a character's type, such as the professor's わし and 〜じゃ; a term coined by the linguist Kinsui Satoshi. [section 6.3](../06-spoken-japanese/03-role-language.md)

**Romaji** — ローマ字, Japanese written in the Latin alphabet. [section 2.1](../02-sounds-of-japanese/01-how-japanese-sounds-work.md)

**Rōnin** — 浪人, a masterless samurai; today also a student who has failed university entrance exams and is studying to retake them. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Ryukyuan languages** — The languages of the Ryukyu Islands, including Okinawan, a branch of the Japonic family related to Japanese but not mutually intelligible with it. [section 6.2](../06-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md)

### S

**Saijiki** — 歳時記, a dictionary of season words arranged by season, with example poems. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

**Sakoku** — 鎖国, "closed country," the later name for the Edo-period policy of strictly limiting foreign contact and trade. [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Samurai** — 侍, a member of the hereditary warrior class that governed Japan from the late 12th century until the 1870s. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Scaled score** — a JLPT score converted onto a fixed scale so that it means the same thing from one test to the next, whatever that paper's difficulty. [section 14.1](../14-the-jlpt/01-overview.md)

**Schwa** — The weak "uh" vowel of unstressed English syllables; Japanese has no such reduction. [section 2.2](../02-sounds-of-japanese/02-vowels-and-consonants.md)

**Sectional pass mark** — the minimum score required in each JLPT scoring section, in addition to the overall pass mark. [section 14.1](../14-the-jlpt/01-overview.md)

**Seireki** — 西暦, the Western (Gregorian) calendar year, as opposed to the Japanese era year. [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)

**Sengoku jidai** — 戦国時代, the Warring States period of civil war among regional lords, from the Ōnin War (1467) to the early 17th century. [section 11.1](../11-history/01-overview.md)

**Sentence mining** — Making flashcards from sentences in Japanese material you are reading or watching, so each new word comes with its own context. [section 3.4](../03-writing-system/04-studying-kanji.md)

**Sentence-final particles** — 終助詞 (shūjoshi), particles such as ね, よ and かな at the end of a sentence that express the speaker's attitude. [section 5.2](../05-politeness/02-casual-japanese.md)

**Seppuku** — 切腹, ritual suicide by cutting the abdomen, also called harakiri, practiced to preserve honor or carried out as a sentence. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)

**Set phrase** — An expression said in a fixed form at a fixed moment, such as いただきます before eating or お疲れ様です at work. [section 4.3](../04-building-sentences/03-phrases-and-idioms.md)

**Shadowing** — A listening and speaking technique in which you play a line of native audio and speak along with it at the same time, matching speed, pitch and emotion. [section 1.2](../01-how-to-learn-japanese/02-study-and-immersion.md)

**Shakuhachi** — 尺八, an end-blown bamboo flute about 54.5 cm long, once played by monks as meditation. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Shamisen** — 三味線, a three-stringed, long-necked lute played with a large plectrum, central to Edo-period music. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Shasetsu** — 社説, a newspaper editorial, among the hardest and most rewarding news texts for learners. [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)

**Shindo** — 震度, seismic intensity: the strength of shaking at a particular place, on a scale from 0 to 7 with levels 5 and 6 split into lower and upper. [section 8.4](../08-getting-around/04-emergencies.md)

**Shinkansen** — 新幹線, Japan's high-speed "bullet train" network, opened between Tokyo and Osaka in 1964. [section 8.1](../08-getting-around/01-trains-and-transit.md)

**Shitamachi** — 下町, the "low city" of old merchant and craft neighborhoods, especially on Tokyo's eastern side, such as Asakusa. [section 8.2](../08-getting-around/02-cities-and-regions.md)

**Shodō** — 書道, the Way of Writing, Japanese brush calligraphy. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Shun** — 旬, the peak season of an ingredient, when it is at its best. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

**Shūshin koyō** — 終身雇用, lifetime employment with a single company, traditionally for regular employees of large firms. [section 13.3](../13-business-japanese/03-meetings-and-office-culture.md)

**Sokuon** — 促音, the small っ or ッ, which marks a one-beat pause that doubles the following consonant, as in きって (kitte). [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Sonkeigo** — 尊敬語, respectful language: verbs and expressions that raise the person who acts, such as いらっしゃる, おっしゃる and お〜になる. [section 5.4](../05-politeness/04-honorific-japanese.md)

**Soto** — 外, "outside": people beyond one's in-group, who are addressed more politely and whose group is raised in speech. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**SOV** — Subject–object–verb word order, the basic order of a Japanese sentence, with the verb at the end. [section 4.1](../04-building-sentences/01-grammar-basics.md)

**Sumo** — 相撲, Japanese wrestling rooted in Shinto ritual and now a professional spectator sport. [section 10.6](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/06-baseball-and-sumo.md)

**Super Chat** — スパチャ (*supacha*), a paid, highlighted comment in a YouTube livestream. [section 10.5](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/05-vtubers-and-streaming.md)

### T

**Taiko** — 太鼓, the Japanese drum, and ensemble drumming performed at festivals and on stage. [section 10.2](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/02-traditional-music.md)

**Tamego** — タメ語, casual speech between equals, using plain forms without です and ます. [section 5.1](../05-politeness/01-levels-overview.md)

**Teichōgo** — 丁重語, courteous language (also called kenjōgo II): humble verbs such as 参る and おる that make your speech more formal toward the listener. [section 5.4](../05-politeness/04-honorific-japanese.md)

**Tōyō kanji** — 当用漢字, the list of 1,850 kanji for official use issued in 1946, the forerunner of today's jōyō kanji. [section 11.3](../11-history/03-postwar-japan.md)

**Tsuyu** — 梅雨, the rainy season, covering most of Japan from about June to July. [section 9.4](../09-customs-and-daily-life/04-seasons-and-nature.md)

### U

**Uchi** — 内, "inside": a person's in-group, such as family, close friends or their own company when facing outsiders. [section 9.1](../09-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)

**Uchi and soto** — 内 and 外, "inside" and "outside": the distinction between your own group and outsiders that decides whom you elevate and whom you humble. [section 5.1](../05-politeness/01-levels-overview.md)

**Umami** — 旨味, the savory "fifth taste," identified and named by the chemist Kikunae Ikeda in 1908. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

**Ushinawareta sanjūnen** — 失われた30年, "the lost 30 years," the long period of economic stagnation after the bubble burst. [section 11.3](../11-history/03-postwar-japan.md)

### V

**Vocaloid** — ボーカロイド, singing-synthesizer software whose character voices, such as Hatsune Miku, launched an online music scene. [section 10.3](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/03-j-pop.md)

**Vowel devoicing** — Whispering or near-silencing of *i* and *u* between voiceless consonants, or at the end of a phrase after one, as in です (*des*). [section 2.2](../02-sounds-of-japanese/02-vowels-and-consonants.md)

**Vtuber** — Vチューバー, a streamer who performs as an animated avatar driven by motion capture. [section 10.5](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/05-vtubers-and-streaming.md)

### W

**Wabi-sabi** — 侘び寂び, the aesthetic of beauty in imperfection, incompleteness and transience. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Wakamono kotoba** — 若者言葉, the slang of teenagers and young adults, which changes quickly and marks the speaker's generation. [section 7.2](../07-living-language/02-contemporary-japanese.md)

**Wasei kango** — 和製漢語, Sino-Japanese words coined or repurposed in Japan, especially in the Meiji period for Western ideas, such as 哲学 (philosophy). [section 7.3](../07-living-language/03-language-evolution.md)

**Washoku** — 和食, traditional Japanese cuisine, inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2013. [section 9.2](../09-customs-and-daily-life/02-food.md)

### Y

**Yatai** — 屋台, an open-air food stall, such as the evening street stalls of Fukuoka or the stalls at a festival. [section 8.2](../08-getting-around/02-cities-and-regions.md)

**Yōgisha** — 容疑者, "suspect," the title news media attach to a person's name after an arrest. [section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)

**Yojijukugo** — 四字熟語, an idiom written in exactly four kanji, such as 一期一会. [section 4.3](../04-building-sentences/03-phrases-and-idioms.md)

**Yōon** — 拗音, a single syllable formed by an i-column kana plus a small ゃ, ゅ or ょ, as in きゃ (kya). [section 3.1](../03-writing-system/01-hiragana.md)

**Yūgen** — 幽玄, the mysterious, suggested beauty that is the aesthetic ideal of noh. [section 10.1](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/01-traditional-arts.md)

**Yukata** — 浴衣, a light, unlined cotton kimono worn in summer and at inns, wrapped left side over right. [section 9.3](../09-customs-and-daily-life/03-matsuri.md)

### Z

**Zanshin** — 残心, "remaining mind," the alert awareness maintained after an action is finished, taught in kendo and kyūdō. [section 11.2](../11-history/02-samurai-and-bushido.md)
