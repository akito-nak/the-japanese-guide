# Modern & "Manual" Keigo — バイト敬語 and the Living Politeness System 🏪

> *"Walk into any convenience store and you'll hear keigo that grammar textbooks call wrong — and language purists complain about in newspaper columns. But it's everywhere, every day. Politeness is evolving in real time, and the konbini counter is the front line."*

The [Honorific Japanese](04-honorific-japanese.md) page covers 敬語 as the textbook teaches it. This page covers what actually comes out of cash-register staff's mouths: **バイト敬語 (baito keigo)**, also called **マニュアル敬語** or **コンビニ敬語** — the polite forms that spread through service-industry training manuals in the late 1990s–2000s.

Some of it is "incorrect" by traditional rules. Some of it has a defensible internal logic that linguists actually argue about. **Either way, you will hear all of it constantly — so the priority is to *recognize* it, then decide what to say yourself.**

> **Why this matters for learners:** Textbooks teach you to *produce* correct keigo. They rarely teach you to *understand* the slightly-off keigo you'll hear 20 times a day. This page fills that gap — and shows the "correct" alternative for each.

---

## The Classic "Wrong" Phrases

For each: what you'll hear, why purists object, and what they'd prefer instead.

| You'll hear | Reading | The objection | "Correct" version |
|---|---|---|---|
| お皿のほう、お下げします | …no **hō**… | のほう normally marks a direction or a contrast between options — here there's no second option, it's pure padding | お皿をお下げします |
| こちら、コーヒーになります | …ni **narimasu** | なる = "to *become*." The coffee isn't transforming — it already *is* coffee | コーヒーでございます／コーヒーです |
| 1000円からお預かりします | 1000-en **kara**… | から marks a range start. The clerk receives the whole note, not "part of" 1000 yen | 1000円お預かりします |
| 1000円ちょうどお預かりします | …chōdo… | お預かりする implies returning change. If it's *exact*, nothing is returned — so "holding" is contradictory | 1000円ちょうど、頂戴します |
| ご注文はよろしかったでしょうか | …**yoroshikatta**… | Past tense (かった) to confirm a *present* order — a tense mismatch | ご注文は以上でよろしいでしょうか |
| お会計のほう、1万円になります | — | Stacks *both* errors: contrast-less のほう **and** the なる misuse | お会計は1万円でございます |

> **The catch:** several of these aren't as clearly "wrong" as purists claim. 〜になります legitimately means "amounts to" (合計1000円になります, for a *computed* total, is fine). よろしかったでしょうか may come from Hokkaidō/Tōhoku dialect, where a past form adds politeness by softening responsibility. Language isn't always on the prescriptivists' side — which is exactly why these forms survive.

## 二重敬語 — Double Keigo

This is the *opposite* failure mode: being *too* polite by stacking two honorifics of the same type on one verb. The 文化庁 (Agency for Cultural Affairs) flags most of these as errors — though it notes a few have become conventionally accepted (お召し上がりになる is fine).

| You'll hear | Reading | Why it's "excessive" | Correct version |
|---|---|---|---|
| おっしゃられる | osshara-reru | おっしゃる is *already* the honorific of 言う; られる adds a second honorific | おっしゃる |
| ご覧になられる | go-ran ni nara-reru | ご覧になる is already honorific; られる piles on another | ご覧になる |
| 拝見させていただく | haiken sasete itadaku | 拝見 is *already* the humble form of 見る; させていただく adds more humility | 拝見します／拝見いたします |

---

## The させていただく Problem

「〜させていただく」 has exploded into an all-purpose humility softener — often where it makes no logical sense. The 文化庁's official guidance (敬語の指針, 2007) says it's appropriate basically only when **both** of these hold:

1. **許可** — you're doing the thing *with the other party's permission*, and
2. **恩恵** — you actually *benefit* from doing it.

| Overused | Reading | Why it's off | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 休業させていただきます | kyūgyō sasete… | No permission or benefit relationship — closing is your own decision | 休業いたします |
| 担当させていただきます | tantō sasete… | Usually no explicit permission; reflexive over-humility | 担当いたします |
| 確認させていただきます | kakunin sasete… | An all-purpose softener where いたします is plenty | 確認いたします |

> This one is worth watching in **business** Japanese too, not just retail — see the [Business Japanese track](../tracks/business-japanese.md). Overusing させていただく is the corporate equivalent of saying "if that makes sense" after every sentence.

## The Service Phrases You *Should* Learn

Not everything at the register is controversial. These are **completely correct**, ubiquitous, and oddly absent from many textbooks. Learn to recognize — and ideally use — them.

| Japanese | Reading | When it's said |
|---|---|---|
| いらっしゃいませ | irasshaimase | "Welcome" on entry (expects no reply) |
| かしこまりました | kashikomarimashita | "Certainly / understood" — on taking your order. More deferential than わかりました |
| 承知いたしました | shōchi itashimashita | "Understood / noted" — the business-side equivalent |
| 少々お待ちください（ませ） | shōshō o-machi kudasai | "One moment, please" (use 少々, not ちょっと) |
| お待たせいたしました | o-matase itashimashita | "Thank you for waiting" — on returning to you |
| 恐れ入りますが | osore-irimasu ga | A cushion phrase: "I'm terribly sorry, but…" before a request. Politer than すみませんが |
| 申し訳ございません | mōshiwake gozaimasen | "I am very sorry" — preferred over すみません in service |
| 〜円のお返しです | …en no o-kaeshi desu | "Here's your ¥— change" |
| 〜でございます | …de gozaimasu | The polite copula "it is…" — the *correct* alternative to 〜になります |

**And a few "fake keigo" to avoid producing yourself:**

| Avoid | Reading | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| とんでもございません | tondemo gozaimasen | 恐れ入ります |
| なるほどですね | naruhodo desu ne | おっしゃる通りです |
| お名前を頂戴できますか | …chōdai…| お名前をうかがえますか (you can't "receive" a name) |

## The Bigger Picture: Politeness Is Alive

The reason these forms exist — and the reason people argue about them — is that **politeness systems evolve like everything else in language**. 貴様 (kisama) and お前 (omae) were once *respectful*; they decayed into insults over centuries (see [Role Language](../foundations/10-role-language.md)). バイト敬語 is the same process, caught in the act: service workers reaching for *extra* politeness, the language settling into new conventions, and an older generation insisting it's wrong.

**Practical advice for learners:**
- **On the job in Japan?** Follow your workplace's manual, even if it uses バイト敬語. That's what your manager expects.
- **For your own polished speech?** Use the "correct" forms in this guide — they're never wrong.
- **Always?** Learn to *recognize* every form here, because you'll hear them daily and need to understand what's being asked.

> Discomfort with these forms keeps rising in the 文化庁's national language surveys — yet the forms keep spreading. That tension *is* a living language. You're watching Japanese evolve at the cash register.

---

*Next time a barista tells you your coffee 「になります」, you'll know exactly what's happening — grammatically *and* sociolinguistically. That's a quietly satisfying superpower.*
