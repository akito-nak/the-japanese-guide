# Practice and Further Reading

Do these with a pen and paper, not just in your head: writing the kana out and saying each word aloud is what fixes them in memory. Cover the romanization in the chapter before you start, try every item, and only then check the answers at the back of the book. If an item goes badly, reread the section and come back to it tomorrow.

## Practice

### 1.1 Hiragana

1. Read these words aloud and write them in romanization: さくら, すし, ねこ, ともだち, ひらがな.
2. Read these words with dakuten and handakuten and write them in romanization: かぜ, ごはん, でんわ, ぶどう, えんぴつ.
3. Romanize each pair and say what makes them sound different: きて and きって; さか and さっか; おと and おっと.
4. Romanize each pair and give the meaning you learned for the first pair: おばさん and おばあさん; ゆき and ゆうき.
5. Read these yōon words and write them in romanization: きょう, しゃしん, りょこう, じしょ, ぎゅうにゅう.
6. One of these words is a hospital and the other a beauty salon: びょういん and びよういん. Which is which, and how do you know?
7. Write these words in hiragana: *arigatō*, *ohayō*, *gakkō*, *sensei*, *tōkyō*.
8. Read these sentences aloud and romanize them, paying attention to the particles: わたしは がくせいです。 がっこうへ いきます。 みずを のみます。
9. How many strokes does each of these characters have: く, し, ん, あ, ふ?
10. Which character is which? Write the hiragana for *nu*, *me*, *ne*, *re*, *ru* and *ro*, then check them against the chart.

### 1.2 Katakana

11. Read these words aloud, romanize them and give their meaning: テレビ, カメラ, ホテル, パン, ケーキ.
12. What is the difference in meaning between ビル and ビール? Romanize both.
13. Decode these loanwords: アイスクリーム, コンピューター, エレベーター, ハンバーガー, スマートフォン.
14. Write these words in katakana: America, taxi, Christmas, juice, game.
15. Read these words, taking care with the look-alike characters: ツアー, シャツ, ソース, ペン, ミシン.
16. Read and decode these words with combination sounds: パーティー, ファン, ディナー, フェリー, ウィキペディア.
17. What does each word actually mean in Japanese: マンション, スマート, カンニング, バイク?
18. From which language did Japanese borrow each of these words: アルバイト, パン, アンケート, ランドセル?
19. Write your own first name in katakana, following the sound of your name rather than its spelling.

### 1.3 Kanji

20. Name the radical these kanji share and what it means: (a) 海, 泳, 洗; (b) 休, 作, 体; (c) 話, 読, 語; (d) 森, 机, 根.
21. Using only the radical, guess the general area of meaning of each kanji: 鮭, 猫, 鉄, 晴, 鶏.
22. Is the kanji read with its on-yomi or its kun-yomi in each case, and what is the reading? (a) 山 on its own, meaning mountain; (b) 山 in 富士山; (c) 水 on its own, meaning water; (d) 水 in 水曜日; (e) 人 in 三人.
23. Read these compounds in romanization and give their meaning: 入口, 出口, 電車, 大学, 新聞, 地震.
24. Write the kanji for each meaning of *kami*: paper, god, hair.
25. In each pair, which stroke comes first? (a) 十: the horizontal or the vertical? (b) 人: the stroke to the left or to the right? (c) 小: the center or the sides? (d) 国: the bottom line or the contents?
26. How many strokes do 山, 木 and 国 have?
27. In 食べる and 飲みます, which part is kanji and which part is okurigana? What does the okurigana do?
28. Read this sentence, romanize it, translate it and say which script each part is written in: 田中さんはスーパーでパンを買いました。

### 1.4 Studying Kanji

29. Match each learner to the method that suits them best: (a) wants to recognize the whole jōyō list quickly and is happy to learn readings later; (b) likes structure and wants meanings, readings and vocabulary taught together in a fixed order; (c) is intermediate, reads manga every day and wants to learn only the kanji they meet.
30. You learn 15 new items in WaniKani at 8 a.m. When do they first come back for review, and why would learning 50 new items in one morning cause trouble two days later?
31. Which Anki button should you press? (a) You recalled the reading correctly, but only with real effort. (b) You got the meaning wrong. (c) The answer came instantly. (d) You recalled it normally, without much effort.
32. Turn this sentence from your reading into a sentence card for the word 新聞: 毎朝新聞を読みます。 Write the front and the back.

## Further Reading

- [Jōyō Kanji Table, Agency for Cultural Affairs](https://www.bunka.go.jp/kokugo_nihongo/sisaku/joho/joho/kijun/naikaku/kanji/) — the official list of the 2,136 kanji for everyday use, with their approved readings (in Japanese).
- [Jisho](https://jisho.org) — a free online dictionary where you can look up words, search kanji by radical and see stroke order diagrams.
- [Kanji alive](https://kanjialive.com) — free stroke order animations, readings and example words for the commonly taught kanji.
- [Tofugu: Learn Hiragana](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-hiragana/) and [Learn Katakana](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-katakana/) — free, illustrated mnemonics and practice for both kana scripts.
- [NHK News Web Easy](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/news/easy/) — real news rewritten in simple Japanese with furigana on every kanji, ideal reading practice once you know the kana.
- *Remembering the Kanji*, by James W. Heisig — the classic book for learning the meanings of the jōyō kanji through stories built from their components.
- [Anki manual: Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) — the official explanation of every Anki scheduling setting, including FSRS.

With the three scripts in hand, you can read Japanese; the next step is to understand it. [Chapter 2](../02-building-sentences/README.md) puts the kana to work, showing how particles, verbs and a core vocabulary combine into sentences you can say and write yourself.
