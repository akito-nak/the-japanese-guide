# Contemporary and Emerging Japanese — The Language That Won't Sit Still

Every language changes, but Japanese does it in public. Buzzwords get a national award every December, everyday words quietly flip their meaning within a generation, and a slang term can go from cool to cringe in 18 months. A learner who memorized the "hot new slang" from a 2019 list could be speaking museum pieces today.

Most of this book is stable: hiragana is not going to change. This section is different. It is deliberately dated, and everything in it describes Japanese as of 2026. Some of it will feel old by 2028, and that is the point: the speed of the turnover is itself something to learn. For the older, settled internet vocabulary (w, 草, 2channel terms, LINE culture), see [section 5.1](01-internet-and-slang.md).

## The Yearly Anchor: The Buzzword Award

Every December, Japan announces its **Buzzword of the Year**, the 新語・流行語大賞 (*shingo ryūkōgo taishō*, New Words and Buzzwords Award). It has run since 1984 and is organized by 自由国民社 (*Jiyū Kokuminsha*), the publisher of the annual dictionary of current terms 『現代用語の基礎知識』. A committee picks 30 nominees in November, then a top ten and one or more grand prize winners. It is the best single way to hear what a year in Japan sounded like, and learning each year's list makes a good December habit.

> **Note:** The award leans toward news, politics, sport and television, so many winners are not words anyone says in conversation. The 2024 grand prize, ふてほど, drew wide criticism for that reason, and the drama's own lead actor said at the ceremony that he had never once said it. Treat the list as a cultural snapshot, not a usage guide.

Here are the recent grand prizes.

| Year | Grand prize | Reading | What it was |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 村神様 | Murakami-sama | "Lord Murakami", for Yakult slugger Munetaka Murakami's 56 home runs, a record for a Japanese-born player |
| 2023 | アレ (A.R.E.) | are | "That thing": Hanshin Tigers manager Akinobu Okada's superstitious way of avoiding the word "championship" (they won it) |
| 2024 | ふてほど | futehodo | Short for the TBS comedy drama 『不適切にもほどがある！』 (*Extremely Inappropriate!*) |
| 2025 | 働いて働いて働いて働いて働いてまいります / 女性首相 | hataraite… mairimasu / josei shushō | Sanae Takaichi's "I will work and work and work and work and work", said on becoming party leader, paired with "female prime minister", as Japan's first |

The top-ten lists are often more useful as vocabulary than the grand prizes:

| Term | Reading | Year | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 知らんけど | shiran kedo | 2022 | "…but what do I know": a Kansai hedge tacked onto confident statements, now nationwide |
| リスキリング | risukiringu | 2022 | reskilling, learning new skills for work |
| 生成AI | seisei ēai | 2023 | generative AI |
| 闇バイト | yami baito | 2023 | "dark part-time job": criminal gigs recruited through social media |
| 蛙化現象 | kaeruka genshō | 2023 | suddenly going off a crush because of a trivial flaw |
| 界隈 | kaiwai | 2024 | a scene or crowd of people into something (see the next table) |
| オールドメディア | ōrudo media | 2025 | "old media": TV and newspapers, often said disparagingly |
| 二季 | niki | 2025 | "two seasons": the wry complaint that Japan now has only summer and winter |

A separate award, 三省堂's 今年の新語 (*kotoshi no shingo*, new word of the year), is chosen by the editors of the Sanseido dictionaries and favors words likely to last. Its 2022 winner was タイパ (time performance).

## Youth Slang That Is Alive as of 2026

**Wakamono kotoba** (若者言葉, young people's language) is the slang of teenagers and people in their twenties. These words are in active use as of 2026. Their register ranges from near-mainstream to friends-only, as noted.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| それな | sore na | "exactly", "so true" | So common it is almost neutral among under-40s |
| あーね | āne | "ahh, I see" (from あー、なるほどね) | A soft, casual reply |
| 過去一 | kako ichi | "the most ever" | 過去一おいしい, the best I've ever eaten |
| エグい | egui | insane, extreme (good or bad) | Very common among young speakers |
| しんどい | shindoi | so good it hurts | Fan sense: 尊すぎてしんどい. Not a complaint here |
| フッ軽 | fukkaru | always up for going out, quick to act | From フットワークが軽い; a compliment |
| チル / チルい | chiru / chirui | chill, mellow | From English "chill" |
| ガチ恋 | gachikoi | real romantic feelings for an idol or streamer | See [section 8.5](../08-arts-entertainment-and-sport/05-vtubers-and-streaming.md) |
| 限界オタク | genkai otaku | a fan so overwhelmed they can't form words | Affectionate, often self-applied |
| 〜すぎ / 〜すぎる | -sugi | way too… | 無理すぎ, 良すぎる: now a general intensifier |
| キャパい | kyapai | over capacity, overwhelmed | From キャパシティ |
| メロい | meroi | so attractive I melt | From メロメロ |
| ビジュ | biju | looks, visuals | From ビジュアル: ビジュいい, looking great |
| 飯テロ | meshitero | "food terrorism": tempting food photos late at night | Used across age groups |

### Two Productive Patterns

Two endings became word factories in 2024 and 2025.

〜界隈 (*kaiwai*), "the … scene, the people who are into …", originally meant "neighborhood" or "vicinity". Now it attaches to any hobby, fandom or habit. The star example is 風呂キャンセル界隈 (*furo kyanseru kaiwai*), the "cancel-my-bath crowd" of people too drained to take a bath tonight, which spread on TikTok and topped at least one 2024 youth trend ranking. It is self-deprecating humor about exhaustion.

〜構文 (*kōbun*), "… syntax", labels a recognizable, often meme-worthy way of talking. ヒス構文 (*hisu kōbun*) is the guilt-tripping, over-the-top argument style ("Fine, so I guess I'm the worst mother in the world, then?"). ちいかわ構文 imitates the cute, wordless characters of the hit manga *Chiikawa*: ワァ…, 〜ってコト！？ and ヤーッ！！ are used ironically and affectionately at once.

## Fading or Cringe: Recognize, Don't Use

This is the part most slang lists leave out. These words were real, but using them in earnest as of 2026 can mark you as out of touch: 痛い (*itai*, painful to watch), in the slang sense. Understand them; don't deploy them.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Status as of 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ぴえん超えてぱおん | pien koete paon | "beyond whimpering, it's trumpeting" (extreme sadness) | A 2020 fad; now the highest cringe risk on this page |
| ぴえん | pien | a cute whimper | Earnest use now sounds childish |
| り / りょ | ri / ryo | "got it" (from 了解) | Widely seen as dated |
| ぐうかわ | gūkawa | insanely cute | Early 2010s; sounds old |
| とりま | torima | anyway, for now | 2000s gyaru origin; dated |
| メンブレ | menbure | mental breakdown | Peaked in the mid-2010s |
| 推ししか勝たん | oshi shika katan | "only my fave wins" | Peaked around 2020 to 2022 |
| 〜み (わかりみ, つらみ) | -mi | cutesy nouns: "relatable-ness", "pain-ness" | Mid-2010s; many people find it grating |
| 蛙化現象 | kaeruka genshō | going off a crush | A 2023 buzzword already called stale in 2024 |
| インスタ映え | insuta-bae | Instagrammable | The 2017 grand prize; the short form 映え survives, the full phrase sounds dated |

> **Tip:** When in doubt, speak plainly. Slang signals which generation and which year you belong to, and native speakers read those signals instantly. Neutral Japanese never goes out of style.

## Words That Flipped Meaning

Nothing shows a living language better than words whose meaning has changed. Some shifts are recent; some are a century old. Both kinds cause real misunderstandings between generations. Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs (文化庁, *Bunkachō*) tracks many of them in its annual survey of Japanese usage, the 国語に関する世論調査.

| Word | Reading | Older meaning | Common meaning today | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 全然 + positive | zenzen | "(not) at all", with a negative | 全然大丈夫, totally fine | Not originally an error: Meiji-era writers, Natsume Sōseki among them, used it with positives; the "negatives only" rule hardened after the war |
| 普通に | futsū ni | in the usual way | genuinely: 普通においしい, really good | A generational trap: young speakers mean real praise, older listeners may hear "merely average" |
| ヤバい | yabai | dangerous, bad | an all-purpose intensifier, good or bad | Mainstream at all ages |
| 微妙 | bimyō | subtle, delicate | meh, iffy | Both senses coexist; the "meh" sense is a soft way to avoid a blunt no |
| 適当 | tekitō | appropriate | sloppy, half-hearted | Both alive: 適当な答え can be "a suitable answer" or "a careless answer" |
| ガチ | gachi | a real sumo bout | serious, for real | Weightier than マジ |
| 確信犯 | kakushinhan | a crime of political or moral conviction | doing wrong knowingly | In the agency's 2002 survey, 57.6% used the new sense and 16.4% the original |
| 役不足 | yakubusoku | the role is too small for the person | (misused) the person is not up to the role | Roughly half of respondents reverse it in agency surveys; risky in business, where it can insult |
| 鳥肌が立つ | torihada ga tatsu | goosebumps from cold or fear | also goosebumps from being moved | The new sense is now widely accepted |

## Loanwords That Became Verbs

Japanese turns borrowed nouns into verbs by adding 〜る and conjugating them as godan verbs: ググる, ググった, ググって.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Status as of 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ググる | guguru | to google | Core vocabulary |
| サボる | saboru | to skip, slack off (from French *sabotage*, Taishō era) | Fully standard |
| メモる / ミスる / パニクる | memoru / misuru / panikuru | to jot down / to mess up / to panic | Everyday |
| バズる | bazuru | to go viral | Mainstream |
| ディスる | disuru | to diss | Current |
| ポチる | pochiru | to click "buy" online | Current |
| バグる | baguru | to glitch, go haywire | Current |
| タピる | tapiru | to drink bubble tea | A 2019 fad and buzzword; faded |
| タクる | takuru | to take a taxi | 1990s; dead |

Japanese also builds its own English-looking words and clips long loanwords. **Wasei eigo** (和製英語, Japan-made English) means English-based words coined in Japan, such as サラリーマン (salaried office worker).

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Status as of 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| サブスク | sabusuku | subscription | Mainstream |
| コスパ / タイパ | kosupa / taipa | value for money / value for time | Entrenched |
| ステマ | sutema | stealth marketing | Current; undisclosed advertising became illegal in Japan in October 2023 |
| 映え | bae | photogenic | Current |
| 〜活 | -katsu | "doing …": 就活 (job hunting), 推し活, ソロ活 (doing things solo) | Highly productive |
| 親ガチャ | oya gacha | "parent lottery": the luck of the family you're born into | Current; a 2021 top-ten word |
| リモート / テレワーク | rimōto / terewāku | remote work | リモート sounds everyday; テレワーク sounds official |

## How to Stay Current

A printed list ages; a habit doesn't. These keep your contemporary Japanese fresh:

- Each December, read the buzzword award's top ten on the 自由国民社 site.
- Check 三省堂's new-word-of-the-year list for words with staying power.
- Watch how young Japanese people actually talk on short-form video, streams and replies on X, where slang appears first.
- Look up new terms in an online dictionary such as Weblio, which collects current slang that print dictionaries don't yet include.
- When unsure, don't force it. Slang is seasoning, not the meal.

## Summary

- Japanese slang and buzzwords change quickly, so this section describes usage as of 2026, and some of it will date within a few years.
- The 新語・流行語大賞 is announced every December; its 2025 grand prize went to Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's 働いて働いて… line together with 女性首相.
- Productive patterns such as 〜界隈 ("the … crowd") and 〜構文 ("… syntax") create new slang constantly.
- Words such as ぴえん, り and インスタ映え were once fashionable and now sound dated, so recognize them without using them.
- Everyday words shift meaning across generations: 普通に can be praise or faint praise, and 役不足 is often used in reverse.
- Japanese turns loanwords into godan verbs with 〜る, as in ググる and バズる.
