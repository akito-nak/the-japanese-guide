# N4: Building Confidence

Somewhere on the road to N4, something changes. You stop translating every word in your head. A friend's message makes sense on the first read. A character in a slice-of-life anime says 「行けばよかった」 ("I should have gone"), and you feel the regret before you have worked out the grammar. You are no longer only studying Japanese; you are using it.

N4 is the JLPT's second rung, officially "the ability to understand basic Japanese": reading passages on familiar daily topics written in basic vocabulary and kanji, and following everyday conversations spoken slowly. It is a real step up from N5, mainly because the grammar starts to express conditions, intentions and favors rather than only facts.

## What N4 Covers

| Area | Target (est.) |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | ~1,500 words |
| Kanji | ~300 characters |
| Grammar | ~150 patterns in total |
| Reading | Short notices, messages and passages on everyday topics |
| Listening | Everyday conversations at a slightly slow pace |

As at every level, these are community estimates rather than official lists.

## New Grammar at N4

### Conditionals

Japanese has four common ways to say "if", and N4 expects you to tell them apart.

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜たら | If, when (once ~ has happened) | 着い**たら**連絡してね。 |
| 〜ば | If (hypothetical condition) | 行け**ば**よかった。 |
| 〜と | Whenever, if (natural consequence) | 右に曲がる**と**駅があります。 |
| 〜なら | If (given that, as for) | 行く**なら**早く言って。 |

### Extending the て-Form

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜てから | After doing ~ | 食べ**てから**行く。 |
| 〜ても | Even if ~ | 雨が降っ**ても**行く。 |
| 〜てみる | Try doing ~ | 食べ**てみる**。 |
| 〜ておく | Do in advance | 予約し**ておく**。 |
| 〜てしまう | End up doing, finish completely (often with regret) | 食べ**てしまった**。 |
| 〜てあげる / くれる / もらう | Give or receive a favor | 教え**てあげる**。 |

### Verb Patterns

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜ことができる | Can, be able to | 泳ぐ**ことができます**。 |
| 〜たことがある | Have done before | 日本に行っ**たことがある**。 |
| 〜ようになる | Come to (a change over time) | 話せる**ようになった**。 |
| 〜ようにする | Make a point of | 早く寝る**ようにしている**。 |
| 〜なければならない | Must, have to | 宿題をし**なければならない**。 |
| 〜はずだ | Should be, is expected to | 来る**はずです**。 |
| 〜つもりだ | Intend to | 行く**つもりです**。 |
| 〜そうだ | Looks like, seems about to | 雨が降り**そうだ**。 |

### Connecting Ideas

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Plain verb + 前に | Before doing | 食べる**前に**手を洗う。 |
| た-form + 後で | After doing | 食べた**後で**、歯を磨く。 |
| 〜ので | Because (softer, more polite reason) | 忙しい**ので**行けません。 |
| 〜のに | Even though, despite | 頑張った**のに**失敗した。 |
| 〜だけ | Only, just | これ**だけ**でいい。 |
| 〜しか〜ない | Nothing but, only | 100円**しかない**。 |

## N4 Vocabulary: Key Words Beyond N5

**Time and frequency:** いつも (always), たいてい (usually), よく (often), ときどき (sometimes), あまり〜ない (not often), 全然〜ない (not at all), もう (already), まだ (still, not yet), もうすぐ (soon), すぐ (immediately).

**Feelings and states:** うれしい (happy), 悲しい (sad), 怖い (scary), 恥ずかしい (embarrassed), びっくりする (be surprised), 心配する (worry), 疲れる (get tired), 元気 (well, energetic), 大丈夫 (all right), 残念 (a pity, disappointing).

**Actions:** 説明する (explain), 紹介する (introduce), 準備する (prepare), 決める (decide), 続ける (continue), 終わる / 終える (end), 始まる / 始める (begin), 選ぶ (choose), 渡す (hand over), 送る (send), 調べる (look up), 比べる (compare), 変える (change something).

**Adjectives:** 親切 (kind), 丁寧 (polite, careful), 便利 (convenient), 不便 (inconvenient), 大切 / 大事 (important), 有名 (famous), 必要 (necessary), 特別 (special), 簡単 (simple, easy), 正しい (correct).

**Abstract nouns:** 理由 (reason), 目的 (purpose), 方法 (method), 機会 (opportunity), 経験 (experience), 関係 (relationship, connection), 意味 (meaning), 例 (example), 気持ち (feeling), 様子 (appearance, state).

Notice the pairs 始まる / 始める and 終わる / 終える: one verb says something happens by itself, the other says someone does it. Learning such pairs together roughly halves the work.

## Reading at N4

N4 reading moves from single sentences to short passages: notices, simple letters and emails, instructions and simple news written for learners, such as NHK News Web Easy ([section 12.3](../12-japanese-for-media/03-news.md)). The core skills are finding the main topic, picking out specific details such as times and numbers, and answering simple comprehension questions about a passage of a few hundred characters.

Here is a passage at about N4 level:

> 田中さんは週に三回、スポーツジムに行っています。最近、太ってしまったので、ダイエットを始めることにしました。毎朝、ジョギングもしています。最初は大変でしたが、今は楽しくなりました。来年、マラソン大会に出るつもりです。\
> Mr. Tanaka goes to the gym three times a week. He has put on weight recently, so he decided to start a diet. He also jogs every morning. It was hard at first, but now he enjoys it. Next year he intends to run in a marathon.

The questions check details and reasons: how often he goes to the gym (週に三回, three times a week), why he started a diet (太ってしまったから, because he put on weight) and what his goal for next year is (マラソン大会に出ること, running in a marathon). Notice how much N4 grammar the passage uses: 〜てしまった, 〜ので, 〜ことにする, 〜くなる and 〜つもり.

## Listening at N4

N4 listening uses everyday conversations between two people, simple instructions and directions, and short announcements, at a pace a little slower than natural speech. The key skills are understanding the topic, catching numbers, times and quantities, and following a simple sequence of events: who will do what, and when.

> **Tip:** In many listening questions, the speakers change their minds mid-conversation ("Let's meet at 3. Oh wait, make it 4"). Write down every number you hear and listen for the final decision.

## After N4

N4 is often described as solid survival Japanese. You can hold simple conversations on familiar topics, read many menus and signs, follow the main plot of a simple anime, write short messages and emails, and get around Japan on your own, even if complicated situations still stretch you. The next level asks for much more vocabulary and much more natural input, so this is the moment to make daily reading and listening a habit.

## Summary

- N4 tests "basic Japanese": about 1,500 words and 300 kanji (community estimates), and conversations spoken a little slowly.
- N4 grammar adds the four conditionals たら, ば, と and なら, which differ in meaning and are not interchangeable.
- The て-form extends into patterns such as 〜てみる (try), 〜ておく (do in advance) and 〜てしまう (end up doing).
- Transitive and intransitive verb pairs such as 始まる / 始める are best learned together.
- N4 reading passages run to a few hundred characters, and the questions check main ideas, details and reasons.
