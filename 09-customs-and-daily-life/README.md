# Chapter 9 — Customs and Daily Life

Learners often say that Japanese suddenly "clicked" not when they mastered a grammar point, but when they understood why a friend refused help three times, why a colleague said "I'll consider it" when she meant no, or why the whole office lit up when someone brought back a box of sweets from a weekend away. Language and custom grow from the same roots, and in Japanese the roots run deep.

This chapter is about the texture of everyday life: the unspoken rules of social behavior, the food and the words that go with it, the festivals that fill the calendar and the seasons that shape everything from letters to lunch. None of it is a list of rules to memorize in fear of offending. Japanese people are generous with visitors who make an honest effort, and knowing the reasons behind a custom makes it easy to follow and much more interesting.

The culture here also explains the language you have already learned. Keigo, indirect refusals, dropped subjects and the softening particles at the ends of sentences all make more sense once you can see the social map they were built for.

## In This Chapter

- Explain uchi and soto, reading the air, tatemae and honne, and how they shape what people say
- Follow the everyday etiquette of bowing, shoes, gifts, onsen and the dining table
- Read a Japanese menu by its cooking methods and flavors, and order, ask about ingredients and pay in a restaurant or izakaya
- Name the major festivals of the year, what happens at each and what to say there
- Use the vocabulary of the four seasons, the weather and the landscape
- Recognize seasonal greetings and kigo, and read a haiku by its 5-7-5 rhythm

## Before You Start

You will get the most from this chapter if you know the politeness levels and the basics of keigo from [chapter 5](../05-politeness/README.md), since many customs here are the social side of that system. [Chapter 8](../08-getting-around/README.md) introduced the cities, shops and konbini that this chapter returns to from a cultural angle.
