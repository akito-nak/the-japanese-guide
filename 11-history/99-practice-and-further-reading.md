# Practice and Further Reading

The practice below moves from reading era names and historical terms to converting dates and answering short comprehension questions. Try each item before checking the answers at the back of the book; for the comprehension questions, a sentence or two in your own words is enough.

## Practice

### 11.1 Japanese History in Brief

1. Read these era names aloud and give their romanization: 明治, 大正, 昭和, 平成, 令和.
2. Give the reading and meaning of each historical term: 幕府, 大名, 鎖国, 明治維新, 戦国時代.
3. Convert to Western years: 令和元年, 平成31年, 昭和64年, 令和8年.
4. Convert to era years: 1964 (the Tokyo Olympics), 1995, 2020.
5. Which period is each of these associated with: The Tale of Genji, the tea ceremony and Noh, ukiyo-e and kabuki, the first shogunate, the first constitution?
6. Why do the Sengoku and Edo periods overlap in the timeline?

### 11.2 Samurai and Bushido

7. Give the reading and meaning: 侍, 武士道, 切腹, 浪人, 義理, 人情.
8. Which samurai-era expression fits each situation? (a) You help a rival company that has run out of a key part. (b) A coworker hands out chocolate on Valentine's Day to everyone in the office, out of duty. (c) A kendo teacher reminds you to stay alert after your strike.
9. Translate into English: 覚悟しろ！ and 面目ない。
10. Where does the familiar list of seven bushidō virtues come from, and why do historians treat it with caution?
11. Put these Meiji-era events in order: the Satsuma Rebellion, the Sword Abolition Edict (廃刀令), the abolition of the domains (廃藩置県), the conscription law.

### 11.3 Postwar Japan

12. Give the reading and meaning: 戦後, 終戦, 復興, 少子化, 引きこもり.
13. Match each term to its decade: 三種の神器 (white goods), the 3C, バブル経済, 就職氷河期.
14. A government white paper of 1956 declared 「もはや戦後ではない」. Translate it and explain why it was significant.
15. What does Article 9 of the constitution renounce, and what tension has it created?
16. Name two reforms of 1946 to written Japanese and say how each affects what you read today.
17. A Japanese friend says, 「3.11のとき、私は東京にいました」. What event is she talking about, and how do you read 3.11 aloud?

## Further Reading

- [Birth of the Constitution of Japan (National Diet Library)](https://www.ndl.go.jp/constitution/e/) — the original drafts, memos and debates behind the 1947 constitution, with English explanations.
- [Modern Japan in Archives (National Diet Library)](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/) — primary documents on political history from the opening of the country to the postwar years, arranged by period.
- [Bushido: The Soul of Japan, by Nitobe Inazō (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/12096) — the 1900 book that created the modern image of bushidō; short, readable and worth knowing as a source rather than a description.
- [Embracing Defeat, by John W. Dower (W. W. Norton)](https://wwnorton.com/books/Embracing-Defeat/) — a Pulitzer Prize-winning history of the occupation years, rich in the words and voices of ordinary Japanese people.
- [The Imperial Household Agency](https://www.kunaicho.go.jp/eindex.html) — the official English site on the imperial family, with background on the emperors whose reigns name the modern eras.
- [JNTO Japan Tourism Statistics](https://statistics.jnto.go.jp/en/) — official visitor figures, useful for keeping the visitor numbers in [section 11.3](03-postwar-japan.md) up to date.

History explains why Japanese looks the way it does; the next step is to use it on the media you care about.
