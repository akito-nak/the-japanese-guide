# Chapter 11 — History

Why does a Japanese form ask for your birth year as 平成10年? Why does a kendo teacher talk about 残心, and why does every adult in Japan know what "3.11" means? Sooner or later every learner runs into a word, a date or a phrase that only makes sense with some history behind it. Japanese is a language with a long memory.

This chapter gives you that memory in compact form. It moves from the earliest pottery-makers through the Heian court that invented kana, the seven centuries of warrior rule, the Tokugawa peace and the headlong modernization of the Meiji era, and on to the war, the occupation, the economic miracle and the lost decades that shaped the Japan you will visit today. Along the way you will learn to read the names of eras on museum captions and government forms, and you will meet the samurai: who they were, how their legend was built and which of their words are still in everyday use.

None of this is history for its own sake. Each period left something in the language, from kanji and kana to Portuguese bread and English loanwords, and knowing where a word came from is one of the most reliable ways to remember it.

## In This Chapter

- Name the major periods of Japanese history in order, read their kanji and say what each one is known for
- Convert between era years (和暦) and Western years in both directions, including the special year 元年
- Explain who the samurai were, where the idea of bushidō comes from and how much of the legend is modern
- Use samurai-era words that survive in modern Japanese, such as 義理, 覚悟 and 敵に塩を送る
- Describe the occupation, the economic miracle, the bubble and the lost decades, and the vocabulary Japanese uses for each
- Recognize the postwar reforms that created the kanji and kana spelling you read today

## Before You Start

You should be able to read hiragana and katakana and recognize common kanji ([chapter 3](../03-writing-system/README.md)). The history is told in English, with Japanese terms given in kanji and romanization, so no particular grammar level is needed. Knowing how Japanese changed over time ([section 7.3](../07-living-language/03-language-evolution.md)) and how traditional arts developed ([chapter 10](../10-arts-entertainment-and-sport/README.md)) will add depth but isn't required.
