# Seasons and Nature — Living by the Calendar

In late August the konbini shelves still sag with cold noodles and ice cream, but a week into September the first chestnut desserts and "moon-viewing" burgers appear, and by October everything seems to be flavored with sweet potato. Formal letters open with a comment on the weather of that particular month, restaurants change their menus and their dishes with the season, and even the anime you watch is scheduled by it, in spring, summer, autumn and winter "cours". Japan lives by its calendar with an intensity that surprises most visitors.

The four seasons (四季, *shiki*) run through the language too. The word 旬 (*shun*), the peak moment of a food, met in [section 7.2](02-food.md), is only the beginning: there are words for the first strong wind of spring, for the sound of a thousand cicadas and for the moment you put away your summer clothes. Learn them and you will notice the seasons the way your Japanese friends do.

## Spring (春, Haru)

*Months:* March, April and May

Spring is the season of beginnings. The school year and most companies' fiscal years start in April, so the cherry blossoms coincide with entrance ceremonies, new jobs and new commuters in stiff new suits.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 桜 | sakura | Cherry blossom |
| 花見 | hanami | Cherry-blossom viewing |
| 春一番 | haru ichiban | The first strong south wind of spring |
| 入学式 | nyūgakushiki | School entrance ceremony |
| 卒業式 | sotsugyōshiki | Graduation ceremony (in March) |
| 新年度 | shinnendo | The new school or fiscal year (from April) |
| 霞 | kasumi | Spring haze |
| うぐいす | uguisu | Bush warbler, whose song announces spring |
| たんぽぽ | tanpopo | Dandelion |
| 菜の花 | nanohana | Rapeseed blossom (fields of bright yellow) |
| つくし | tsukushi | Field horsetail shoots, a spring delicacy |

Two words capture the season's mood. 春めく (*harumeku*) means "to become spring-like", the moment the air softens. 花吹雪 (*hanafubuki*, "flower blizzard") is the swirl of cherry petals in the wind at the end of the blossom season, and it is one of the loveliest words in the language.

> だんだん春めいてきましたね。\
> *Dandan harumeite kimashita ne.*\
> It's starting to feel like spring, isn't it?

## Summer (夏, Natsu)

*Months:* June, July and August

Summer arrives with rain. **Tsuyu** (梅雨, also read *baiu*) is the rainy season, which covers most of Japan from early or mid-June to mid or late July; Okinawa's comes in May and June, and Hokkaido largely escapes it. Then comes the heat: July and August in Tokyo and Osaka are hot and very humid, and heatstroke warnings are a daily feature of the news.

|---|---|---|
| 梅雨 | tsuyu / baiu | Rainy season |
| 梅雨明け | tsuyu ake | The end of the rainy season |
| 蒸し暑い | mushiatsui | Hot and humid |
| 猛暑日 | mōshobi | A day of 35 °C or more |
| 熱中症 | necchūshō | Heatstroke |
| 打ち水 | uchimizu | Sprinkling water on the street to cool it |
| 夏祭り | natsu matsuri | Summer festival |
| 花火大会 | hanabi taikai | Fireworks display |
| 蝉 | semi | Cicada |
| 蛍 | hotaru | Firefly |
| 西瓜 | suika | Watermelon |
| かき氷 | kakigōri | Shaved ice |
| 浴衣 | yukata | Light summer kimono |
| 向日葵 | himawari | Sunflower |
| 扇子 / うちわ | sensu / uchiwa | Folding fan / flat round fan |
| 台風 | taifū | Typhoon |

The Japan Meteorological Agency announces the probable end of the rainy season for each region, and 梅雨明け is eagerly awaited, since it means blue skies and real summer. The sound of that summer is the cicada (蝉). The roar of countless cicadas in the trees has its own poetic name, 蝉時雨 (*semishigure*, "cicada shower"), as if the noise were falling like rain.

> 毎日蒸し暑いですね。\
> *Mainichi mushiatsui desu ne.*\
> It's hot and humid every day, isn't it?

## Autumn (秋, Aki)

*Months:* September, October and November

The heat breaks, the skies clear and the food is at its best. 紅葉 is read *kōyō* when it means the turning of the leaves and *momiji* when it means the maple trees themselves, and from October the colors move down the country from Hokkaido, reaching Kyoto and Tokyo in late November.

|---|---|---|
| 紅葉 | kōyō / momiji | Autumn leaves / maple |
| 銀杏 | ichō | Ginkgo (turns bright yellow) |
| 秋晴れ | akibare | Clear autumn weather |
| 秋風 | akikaze | Autumn breeze |
| 虫の音 | mushi no ne | The chirping of insects at night |
| 実り | minori | Harvest, ripening |
| 松茸 | matsutake | Matsutake mushroom (aromatic and expensive) |
| 栗 | kuri | Chestnut |
| 柿 | kaki | Persimmon |
| 梨 | nashi | Japanese pear |
| さつまいも | satsumaimo | Sweet potato |
| 秋刀魚 | sanma | Pacific saury, grilled whole |
| 月見 / お月見 | tsukimi / otsukimi | Moon viewing |
| 中秋の名月 | chūshū no meigetsu | The harvest moon |

### The Many Autumns

Autumn is so pleasant that it has been claimed for everything. 食欲の秋 (*shokuyoku no aki*) is "autumn, the season of appetite", and it has siblings: 読書の秋 (autumn for reading), 芸術の秋 (autumn for the arts) and スポーツの秋 (autumn for sports). Advertisers invent new ones every year, so "〜の秋" is a pattern worth knowing.

### Tsukimi: Moon Viewing

On the 15th night of the eighth month of the old lunar calendar, which falls between mid-September and early October, people admire the harvest moon (中秋の名月). The custom is to set out rice dumplings (月見団子, *tsukimi dango*) and pampas grass (すすき, *susuki*). Its modern heir is the 月見バーガー (*tsukimi bāgā*): fast-food chains add a fried egg, standing in for the moon, to their burgers every autumn.

## Winter (冬, Fuyu)

*Months:* December, January and February

Winter brings heavy snow to the Sea of Japan coast and Hokkaido, and crisp, dry, sunny days to the Pacific side, including Tokyo. It also brings the year's biggest holiday and its warmest food.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 雪 | yuki | Snow |
| 初雪 | hatsuyuki | The first snow of the season |
| 雪景色 | yukigeshiki | Snowy scenery |
| 霜 | shimo | Frost |
| 氷 | kōri | Ice |
| 鍋 | nabe | Hot pot |
| おでん | oden | Ingredients simmered in dashi, a winter favorite |
| 冬至 | tōji | The winter solstice |
| ゆず湯 | yuzuyu | A bath with yuzu citrus, taken on the winter solstice |
| 年末 | nenmatsu | The end of the year |
| 大晦日 | ōmisoka | New Year's Eve |
| 除夜の鐘 | joya no kane | Temple bells rung 108 times around midnight on New Year's Eve |
| お正月 | oshōgatsu | New Year |
| 初詣 | hatsumōde | The first shrine visit of the year |
| お節料理 | osechi ryōri | Traditional New Year dishes |

The 108 strikes of the 除夜の鐘 are traditionally said to match the 108 worldly desires of Buddhist teaching, one rung away for each.

### Kotatsu Culture

Many Japanese homes have little or no central heating, and the great comfort of winter is the **kotatsu** (こたつ): a low table with a heater fixed underneath, covered by a thick quilt with the tabletop laid over it. You sit on the floor with your legs under the quilt, and getting out again requires real willpower.

> こたつから出られない。\
> *Kotatsu kara derarenai.*\
> I can't get out of the kotatsu.

The household cat usually gets there first. こたつ猫 (*kotatsu neko*), the cat curled up inside or on top of the kotatsu, is a winter cliché with its own photo genre.

## Seasonal Greetings

Formal letters and business emails in Japanese traditionally open with a **jikō no aisatsu** (時候の挨拶), a seasonal greeting that comments on the weather of the month before getting to the point. Many set phrases use the pattern 〜の候 (*~ no kō*, "in the season of ~").

| Season | Greeting | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | 春暖の候 | In this warm spring season |
| Spring | 花の便りも聞こえる今日この頃 | Now that news of the blossoms is arriving |
| Summer | 盛夏の候 | In the height of summer |
| Summer | 猛暑の候 | In this fierce heat |
| Autumn | 秋冷の候 | As autumn turns cool |
| Autumn | 紅葉の便りも聞かれる頃 | Now that news of the autumn leaves is arriving |
| Winter | 師走の候 | In the busy last month of the year |
| Winter | 年の瀬も押し迫り | As the year draws to a close |

There are also seasonal greeting cards, each with its own window:

> 暑中お見舞い申し上げます。\
> *Shochū o-mimai mōshiagemasu.*\
> Wishing you well in the summer heat. (sent from the end of the rainy season until about August 7)

> 残暑お見舞い申し上げます。\
> *Zansho o-mimai mōshiagemasu.*\
> Wishing you well in the lingering heat. (sent from about August 8, the traditional start of autumn, to the end of August)

> 寒中お見舞い申し上げます。\
> *Kanchū o-mimai mōshiagemasu.*\
> Wishing you well in the cold season. (sent from about January 8 until early February)

> **Tip:** You don't need to write formal letters to use this. Opening a message or a conversation with a remark about the weather, such as 寒くなりましたね (*it's gotten cold, hasn't it*), is the everyday version, and it instantly sounds natural.

## Kigo: Season Words

A **haiku** (俳句) is a short poem of three phrases in a 5-7-5 pattern of sound units (mora, or 音 *on*), and it traditionally contains a **kigo** (季語), a season word that places the poem in a particular time of year. Poets look kigo up in a **saijiki** (歳時記), a dictionary of season words arranged by season with example poems. Even if you never write a haiku, kigo are a map of what Japanese culture associates with each time of year.

|---|---|---|---|
| Spring | 桜 | sakura | Cherry blossoms |

| Spring | 蛙 | kawazu | Frogs |

| Summer | 蝉 | semi | Cicadas |
| Summer | 梅雨 | tsuyu | The rainy season |
| Autumn | 紅葉 | momiji | Maple leaves |

| Autumn | 虫の音 | mushi no ne | Insects chirping |

| Winter | こたつ | kotatsu | The heated table |

## Haiku: Seventeen Sounds

The most famous haiku of all was written by Matsuo Bashō in 1686:

> 古池や蛙飛び込む水の音\
> *Furuike ya / kawazu tobikomu / mizu no oto*\
> An old pond; a frog jumps in, the sound of water.

Count the sounds and you get 5-7-5: ふ・る・い・け・や, か・わ・ず・と・び・こ・む, み・ず・の・お・と. The kigo is 蛙 (*kawazu*, frog), which marks the poem as spring. The や after 古池 is a **kireji** (切れ字, "cutting word"), a particle that creates a pause and a turn, a little like a spoken dash. The poem sets a still, ancient pond against one small splash, and leaves the silence afterward for the reader to hear.

## Summary

- Japan's four seasons (四季) shape food, clothing, greetings, festivals and even the school and business year, which starts in April with the cherry blossoms.
- Summer begins with tsuyu (梅雨), the rainy season of June and July, followed by intense humid heat; autumn is the season of appetite, reading and the arts (〜の秋).
- Winter life centers on the kotatsu, hot pots and the New Year, when temple bells ring 108 times on 大晦日.
- Formal letters open with a seasonal greeting (時候の挨拶) such as 盛夏の候, and summer and winter greeting cards are sent within set dates.
- A haiku has 5-7-5 sound units, a kigo (season word) and often a kireji such as や; Bashō's 古池や蛙飛び込む水の音 uses 蛙 as its spring kigo.
