# Practice and Further Reading

Culture is learned by doing, so most of these exercises put you in a situation and ask what you would say or do. There is often more than one good answer; compare yours with the model answers at the back of the book and notice why they work. Say the Japanese aloud, and when an item asks for a reason, answer in a full sentence as if explaining it to a friend.

## Practice

### 7.1 Culture and Norms

1. Give the reading and meaning of each word: 内, 外, 建前, 本音, 迷惑.
2. A Japanese acquaintance says 日本語がお上手ですね. Reply modestly, in Japanese.
3. You propose a plan, and your colleague answers ちょっと…検討します. What does she most likely mean?
4. You visit a friend's home. Describe what you do with your shoes at the genkan, and with the slippers you find at the toilet door.
5. You bring a box of sweets as a gift. What do you say as you hand it over, how do you hold it, and why would a set of four items be a poor choice?
6. You work at a Japanese company and phone a client. The client asks for your manager, 田中部長, who is out. Which is correct, and why: 田中部長はいらっしゃいません or 田中は席を外しております?
7. What is 根回し, where does the word come from, and why is it useful in a Japanese workplace?
8. Name four rules of onsen etiquette.

### 7.2 Food

9. Use the cooking verbs to decode these menu items: 焼き魚, 揚げ出し豆腐, 蒸し鶏, 野菜炒め.
10. Give the Japanese for "What do you recommend?" and "Without wasabi, please."
11. At an izakaya a small dish you didn't order arrives, and later appears on the bill. What is it called, and is this a mistake?
12. Match each ramen broth to the city it is most associated with: 豚骨, 味噌, 醤油. Cities: Tokyo, Sapporo, Fukuoka.
13. At a konbini, the cashier asks お弁当温めますか. Answer yes, politely.
14. You are vegetarian. Ask what the soup stock is made from, and explain why this question matters in Japan.
15. Translate into Japanese: "The bill, please" and "Separate bills, please." What do you say as you leave?

### 7.3 Matsuri

16. Give the date of each: Hina Matsuri, Kodomo no Hi, Tanabata, Setsubun.
17. What do people shout while throwing beans on Setsubun, and what does it mean?
18. Name Japan's three great festivals and the city of each.
19. You are putting on a yukata. Which side goes on top, and why does it matter?
20. It is January 2. A neighbor greets you. What do you say, and what is 初詣?
21. Write a Tanabata wish in Japanese: "May I pass N3 of the JLPT."

### 7.4 Seasons and Nature

22. Sort these words into the four seasons: 蝉, 紅葉, こたつ, うぐいす, 梅雨, 名月, 雪, 花見.
23. Make small talk about the weather: say "It's hot and humid, isn't it?" and "It's gotten cold, hasn't it?"
24. You want to send a greeting card on August 20. Which phrase do you use: 暑中お見舞い申し上げます or 残暑お見舞い申し上げます? Why?
25. What is 食欲の秋? Make one more "〜の秋" phrase and translate it.
26. Count the sound units in each line of 古池や蛙飛び込む水の音, then name its kigo, the season and its kireji.

## Further Reading

- [Washoku on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/washoku-traditional-dietary-cultures-of-the-japanese-notably-for-the-celebration-of-new-year-00869) — the official 2013 inscription, explaining what washoku is and why it was recognized.
- [Our Regional Cuisines (MAFF)](https://www.maff.go.jp/e/policies/market/k_ryouri/) — the Ministry of Agriculture's database of regional dishes from every prefecture, with history and recipes.
- [Travel Japan (JNTO)](https://www.japan.travel/en/) — the national tourism organization's guide, with festival calendars, etiquette pages and seasonal features.
- [Erin's Challenge! I can speak Japanese (Japan Foundation)](https://www.erin.jpf.go.jp/en/) — a free video course following a student's daily life in Japan, with notes on customs and manners.
- [NHK World Learn Japanese](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/learnjapanese/) — free courses from the national broadcaster, with short cultural notes alongside every dialogue.
- [The Book of Tea by Kakuzō Okakura](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/769) — a short 1906 classic, free online, on the aesthetics of tea, simplicity and imperfection behind many of the ideas in this chapter.
- [Jisho](https://jisho.org/) — a free dictionary for menu items, festival names and season words, with example sentences.

Customs and seasons give you the rhythm of daily life; the arts give it its color. [Chapter 8](../08-arts-entertainment-and-sport/README.md) turns to Japan's arts, entertainment and sport, from tea ceremony and kabuki to J-pop, anime, Vtubers, baseball and sumo.
