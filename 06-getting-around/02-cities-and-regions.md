# Cities and Regions — Japan Beyond Tokyo

Ask someone in Osaka for directions and you may get a joke, a food recommendation and the directions, in that order. Ask in Kyoto and the answer will be gentler and more roundabout; ask in Sapporo and it will sound almost like a textbook. Japan is a small country on the map, but its cities have distinct personalities, accents and dinner tables, and the more of them you see, the better you understand the whole.

Japan is made up of 47 prefectures, the **todōfuken** (都道府県): one 都 (*to*, metropolis: Tokyo), one 道 (*dō*, circuit: Hokkaido), two 府 (*fu*, urban prefectures: Osaka and Kyoto) and 43 県 (*ken*, prefectures). They spread across four main islands, Honshū, Hokkaidō, Shikoku and Kyūshū, plus Okinawa and thousands of smaller islands; a government recount in 2023 put the total at about 14,000. The populations below are rounded mid-2020s figures for each city proper.

## Tokyo (東京): The Capital

*Population:* about 14 million in Tokyo Metropolis; about 37 million in the wider metropolitan area, one of the largest in the world\
*Speech:* standard Japanese, plus casual Tokyo speech\
*Character:* intense, efficient, endlessly varied and expensive

Tokyo is less one city than a federation of neighborhoods. Its core is 23 special wards (特別区, *tokubetsuku*), each with its own city hall, surrounded by dozens of further cities and towns. The table lists the districts visitors meet first.

| Area | Character |
|---|---|
| 新宿 (Shinjuku) | Skyscrapers, Golden Gai's tiny bars, the Kabukichō entertainment district, the world's busiest station |
| 渋谷 (Shibuya) | Youth fashion, the Shibuya Crossing, music venues |
| 原宿 (Harajuku) | Street fashion on Takeshita Street, luxury on Omotesandō |
| 秋葉原 (Akihabara) | Electronics, anime goods, manga, maid cafés |
| 浅草 (Asakusa) | Old Tokyo, Sensō-ji temple, rickshaws |
| 銀座 (Ginza) | High-end shopping, galleries, cocktail bars |
| 六本木 (Roppongi) | Nightlife, an international crowd, art museums |
| 上野 (Ueno) | Museums, the zoo, the Ameyoko market, Ueno Park |
| 下北沢 (Shimokitazawa) | Vintage shops, live music, independent cafés |
| 吉祥寺 (Kichijoji) | Inokashira Park; a perennial favorite in "best place to live" rankings |

Asakusa and Ueno belong to the **shitamachi** (下町), the "low city" of old merchant and craft neighborhoods on Tokyo's eastern flatlands, as opposed to the 山の手 (*yamanote*), the hilly "high city" to the west where samurai and later the well-to-do lived. The words still color how Tokyoites describe their own neighborhoods.

Standard Japanese is based on educated Tokyo speech, but casual Tokyo speech has its own flavor. It drops particles freely and leans on じゃん (*jan*, "right?", "isn't it?"), a tag that spread to Tokyo from neighboring Kanagawa and is now heard everywhere among younger speakers.

> これ、いいじゃん。\
> *Kore, ii jan.*\
> Hey, this is nice, isn't it?

## Osaka (大阪): The Nation's Kitchen

*Population:* about 2.8 million (Keihanshin metropolitan area, with Kyoto and Kobe: about 19 million)\
*Speech:* Osaka-ben, the best-known variety of Kansai dialect\
*Character:* loud, warm, food-obsessed and funny

Osaka grew rich as a merchant city, and it still prizes a good deal, a good meal and a good laugh. Locals are proud of being more direct than Tokyo, and the city is the home of manzai comedy: the entertainment company Yoshimoto Kōgyō (吉本興業) was founded here in 1912. Its unofficial motto is **kuidaore** (食い倒れ), "eat until you drop" or, more literally, eat yourself into ruin.

| Area | Character |
|---|---|
| 道頓堀 (Dōtonbori) | Neon-lit canal of food and theaters, the Glico running-man sign |
| なんば (Namba) | Shopping, nightlife, comedy theaters, takoyaki |
| 梅田 (Umeda) | Osaka's Shinjuku: the northern transport and shopping hub |
| 心斎橋 (Shinsaibashi) | Covered shopping street, fashion |
| 天王寺 (Tennōji) | The zoo and Abeno Harukas, once Japan's tallest building |
| 新世界 (Shinsekai) | Retro entertainment district, Tsūtenkaku tower, kushikatsu |

A handful of Osaka words will win you instant affection. [Section 4.2](../04-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md) covers Kansai dialect in depth; the table gives the essentials.

| Standard | Osaka | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| とても | めっちゃ | Very |
| 本当に | ほんまに | Really |
| ありがとう | おおきに | Thank you |
| だめ | あかん | No good |
| いい | ええ | Good |
| ちがう | ちゃう | Wrong; that's not it |

> それ、めっちゃええやん。\
> *Sore, metcha ee yan.*\
> That's really great!

## Kyoto (京都): The Ancient Capital

*Population:* about 1.4 million\
*Speech:* Kyoto-ben, a soft and famously polite variety of Kansai dialect\
*Character:* refined, traditional, deeply historical and busy with visitors

Kyoto was the imperial capital for more than a thousand years, from 794 until the court moved to Tokyo in 1868. It holds an extraordinary concentration of temples and shrines (around 1,600 temples is the commonly quoted figure), and 17 historic sites in and around the city are listed together as a UNESCO World Heritage property.

| Area | Character |
|---|---|
| 嵐山 (Arashiyama) | Bamboo grove, river views, temples, kimono rental |
| 祇園 (Gion) | Geiko district, teahouses, stone-paved lanes |
| 清水寺 (Kiyomizu-dera) | Hillside temple with its famous wooden stage |
| 伏見稲荷大社 (Fushimi Inari Taisha) | Thousands of vermilion torii gates climbing a mountain |
| 金閣寺 (Kinkaku-ji) | The Golden Pavilion |
| 哲学の道 (Tetsugaku no Michi) | The Philosopher's Path, a canal-side walk lined with cherry trees |
| 錦市場 (Nishiki Ichiba) | A narrow covered food market, "Kyoto's kitchen" |

Kyoto keeps several customs that surprise visitors:

- **Ichigen-san okotowari** (一見さんお断り, "no first-timers"): some traditional teahouses in Gion accept new guests only on an existing customer's introduction. The rule protects long-term relationships and the house's ability to vouch for every guest.
- Geiko and maiko: Kyoto calls its fully trained geisha 芸妓 (*geiko*); 舞妓 (*maiko*) are apprentices. Photographing them without permission is rude, and parts of Gion now post signs banning photography on private lanes.
- Indirect speech: Kyoto speech is soft and courteous, and the city has a reputation, which Kyotoites joke about themselves, for saying things indirectly. Reading the air ([section 7.1](../07-customs-and-daily-life/01-culture-and-norms.md)) is a Kyoto specialty.

## Kobe (神戸): The Cosmopolitan Port

*Population:* about 1.5 million\
*Speech:* Kansai dialect, often described as milder than Osaka's\
*Character:* stylish, international and relaxed, between the mountains and the sea

Kobe opened to foreign trade in 1868, and the merchants who settled there gave it an international flavor that it still wears proudly.

- 南京町 (Nankinmachi): Kobe's Chinatown, one of Japan's three major Chinatowns with Yokohama and Nagasaki.
- 北野異人館 (Kitano Ijinkan): Western-style merchants' houses from the foreign-settlement era.
- 神戸ビーフ (Kōbe bīfu): Kobe beef, the strictly certified meat of Tajima cattle raised in Hyōgo prefecture.
- ルミナリエ (Luminarie): a festival of light first held in December 1995 to remember the victims of the Great Hanshin-Awaji Earthquake of January that year; since 2024 it has been held in late January and early February.

## Nagoya (名古屋): The Practical City

*Population:* about 2.3 million\
*Speech:* Nagoya-ben, known for endings such as 〜だがや and 〜みゃあ\
*Character:* industrial, practical, understated, with a fierce local pride

Nagoya is Japan's fourth-largest city and the center of the Chūkyō industrial region; Toyota's headquarters is in nearby Toyota City. Visitors often pass straight through on the Shinkansen, which means those who stop find it less crowded and more affordable. The city's real claim to fame is 名古屋めし (*Nagoya meshi*, Nagoya food), a cuisine of bold, sweet and savory flavors.

| Food | Description |
|---|---|
| 味噌カツ (miso katsu) | Pork cutlet with a rich red-miso sauce |
| 手羽先 (tebasaki) | Chicken wings glazed in sweet soy and pepper |
| 名古屋コーチン (Nagoya kōchin) | A prized local breed of chicken |
| あんかけスパ (ankake supa) | Spaghetti in a thick, peppery sauce |
| ひつまぶし (hitsumabushi) | Grilled eel over rice, eaten three ways |
| 小倉トースト (ogura tōsuto) | Toast with sweet red-bean paste and butter |

## Hiroshima (広島): Resilience and Peace

*Population:* about 1.2 million\
*Speech:* Hiroshima-ben\
*Character:* warm, resilient and surprisingly lively

On August 6, 1945, Hiroshima became the first city destroyed by an atomic bomb. It was rebuilt as a thriving modern city and has become a global voice for peace, and few visitors leave unmoved.

- 平和記念公園 (Heiwa Kinen Kōen): the Peace Memorial Park and its museum.
- 原爆ドーム (Genbaku Dōmu): the Atomic Bomb Dome, the ruined shell of a building near the hypocenter, preserved as a memorial and listed by UNESCO.
- 宮島 (Miyajima): an island a short ferry ride away, home to Itsukushima Shrine and its great torii gate, which appears to float at high tide.

Hiroshima's food is just as memorable. Its お好み焼き (*okonomiyaki*) is layered, with noodles inside, rather than mixed as in Osaka; the prefecture produces around 60% of Japan's farmed oysters (牡蠣, *kaki*); and もみじ饅頭 (*momiji manjū*), maple-leaf-shaped cakes filled with sweet bean paste, are Miyajima's favorite souvenir.

## Fukuoka (福岡): Gateway to Asia

*Population:* about 1.6 million\
*Speech:* Hakata-ben (博多弁)\
*Character:* energetic, young, warm and growing

Fukuoka is one of Japan's fastest-growing big cities and sits closer to Busan in South Korea than to Tokyo, a closeness you can taste in its food and feel in its outward-looking mood. It is really two old towns joined together: Fukuoka, the castle town west of the Naka River, and Hakata, the merchant town to the east, where the main station still carries the Hakata name.

The city is famous for its **yatai** (屋台), open-air food stalls that set up on the street in the evening, with a counter and a handful of stools; Fukuoka has the largest concentration of them in Japan, especially along the river at Nakasu.

| Food | Description |
|---|---|
| 博多ラーメン (Hakata rāmen) | Pork-bone (tonkotsu) broth with thin noodles; order a 替え玉 (*kaedama*, extra noodles) when you finish |
| 明太子 (mentaiko) | Spicy pollock roe, Fukuoka's most famous souvenir |
| もつ鍋 (motsu nabe) | Offal hot pot, rich and garlicky |
| 水炊き (mizutaki) | Chicken hot pot served with ponzu |

## Sapporo (札幌): Hokkaido's Capital

*Population:* about 2 million\
*Speech:* close to standard Japanese, with Hokkaido words such as なまら (*namara*, very)\
*Character:* cold, open, outdoorsy and dairy-loving

Hokkaido is home to the Ainu, its Indigenous people, and was settled on a large scale by Japanese from the mainland only after 1869, when the government began colonizing the island. Sapporo was laid out then on a grid, and Hokkaido still feels different from the rest of Japan: wider roads, bigger skies and real winters.

| Food | Description |
|---|---|
| 味噌ラーメン (miso rāmen) | Sapporo's gift to the ramen world |
| ジンギスカン (jingisukan) | Lamb grilled on a dome-shaped skillet, named after Genghis Khan |
| カニ (kani) | Crab, especially 毛ガニ (*kegani*, horsehair crab) and タラバガニ (*tarabagani*, king crab) |
| スープカレー (sūpu karē) | Soup curry: a thin, spiced broth with large vegetables |
| 乳製品 (nyūseihin) | Dairy: milk, butter, cheese and outstanding soft-serve |
| じゃがいも (jagaimo) | Potatoes; Hokkaido grows most of Japan's crop |

The Sapporo Snow Festival (さっぽろ雪まつり), held every February since 1950, fills the city center with huge snow and ice sculptures.

## Okinawa (沖縄): The Southern Islands

*Population:* about 1.5 million (prefecture)\
*Speech:* Okinawan Japanese; the traditional Ryukyuan languages, such as Okinawan (うちなーぐち, *Uchināguchi*), are endangered\
*Character:* relaxed, warm, subtropical and culturally distinct

Okinawa was the heart of the Ryūkyū Kingdom (琉球王国), which traded across East Asia for centuries, came under the control of the Satsuma domain in 1609 and was annexed by Japan in 1879. After the Second World War it was under US administration until 1972. Its music, food, architecture and greetings all show that separate history.

| Place | Character |
|---|---|
| 那覇 (Naha) | The capital: Shuri Castle (being rebuilt after a fire in 2019), Kokusai-dōri shopping street |
| 美ら海水族館 (Churaumi Aquarium) | A huge aquarium famous for its whale sharks |
| 古宇利島 (Kōri-jima) | A small island reached by a long bridge, with clear water |
| 竹富島 (Taketomi-jima) | A traditional village of coral-stone walls and red-tiled roofs |

| Food | Description |
|---|---|
| ゴーヤーチャンプルー (gōyā chanpurū) | Bitter-melon stir-fry, Okinawa's signature dish |
| ソーキそば (sōki soba) | Okinawan wheat noodles with braised pork ribs |
| ラフテー (rafutē) | Slow-braised pork belly |
| ヒラヤーチー (hirayāchī) | A thin savory pancake |
| 泡盛 (awamori) | Okinawa's distilled spirit, made from long-grain Thai rice |

Okinawa has long been known for its many centenarians (百寿者, *hyakujusha*). Researchers have credited diet, close-knit communities and a sense of purpose, often linked to the word 生き甲斐 (*ikigai*, a reason for living), though the prefecture's longevity lead has narrowed in recent decades.

## Regional Greetings at a Glance

Every region has a few words that locals love hearing from visitors. The table gives one each; [section 4.2](../04-spoken-japanese/02-dialects.md) explains the patterns behind them.

| Region | Standard | Local form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osaka | 〜じゃない | 〜やん | Isn't it? |
| Fukuoka | 〜よ | 〜ばい / 〜たい | Emphatic "you know" |
| Nagoya | そうだね | そうだがや | That's right |
| Hiroshima | 〜しなさい | 〜しんさい | Do ~ (a gentle instruction) |
| Kyoto and Osaka | ありがとう | おおきに | Thank you |
| Okinawa | こんにちは | はいさい (men) / はいたい (women) | Hello |

## The Eight Regions of Japan

Weather forecasts, sports leagues and travel guides divide the country into eight traditional regions, and you will hear the names constantly.

| Region | Prefectures | Main cities |
|---|---|---|
| 北海道 (Hokkaidō) | Hokkaido | Sapporo, Hakodate |
| 東北 (Tōhoku) | Aomori, Iwate, Miyagi, Akita, Yamagata, Fukushima | Sendai |
| 関東 (Kantō) | Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba, Ibaraki, Tochigi, Gunma | Tokyo, Yokohama |
| 中部 (Chūbu) | Niigata, Toyama, Ishikawa, Fukui, Yamanashi, Nagano, Gifu, Shizuoka, Aichi | Nagoya, Kanazawa |
| 近畿 / 関西 (Kinki / Kansai) | Osaka, Kyoto, Hyōgo, Nara, Shiga, Wakayama, Mie | Osaka, Kyoto, Kobe |
| 中国 (Chūgoku) | Hiroshima, Okayama, Tottori, Shimane, Yamaguchi | Hiroshima, Okayama |
| 四国 (Shikoku) | Ehime, Kagawa, Kōchi, Tokushima | Matsuyama, Takamatsu |
| 九州・沖縄 (Kyūshū and Okinawa) | Fukuoka, Saga, Nagasaki, Kumamoto, Ōita, Miyazaki, Kagoshima, Okinawa | Fukuoka, Naha |

> **Note:** 中国 (*Chūgoku*) is also the Japanese word for China. In context it is rarely confusing, and the region is often called 中国地方 (*Chūgoku chihō*) to make it explicit.

## Summary

- Japan has 47 prefectures (都道府県): Tokyo-to, Hokkai-dō, Osaka-fu, Kyoto-fu and 43 ken, grouped into eight traditional regions.
- Tokyo is a cluster of distinct neighborhoods, from the old shitamachi of Asakusa to the skyscrapers of Shinjuku, and its speech is the basis of standard Japanese.
- Osaka is the merchant and comedy capital, proud of its food (食い倒れ) and its dialect (めっちゃ, ほんま, おおきに); Kyoto, the capital from 794 to 1868, is soft-spoken and traditional.
- Each city has signature foods: Nagoya's miso katsu, Hiroshima's layered okonomiyaki, Fukuoka's tonkotsu ramen and yatai, Sapporo's miso ramen and Okinawa's chanpurū.
- Hokkaido and Okinawa have their own histories, of the Ainu and of the Ryūkyū Kingdom, which still shape their culture and language.
