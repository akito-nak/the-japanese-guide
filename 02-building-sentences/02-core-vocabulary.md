# Core Vocabulary — The Words That Carry You Everywhere

You step off the train, find the 駅 (*eki*, station) exit, buy an おにぎり (*onigiri*, rice ball) at the コンビニ (*konbini*, convenience store), ask いくらですか (*ikura desu ka*, how much is it?) and say ありがとうございます (*arigatō gozaimasu*, thank you very much) on the way out. Five words and phrases, and you have just handled a whole slice of daily life in Japan.

The words in this section were chosen because they turn up constantly: on signs, on menus, in conversation and in the news. They will not make you fluent on their own, but they are the bricks every grammar pattern from [section 2.1](01-grammar-basics.md) is built from. Learn them in context (in a sentence, out loud) rather than as a bare list, and come back to the tables often.

## Greetings and Social Phrases

Greetings are the words you will say most often, so they repay learning first. Several shorten among friends.

| Japanese | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| おはようございます | *ohayō gozaimasu* | Good morning | Drop ございます with close friends |
| こんにちは | *konnichiwa* | Hello / Good afternoon | The final は is read *wa* |
| こんばんは | *konbanwa* | Good evening | |
| おやすみなさい | *oyasuminasai* | Good night | Said before bed, not as a goodbye in the evening |
| さようなら | *sayōnara* | Goodbye | Formal and somewhat final; friends rarely use it |
| じゃあね / またね | *jā ne* / *mata ne* | See you / Bye | Casual |
| ありがとうございます | *arigatō gozaimasu* | Thank you very much | |
| ありがとう | *arigatō* | Thank you | Casual |
| どういたしまして | *dō itashimashite* | You're welcome | |
| すみません | *sumimasen* | Excuse me / I'm sorry | Multi-purpose: getting attention, minor apologies, even thanks |
| ごめんなさい | *gomen nasai* | I'm sorry | A sincere, personal apology |
| はい | *hai* | Yes | Also "I'm listening" |
| いいえ | *iie* | No | |
| そうです | *sō desu* | That's right | |
| そうですね | *sō desu ne* | I see / Yes, indeed | Agreement, or a pause to think |
| よろしくお願いします | *yoroshiku onegaishimasu* | Nice to meet you / I'm counting on you | Said at introductions and when asking favors |
| はじめまして | *hajimemashite* | Nice to meet you | Only on a first meeting |
| お元気ですか | *ogenki desu ka* | How are you? | Used after a long time apart, not as a daily greeting |
| おかげさまで | *okagesama de* | I'm well, thank you | Literally "thanks to you"; the polite reply to お元気ですか |

> はじめまして。スミスです。よろしくお願いします。\
> *Hajimemashite. Sumisu desu. Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.*\
> Nice to meet you. I'm Smith. I look forward to getting to know you.

## Numbers

Japanese numbers are logical once you have one to 10: eleven is "ten-one", twenty is "two-ten", and so on.

| Number | Kanji | Hiragana | Romaji |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 零 / 〇 | ゼロ / れい | *zero* / *rei* |
| 1 | 一 | いち | *ichi* |
| 2 | 二 | に | *ni* |
| 3 | 三 | さん | *san* |
| 4 | 四 | し / よん | *shi* / *yon* |
| 5 | 五 | ご | *go* |
| 6 | 六 | ろく | *roku* |
| 7 | 七 | しち / なな | *shichi* / *nana* |
| 8 | 八 | はち | *hachi* |
| 9 | 九 | く / きゅう | *ku* / *kyū* |
| 10 | 十 | じゅう | *jū* |
| 11 | 十一 | じゅういち | *jūichi* |
| 20 | 二十 | にじゅう | *nijū* |
| 100 | 百 | ひゃく | *hyaku* |
| 1,000 | 千 | せん | *sen* |
| 10,000 | 万 | まん | *man* |
| 100,000,000 | 億 | おく | *oku* |

The alternative readings for four, seven and nine are not interchangeable everywhere. よん, なな and きゅう are the safe choices when counting or saying prices; し and く sound like 死 (*shi*, death) and 苦 (*ku*, suffering), which is one reason they are avoided in many contexts.

> **Note:** Japanese groups large numbers in units of 10,000 (万, *man*) rather than 1,000. So 30,000 is 三万 (*sanman*, "three ten-thousands") and 1,000,000 is 百万 (*hyakuman*, "a hundred ten-thousands"). Practicing this pays off the first time you read a price tag or a rent listing.

> 三万円です。\
> *Sanman en desu.*\
> It's 30,000 yen.

### Days of the Week

Each weekday name pairs a celestial body or classical element with 曜日 (*yōbi*, day of the week), so the kanji double as a memory trick.

| Japanese | Romaji | Day | Memory trick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 月曜日 | *getsuyōbi* | Monday | 月 = moon |
| 火曜日 | *kayōbi* | Tuesday | 火 = fire |
| 水曜日 | *suiyōbi* | Wednesday | 水 = water |
| 木曜日 | *mokuyōbi* | Thursday | 木 = tree, wood |
| 金曜日 | *kin'yōbi* | Friday | 金 = gold, metal |
| 土曜日 | *doyōbi* | Saturday | 土 = earth |
| 日曜日 | *nichiyōbi* | Sunday | 日 = sun |

### Months

Months are the easiest vocabulary in this chapter: number plus 月 (*gatsu*). January is 一月 (*ichigatsu*), February is 二月 (*nigatsu*), and so on to December, 十二月 (*jūnigatsu*). Watch the three irregular readings: 四月 (*shigatsu*, April), 七月 (*shichigatsu*, July) and 九月 (*kugatsu*, September).

> 誕生日は十月です。\
> *Tanjōbi wa jūgatsu desu.*\
> My birthday is in October.

## Family

Family words come in pairs: a plain word for your own family when you talk to others, and a respectful word for someone else's family (and for addressing your own relatives directly). [Chapter 3](../03-politeness/README.md) explains the logic of this in-group and out-group split.

| Japanese | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 家族 | *kazoku* | family | |
| 父 / お父さん | *chichi* / *otōsan* | father | 父 = my father (to others); お父さん = someone else's, or addressing your own |
| 母 / お母さん | *haha* / *okāsan* | mother | Same pattern |
| 兄 / お兄さん | *ani* / *oniisan* | older brother | Same pattern |
| 姉 / お姉さん | *ane* / *onēsan* | older sister | Same pattern |
| 弟 | *otōto* | younger brother | |
| 妹 | *imōto* | younger sister | |
| 祖父 / おじいさん | *sofu* / *ojiisan* | grandfather | |
| 祖母 / おばあさん | *sobo* / *obāsan* | grandmother | |
| 夫 / 旦那 | *otto* / *danna* | husband | |
| 妻 / 奥さん | *tsuma* / *okusan* | wife | 奥さん is used for someone else's wife |
| 息子 | *musuko* | son | |
| 娘 | *musume* | daughter | |
| 友達 | *tomodachi* | friend | |

> 母は先生です。田中さんのお母さんは？\
> *Haha wa sensei desu. Tanaka-san no okāsan wa?*\
> My mother is a teacher. What about your mother, Tanaka?

## Asking for Help

These survival sentences combine the vocabulary above with the patterns from [section 2.1](01-grammar-basics.md). Swap any noun into the 〜 slot.

| Japanese | Romaji | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 〜はどこですか。 | *… wa doko desu ka.* | Where is …? |
| 〜をください。 | *… o kudasai.* | … please. / Please give me …. |
| 〜はありますか。 | *… wa arimasu ka.* | Do you have …? |
| いくらですか。 | *Ikura desu ka.* | How much is it? |
| 分かりません。 | *Wakarimasen.* | I don't understand. |
| もう一度お願いします。 | *Mō ichido onegaishimasu.* | Once more, please. |
| ゆっくり話してください。 | *Yukkuri hanashite kudasai.* | Please speak slowly. |
| 日本語が少しだけ話せます。 | *Nihongo ga sukoshi dake hanasemasu.* | I can speak only a little Japanese. |
| 英語を話せますか。 | *Eigo o hanasemasu ka.* | Can you speak English? |
| 助けてください！ | *Tasukete kudasai!* | Please help me! |
| 道に迷いました。 | *Michi ni mayoimashita.* | I'm lost. |

> すみません、駅はどこですか。\
> *Sumimasen, eki wa doko desu ka.*\
> Excuse me, where is the station?

These few hundred words are a toolkit, not a destination. With them you can get through most everyday situations in Japan, and every new grammar pattern you learn will give them more to do.

## Summary

- Greetings such as おはようございます, こんにちは, ありがとうございます and すみません are the most-used words in daily life, and すみません works for "excuse me", "sorry" and even "thank you".
- Japanese counts large numbers in units of 10,000 (万, *man*), so 30,000 is 三万 and one million is 百万.
- Days of the week combine an element or celestial body with 曜日 (月曜日 is Monday), and months are a number plus 月.
- Family words come in pairs: plain words like 母 for your own family when speaking to others, and respectful words like お母さん for someone else's family.
- 暑い and 寒い describe the weather, while 熱い and 冷たい describe things you touch.
- Survival patterns such as 〜はどこですか and 〜をください turn any noun in this section into a usable sentence.
